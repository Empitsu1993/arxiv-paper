#1 A Measure-Geometric Theory of Robustness in Machine Learning
著者: Cheng-Chao Huang
公開日: Tue, 27 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33123
要約:
This paper develops a foundational measure-geometric theory of robustness in machine learning. Robustness is formulated as escape from a retained task state relative to a reference measure on a data-bearing manifold, a perturbation gauge, and an admissible realization mechanism. The theory distinguishes the structural possibility of escape on the reference manifold from its ambient realization under mechanism-dependent observable readouts, while placing both within a unified geometric account. It introduces a mesoscopic organization of escape geometry, through which vulnerable mass, finite-scale morphology, delivered progress, and branch coherence are characterized by explicit laws as linked but non-identical robustness phenomena. By recasting robustness as the geometric formation, organization, realization, and persistence of escape, the theory provides a structural basis for analysis beyond scalar criteria.
#2 Kernelised covariance matrices, dimension reduction and imbalanced spectral clustering
著者: Xiaoyu Dong, Subhro Ghosh, Soumendu Sundar Mukherjee, Himasish Talukdar
公開日: Tue, 27 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33177
要約:
The Gaussian Mixture Model (abbrv. GMM) is a classic generative model for clustered data, for which spectral clustering based on dimension reduction via PCA has been an effective framework. In this work, we investigate kernelised covariance matrices as an alternative mechanism for dimension reduction and spectral clustering, focusing on truncation kernels that select pairs of data points with suitable separation constraints. The structure of these matrices is motivated by considerations from statistical mechanics of repulsive particle systems. Our approach is particularly relevant for the setting of imbalanced clusters, where the sizes of the largest and smallest clusters have a diverging ratio with the problem size, and existing PCA-based methods turn out to be of limited effectiveness. We demonstrate efficient dimension reduction and clustering performance of these modified covariance matrices, substantiated by quantitative bounds. In particular, they achieve superior dimension reduction guarantees compared to PCA for a wide range of parameters in the regime of imbalanced clusters. For spectral clustering, our approach exhibits improvements over PCA in the imbalanced setting, in particular guaranteeing accurate clustering in a much larger regime of intra-cluster separations. Our theoretical guarantees reflected in a range of simulation experiments that exhibit these improvements for unbalanced clustering problems. Our analytical approach, based on kernel Laplacians, provide a toolbox as well as a relevant conceptual ingredient for clustering problems at large. We believe that our work will motivate the application of such kernelised covariance matrices for related classes of statistical problems, as well as spur new investigations in the theory of random matrices.
#3 Online Covariance Estimation for Averaged Stochastic Gradient Descent on Riemannian Manifolds
著者: Jingrong Guan, Liwei Jiang, Sen Na
公開日: Tue, 27 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33419
要約:
We study online inference for averaged Riemannian stochastic gradient descent (RSGD) when the unknown parameter lies on a manifold. Although the averaged RSGD iterate is asymptotically normal, practical inference requires estimating the unknown covariance operator in its limiting distribution. Existing online batch-means methods cannot be applied directly because a general Riemannian manifold lacks an addition operation, and the tangent space containing the Riemannian gradient varies across iterations. Through vector transport, we construct overlapping and non-overlapping covariance estimators that adaptively combine information from successive batches while accounting for the changing tangent spaces. The proposed estimators are online, require neither Riemannian Hessian computations nor a prespecified terminal sample size, and attain the same convergence rates as their Euclidean counterparts (Zhu et al., 2023). Together with asymptotic normality, these estimators enable online statistical inference for RSGD on manifolds. We illustrate the proposed framework through a detailed study of streaming principal component analysis on Grassmann manifolds.
#4 Affine-invariant Cubic Newton with Weak Learners
著者: Nikita Zozoulenko, Daniel Falkowski, Thomas Cass, Lukas Gonon
公開日: Tue, 27 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33516
要約:
Modern gradient boosting algorithms are based on Newton's method, which excels empirically but lacks global convergence guarantees. While recent work on Gradient-Regularized Newton (GRN) boosting achieved a global $\mathcal{O}(1/\gamma^{12}k^2)$ rate, its analysis was not able to exploit the natural Hessian geometry and suffers from a heavy $\gamma^{12}$ dependence on a worst-case `weak gradient edge' $\gamma$. In this paper, we tackle these theoretical drawbacks by generalizing the Affine-Invariant Cubic Newton (AICN) scheme to convex optimization with weak learners. Our analysis is based on average cosine values $\overline{\Theta}_k$ along the optimization path and directional improvements $\delta_k$ in the Hessian-induced norm. We show that the weak-AICN update reduces to a damped weak-Newton step with an explicit stepsize determined by $\delta_k$. Under semi-strong self-concordance, we establish a global $\mathcal{O}(1/\overline{\Theta}_k^4 k^2)$ rate, significantly improving upon the weak-GRN bound. We further prove a local linear rate, global linear convergence for Hessian-dominated losses, cosine angle lower bounds, and present weak-AdaAICN, which adaptively tunes the cubic regularization. Finally, we demonstrate the broader applicability of this framework beyond gradient boosting by showing that Newton coordinate descent emerges naturally as a special case of optimization with weak learners.
#5 On the Error Analysis of Triangular Transport Maps for Conditional Generation via MMD-based Objectives
著者: Michele Martino, Bamdad Hosseini, Amir Sagiv, Amirhossein Taghvaei
公開日: Tue, 27 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33610
要約:
Conditional generative models parameterized by triangular transport maps are widely used for conditional sampling, yet theoretical guarantees on the quality of the conditional measures they produce remain scarce. We address this question for minimum divergence estimators of triangular maps. We use MMD-based objectives as a tractable setting in which the gap between joint and conditional errors can be analyzed precisely, and we measure performance through the average conditional estimation error, defined as the squared maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) between the estimated and the true conditionals, averaged over the conditioning variable. We first show that the common practice of minimizing a joint MMD may fail to recover the conditionals: writing the joint and the conditional criteria as quadratic energies of the same conditional mean embedding residual, we prove that, for parametric classes, joint minimizers are generically inconsistent in the misspecified setting. We then analyze an estimator that directly minimizes the conditional criterion through a surrogate loss computable from joint target samples and reference samples. We prove a non-asymptotic error bound that decomposes the average conditional estimation error into approximation, estimation, and reference sampling errors, with fast rates independent of the ambient dimension in the parametric setting. Finally, for joint MMDs built from concentrating Gaussian kernels on the conditioning variable, we show that the joint criterion approximates a density-reweighted version of the conditional one, and we derive an error bound for the joint minimizer whose rate suffers from a curse of dimensionality in the conditioning variable. Our results suggest that training criteria for conditional generative models should target the conditional rather than the joint error, a principle that extends to other divergences and to dynamic transport.
#6 Projection-Based Federated Learning on Compact Submanifolds with Geometry-Independent Differential Privacy

著者: Thibault Pautrel, Florent Bouchard, Guillaume Ginolhac, Ammar Mian, Aur\'elien Bellet
公開日: Tue, 27 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33712
要約:
Federated learning trains a shared model across clients without exchanging their data, yet the updates they transmit still leak information about individual records. Enforcing differential privacy is harder still when the model parameters lie on a Riemannian manifold. Existing methods rebuild the noise added for privacy in the geometry of each new iterate, aggregate through intrinsic maps that often lack a closed form, and prove convergence only in restricted regimes. We introduce DP-RFedProj, a record-level differentially private federated learning method for compact submanifolds that aggregates through closed-form projections and privatises gradients in a fixed ambient geometry. Each client may run any stochastic first-order optimiser, take several local steps, and participate only intermittently. An explicit record-level $f$-DP guarantee holds for the entire training transcript, independently of the manifold, the aggregation scheme and the local optimiser. We establish convergence of DP-RFedProj when each client runs Riemannian SGD locally and the server aggregates by projected averaging, and show that privacy enters the rate through a term scaling with the manifold's intrinsic dimension rather than the ambient one. Synthetic experiments on federated PCA over the Grassmann manifold corroborate these findings. On EEG motor-imagery classification with SPDNet, whose weights lie on the Stiefel manifold, the method outperforms a Euclidean baseline at every privacy budget considered. In both experimental settings, our accounting requires five to six times less noise than that of the closest prior method for the same budget, which translates into higher utility.

要約:
With the rapid development of modern scientific research, tensor data have become increasingly prevalent across a wide range of fields. How to effectively characterize the relationship between tensor predictors and a response while preserving the intrinsic structure of tensor data has therefore become an important problem in high-dimensional statistical analysis. Existing tensor regression methods mainly focus on conditional means or conditional quantiles. When the response distribution is asymmetric, heavy-tailed, or contaminated by outlying observations, the conditional modal provides a robust and interpretable alternative characterization. In this paper, we propose a partial modal tensor regression (PMTR) method. The proposed method constructs the partial modal tensor covariance, which incorporates information on the conditional modal to guide tensor dimension reduction, and subsequently establishes a modal tensor regression model in the reduced tensor space. Under suitable regularity conditions, we establish the consistency and asymptotic properties of the proposed estimators and employ a data-driven dimension-selection procedure to determine the effective dimension along each tensor mode.

Classical methods like ARIMA, however, remain configured and trained independently for each series, discarding knowledge from previous experiments. #10 Intrinsic Gradient Barriers in Pushforward Deep Generative Models
著者: Edric Tam, Qingyang Liu, Barbara Engelhardt, Ray Bai
公開日: Tue, 27 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34484
要約:
Broad classes of deep generative models (DGMs) operate by learning to map a simple source distribution, such as a standard Gaussian, to a complex high-dimensional target distribution. Despite their success, DGMs often empirically struggle to accurately represent distributions with heavy tails or well-separated modes. In this work, we show that these difficulties reflect intrinsic properties of the underlying transport problem rather than particular neural network architectures or training procedures. We establish quantitative lower bounds on the gradients and Jacobians of pushforward mappings that transport log-concave source distributions to such targets. For heavy-tailed target distributions, the required gradient magnitude grows as the target develops increasingly large moments. For multimodal target distributions, the low-probability regions between well-separated modes similarly force large gradients in the transport map. We discuss how such large derivatives relate to trade-offs between expressivity and numerical stability. Our results hold under substantially weaker regularity conditions than previous analyses and remain stable under perturbations of the target distribution. Finally, we show that these obstructions can be mitigated when the goal is approximation of a given density rather than generative modeling. Specifically, we propose simple classes of bijective reparametrizations that can alleviate the difficult geometric structure and reduce the gradient burden placed on the learned generative map. Numerical experiments support our theoretical analysis.

A scalar measurement can determine a robust decision without identifying the latent covariance or every decision risk. We study finite Gaussian projection experiments with a fixed mean-plus-standard-deviation target. The observation laws define a set of indistinguishable moment models. #12 Joint Independence Testing for Latent Position Random Graphs
著者: Xiaoyi Wen, Zoltan Szabo
公開日: Tue, 27 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34544
要約:
Kernel techniques are among the most powerful methods in data science and statistics. One of the key properties of kernels contributing to their numerous successful applications is that the associated reproducing kernel Hilbert space is expressive enough to encode probability distributions without loss of information or to capture the joint independence of $M\ge 2$ random variables, under mild conditions. Computationally, these measures (i) rely on the so-called Gram matrix which contains the pairwise similarity of sample points, and (ii) converge at a rate $O \left(n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)$, where $n$ denotes the sample size. We focus on measuring the independence of latent position random graphs (LPRGs), a model widely-applied for instance in neuroscience, sociology, and epidemiology. In LPRGs a latent representation is associated to each node. The similarity of these representations (meant via a kernel) determines whether two nodes are connected, making the Gram matrices only implicitly observable and the estimation challenging. Despite the fundamental importance of quantifying the independence of $M$ LPRGs, very little is known about the solution of this problem; existing techniques are restricted to $M=2$ components and only guarantee consistency without rates. In this work, (i) we define an independence measure called graph Hilbert-Schmidt independence criterion (GHSIC) for $M\ge 2$ LPRGs, (ii) we prove that it can be estimated at a rate $\tilde{O}\left(n^{-\frac{1}{3}}\right)$ relying on spectral decomposition of adjacency matrices, (iii) without the need to choose kernels and their hyperparameters. Numerical experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed GHSIC estimator both on synthetic examples and in the analysis of brain functional connectivity subnetworks in natural language perception.
#13 HIFI: A Highly Efficient Framework for Statistical Inference with Foundation Models
著者: Po-Wei Chen, Shan Yu, Chunlin Li

リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34550
要約:
Foundation models provide powerful representations for unstructured data, such as text and images, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) makes their adaptation to downstream tasks increasingly practical. For neural estimators that lack a tractable sampling distribution, resampling-based procedures offer a flexible route to statistical inference, but they often require hundreds of model fits, making them computationally prohibitive at foundation-model scale. We introduce HIFI, a two-stage fine-tuning framework for statistical inference on parameters such as conditional means. Stage 1 adapts a foundation model once on a larger sample, capturing the complex structure of the data and controlling estimation bias through a single fit. HIFI accommodates a broad class of resampling procedures, including ensemble subsampling and the nonparametric bootstrap. Across vision and text experiments, HIFI scales to pretrained models ranging from tens of millions to more than ten billion parameters while providing accurate point estimates and well-calibrated confidence intervals across diverse architectures, and reduces training compute by up to 91.9% relative to direct resampling. An application to gene-expression inference illustrates the practical value of the framework for uncertainty-aware scientific inference.

Spatio-temporal graph neural networks combine a node's history with information from graph neighbors. The central question is whether graph neighbors provide predictive information beyond each node's own history. Forecast accuracy depends on both the choice of neighbors and the way graph messages enter the temporal model. #15 Intrinsic Information Rates of Summaries in Simulation-Based Inference
著者: Vansh Bansal, Pradip Sharma Poudel, James G. Scott
公開日: Tue, 27 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34703
要約:
Simulation-based inference often encodes observations $Y$ into a fixed-dimensional summary $S$, with the posterior conditioning only on $S$. While exact preservation often requires infinite information, any such summary imposes a finite information budget on inference which is rarely an explicit choice or measurement. In this paper, we make the budget explicit by modeling the summary as a lossy channel from $Y$ to $S$, and ask two questions: how large must the budget be, and how best is it spent? First, we show for parametric models that this budget, measured by the mutual information $I(Y;S)$, grows unboundedly as the posterior concentrates, even when fixed-dimensional sufficient statistics exist. More generally, the required budget is set by the distribution of the random posterior, and we bound it from above by quantizing posteriors and from below by converses that weight each posterior state by its probability. Second, we show that a finite budget is best spent stochastically. Under a common-support condition on the simulator, covering the continuous observation spaces typical of simulators, a deterministic summary necessarily loses something: a stochastic summary achieves \emph{strictly} lower distortion than every deterministic summary and every time-shared mixture of deterministic summaries at every positive budget too small for exact preservation. Motivated by these results, we insert a binary bottleneck into neural posterior estimators, measure each trained summary's rate, and compare summaries at matched rate. Across four simulator benchmarks and several encoder families, stochastic bottlenecks achieve favorable rate--loss tradeoffs, and architecture rankings change at different information budgets.

Clean-image prediction ($x$-prediction) and noise prediction yield equivalent population-optimal denoisers, yet can perform very differently in finite diffusion networks. For noisy input $Y_t$ with noise standard deviation $\sigma_t$, a noise predictor $f_{\varepsilon,t}$ induces the clean-image estimate $\widehat X_1=Y_t-\sigma_t f_{\varepsilon,t}(Y_t)$. This reveals a noise-transmission burden: the predictor must preserve information needed to cancel the input corruption. Under Gaussian corruption of $d$-dimensional data, a linear input bottleneck of rank $r<d$ imposes a denoising-error floor $\sigma_t^2(d-r)$ even with unrestricted downstream computation, while the corresponding optimal risk for image prediction remains bounded. ImageNet experiments with JiT show that measured denoising losses reflect the predicted bottleneck floor and its attenuation or cancellation through preconditioning. Further widening yields larger relative denoising gains at low noise, where corrections beyond linear denoising spread across more directions, consistent with our spectral characterization of the required model width. Together, these results provide a denoising-based account of practical generation-quality gaps across prediction targets, preconditioning choices, and model widths.

Without gradient clipping, the corresponding $L^2$ convergence does not hold in general, but we establish the weaker convergence in probability.
