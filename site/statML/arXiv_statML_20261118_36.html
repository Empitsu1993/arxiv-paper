#1 Modeling Document and Section Hierarchies in Embedding Space
著者: Kriste Krstovski, Sharyn O'Halloran, Kshitij Chandna
公開日: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21980
要約:
Embedded topic models integrate word embeddings into the topic generation process, producing coherent topics and robust performance on large vocabularies. However, each document is represented by a single topic distribution, ignoring its internal structure. Many real world collections, including legal documents, company and government reports, and online discussion forums, exhibit hierarchical organization in which documents consist of multiple sections that often address distinct subtopics. We introduce the Hierarchical Embedded Topic Model (hETM), a structured topic model that couples the topic distribution of a document and those of its sections through a hierarchical prior in the embedding space. We derive an amortized variational inference procedure for hETM and evaluate it on structured collections from each of these domains. Experimental results show that hETM achieves the lowest or near-lowest held-out perplexity among flat and hierarchical topic model baselines, along with comparable or higher topic quality. It also performs strongly on a downstream clustering task, achieving the best clustering performance on most of the collections.
#2 Bandit Online Submodular Minimisation Using Zeroth-Order Oracles
著者: Amir Ali Farzin, Philipp Braun, Iman Shames
公開日: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22130
要約:
We consider online submodular minimisation under bandit feedback, where at each round a decision maker plays a subset and observes only its cost, while the cost functions, and hence their minimiser, change over time. Existing bandit methods only guarantee static regret, which compares against a single fixed subset and is a weak benchmark when the minimiser moves. This paper provides, to the best of our knowledge, the first method with guarantees for tracking a moving minimiser in this setting. We propose a zeroth-order method built on a two-point oracle for the Lov\'asz extension whose two evaluations are both subsets that can be played. The perturbed and base points are rounded by a common random threshold, and drawing the smoothing direction from the unit sphere makes the base evaluation a zero-mean control variate, so the oracle is an unbiased estimator of the smoothed gradient. We establish dynamic regret bounds of order $O\big((1+\sqrt{P^\ast_N})N^{3/4}\big)$ in general and $O\big((1+\sqrt{P^\ast_N})N^{2/3}\big)$ under a sufficiently small drift, where $P^\ast_N$ is the path length of the minimiser sequence, together with the corresponding $O(N^{3/4})$ and $O(N^{2/3})$ static regret bounds. Numerically, the method attains lower regret than the algorithm of (Hazan and Kale (2012)), with the advantage growing with the path length.
#3 Bias-Reduced and Parallel Heavy-Tailed Stochastic Convex Optimization in $\ell_p$ Geometries
著者: Zhengyun Nie, Anant Shyam, Brian Bullins
公開日: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21844
要約:
We consider the fundamental problem of parallel stochastic convex optimization (SCO), whose aim is to approximately minimize a convex objective when given the ability to query a moment-bounded stochastic gradient oracle multiple times in parallel per round. Whereas most previous results have focused on the variance-bounded Euclidean ($\ell_2$-norm) setting, meaning the objective is Lipschitz with respect to the $\ell_2$-norm over an $\ell_2$-bounded feasible set in $\mathbb{R}^d$, in this work we study parallel SCO in $\ell_p$ settings for $2 \le p < \infty$ under a heavy-tailed noise model. We begin by introducing a new class of $\ell_p$-optimal-distance-convergence (ODC$_p$) algorithms, drawing on prior stochastic bias-reduced techniques [ACJ+21], which combine with multilevel Monte Carlo methods to efficiently estimate the optimum of uniformly convex objectives. As a consequence of our ODC$_p$ algorithms, we first establish rates for projection-efficient $\ell_p$-Lipschitz optimization, composite convex minimization, and efficient $\ell_p$-proximal point estimation. Combining our ODC$_p$ algorithms with a new $\ell_p$-generalized Gaussian-based stochastic gradient estimator ($p$-GREG), we provide two efficient $\ell_p$ parallel algorithms, EpochDA and $\ell_p$-AC-SA, which achieve optimization error $\epsilon > 0$ with at most $\tilde{O}(d^{\frac{p}{2p + 2}} \epsilon^{-\frac{p}{p + 1}})$ query depth, improving upon the sequential heavy-tailed stochastic query depth of $O(\epsilon^{-p})$ for $\epsilon \leq \tilde{O}(d^{-1/(2p)})$. We also establish dimension-dependent lower bounds of $\Omega\bigl(\min\{\epsilon^{-p},d^{p/(3p-2)}\}\bigr)$ adaptive rounds for nonsmooth convex optimization over a subset of the unit $\ell_p$ ball. Our results improve upon the $\Omega(\min\{\epsilon^{-p},\epsilon^{2/3}d^{1/3}\})$ bounds of [DG19], when adapted to the same feasible set.
#4 Adaptive Memory for Online Reinforcement Learning under Sparse Model Changes
著者: Sreejeet Maity, Sukanya Modak, Leonardo F. Toso
公開日: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21879
要約:
We study the discounted reinforcement learning (RL) problem in a setting where the underlying environment changes between deployments. Each deployment provides $H$ observations from a fixed MDP. The reward function and transition kernel are fixed within each deployment. Across deployments, at most an $\epsilon >0$ fraction of the reward and transition kernel entries may change. In this setting, learning each model to deploy from scratch may discard useful observations from past deployments, whereas pooling all past observations can bias the estimates of entries that have already changed. We then propose an algorithm that for each state and action pair, selects how much history to reuse to estimate the reward and probabilities of the next state. We start with observations from the current deployment and add earlier deployment observations one at a time, the most recent first. After each addition, we check whether a single average reward agrees with the estimates from all histories considered until that point. We apply the same check to the distribution of next states. For each estimate, we leverage the last history that passes these aforementioned checks. The optimal control policy is then computed on top of the fitted MDP. We characterize the average gap between the optimal return and the return of the learner's policies over a finite number of deployments. We demonstrate that when $0< |S|\epsilon \leq 2$, with $|S|$ being the cardinality of the state space, our algorithm needs only the observations from the most recent $O(1/\epsilon)$ deployments, and achieves a long-run average gap of at most $\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{\epsilon/H})$.
#5 Cancellation-Free Constrained SquareCB for Safe Controller Selection
著者: Kartik Pandit, Sourav Ganguly, Arnob Ghosh
公開日: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21880
要約:
We study constrained contextual bandits, in which a learner chooses one of $K$ actions in each round to minimize loss while keeping the expected cost of its play below a budget. Existing reductions to online regression weight the cost by a Lagrange multiplier learned from past constraint residuals, so slack in some rounds can offset violations in others. We instead run SquareCB proposed in \citep{foster2020beyond} with a rectified weight on the cost: it is zero when the estimated cost is within the budget, and otherwise just large enough to bring it back to the budget. Under realizability and Slater's condition with margin $\delta$, the algorithm attains regret $O(\sqrt{KT\,\mathrm{Reg}_{\mathsf{Sq}}(T)}/\delta)$ and cancellation-free violation $\sum_t(V_c^{p_t}-b)^+=O(\sqrt{KT\,\mathrm{Reg}_{\mathsf{Sq}}(T)})$, where $\mathrm{Reg}_{\mathsf{Sq}}$ is the error of the regression oracle. The same bounds hold when the budget must be met in every context, potentially adversarially chosen contexts. In controller selection for a safe-control-gym quadrotor and a BOPTEST building, the violation is 6 to 9 times lower than that of primal-dual and virtual-queue baselines, with a lower or comparable regret.
#6 DEFTS: Data-Efficient, Training-Free Few-Shot Learning for CLIP in the Text Subspace
著者: Yijin Zeng
公開日: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21993
要約:
Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP) achieves impressive zero-shot image classification performance by comparing image embeddings against class-specific text embeddings. While recent few-shot methods leverage labeled training samples to improve upon this zero-shot baseline, they often predominantly rely on image embeddings and underutilize the semantic structure of the text embeddings. In this paper, we introduce DEFTS, a data-efficient, training-free few-shot classification method strictly in the text subspace. We first demonstrate empirically that while raw text embeddings can be suboptimal as direct classifiers, the linear subspace they span remains highly discriminative. DEFTS therefore proposes to reduce the image-text modality gap by projecting estimated image prototypes directly into this subspace. Importantly, by expressing these prototypes as linear combinations of text embeddings, DEFTS reduces a high-dimensional estimation problem to the derivation of just a few coefficients. These coefficients are then computed via a Bayesian posterior mean, using a prior naturally centered on the original text embedding coordinates. Extensive experiments across 12 image classification benchmarks demonstrate that DEFTS significantly improves upon existing training-free methods.
#7 Locally Linear Generalized Single Index Regression
著者: Tristan Luca Saidi, Arun Kuchibhotla
公開日: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22011
要約:
In this work we propose and analyze a procedure for estimating a generalized single-index regression model. This generalized single-index regression framework models the conditional mean of the responses $Y$ as a function of the covariates $X$ where the mapping is assumed to have the form $f \circ g$, where $g$ is $\beta$-H\"older with $\beta \geq 2$ and $f$ can be a discontinuous function of bounded variation. We propose a general procedure that operates under minimal assumptions about the distribution of covariates $X$, requiring only boundedness, sufficient smoothness and absolute continuity with respect to Lebesgue. Our procedure jointly estimates the gradient vector $\hat v(x) \approx \nabla g(x)$ and the conditional mean $\mu(x) = f(g(x))$ by solving a localized weighted linear least squares problem. We derive central limit theorems for gradient vector estimates $\hat{v}_x$ and regression estimates $\hat{\mu}(x)$, and we derive rates of convergence for our approach, illustrating that it indeed achieves a stochastic convergence rate matching the minimax mean squared error rate when $\beta = 2$ and $f$ has a locally Lipschitz derivative. Unlike prior approaches our velocity estimation results accommodate link functions in the local bounded-variation class, allowing for nonsmooth and even discontinuous links while retaining consistency and asymptotic normality at almost every inference point. Finally, we instantiate this model in the situation where $g = \Pi$ is a closest point projection onto a compact and regular embedded $C^3$ curve $\gamma$, a setting recently studied in the literature. We also supplement our theoretical results with experiments on real and synthetic data to demonstrate the utility of our procedure.

#9 COSI: A Framework for Robustly Valid Inference
著者: Arun K. Kuchibhotla, Sivaraman Balakrishnan, Larry Wasserman
公開日: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22096
要約:
We introduce COSI (COnfidence from Scale Invariance), a framework for constructing confidence sets for statistical functionals. COSI builds on the HulC (Hull-based Confidence), which uses median unbiasedness to construct confidence sets from batch estimates. COSI uses scale-invariance properties shared by many common estimators. We divide the data into batches, compute an estimate in each batch, and retain each candidate parameter value for which the batch estimates pass a test of scale invariance. The resulting confidence sets are asymptotically valid under weaker assumptions than classical Wald and bootstrap procedures require, and their coverage can be closer to the nominal level in small samples. Applications include robust inference for the mean, regression coefficients in misspecified linear models, tensor norms, and Fr\'echet means.
#10 Centroid Spectral Coupling: Implicit Alignment and Representation Diversity in Self-Supervised Learning
著者: M. Hadi Sepanj, Benyamin Ghojogh, Saed Moradi, Paul Fieguth
公開日: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22158
要約:
Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) aims to align augmented views while preserving representation diversity and preventing collapse. Existing non-contrastive methods often address these goals using multiple weighted loss terms. We introduce Centroid Spectral Coupling (CSC), a single-term SSL objective that implicitly couples view alignment, representation diversity, and collapse resistance. CSC constructs centroids from independently normalized augmented-view pairs and applies a ridge-based spectral objective to their uncentered second-moment matrix. Preserving centroid magnitudes encodes view agreement while encouraging balanced representation energy across spectral directions. We establish an exact decomposition into nonnegative alignment and spectral-imbalance components, characterize the geometry of global optima, and derive finite-loss guarantees for alignment and diversity. We also show that complete constant-output collapse is a strict saddle in normalized representation space. Experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Imagenette show that CSC achieves the highest linear Top-1 accuracy among the evaluated non-contrastive methods on all three benchmarks, and the highest kNN Top-1 accuracy on both CIFAR datasets. A ridge-parameter sensitivity study reveals an empirical alignment--diversity trade-off: increasing $\kappa$ strengthens view alignment but reduces representation effective rank. These results show that a unified spectral objective yields competitive visual representations without explicit contrastive negatives or separately weighted alignment and diversity penalties.
#11 Training Neural Networks to be Locally Adaptive
著者: Bilol Banerjee, Soham Sarkar
公開日: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22198
要約:
Deep neural networks fitted by global empirical risk minimization commit to a single model for the entire covariate space. In a regression problem, if the regression function $f_0$ is inhomogeneous --- varying rapidly in one region, while being nearly flat in another --- no single model can be appropriate everywhere, and the fit degrades where the function varies most rapidly. Here we study an alternative in which, instead of fitting the network globally, we fit it locally. For each query point $x$, a \emph{local neural network} is trained on the $k$ nearest-neighbours of $x$ alone, making the training sample and the fitted function query-dependent. We analyze the performance of the resulting estimator through a localization approach, establishing that the pointwise risk of the estimator with an appropriate choice of $k$ attains the rate $n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+d^{*})}$, up to logarithmic factors, when $f_0$ is locally $\alpha$-H\"older with $\alpha\in(0,1]$ and the covariates are supported on a $d^{*}$-dimensional manifold embedded in $\mathbb{R}^{d}$. A matching Le Cam lower bound shows that this rate is pointwise minimax optimal. However, the local rate-optimality requires $k$ to be calibrated to the smoothness of $f_0$, which is unknown in practice. We therefore provide a locally adaptive choice of $k$ that makes the neural network adapt to the local H\"older exponent of $f_0$ and to the intrinsic dimension simultaneously, both of which are potentially varying over the covariate domain. An extensive simulation study demonstrates the dominance of the locally adaptive neural network over a globally trained network and several state-of-the-art methods.
#12 SOTA or Luck? The Winner's Curse in LLM Leaderboards
著者: Sikun Xu, Raphael Thomadsen, Dennis J. Zhang, Heng Zhang
公開日: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22227
要約:
Public LLM leaderboards guide model evaluation and adoption, yet the reported performance of top-ranked models can be systematically overstated. This leaderboard winner's curse arises because ranking noisy benchmark scores rewards sampling luck alongside genuine ability. The same selection effect can distort scores throughout the leaderboard, a phenomenon we call the rank-induced bias. We develop an $m$-out-of-$N$ bootstrap to correct for this bias. It assumes a fixed model pool with independent, identically distributed bounded tasks while allowing arbitrary within-task correlation among models. Under exact population tiers separated by positive gaps, we prove that the bootstrap reduces $\Theta(N^{-1/2})$ ranking bias to expected residual bias $o(N^{-1/2})$. Across 14 benchmarks from 5 benchmark families, the rank-1 adjustments range from approximately 0.09 to 3.86 percentage points. In our SWE-bench Verified snapshot, the tied leaders receive an adjustment of 0.90 percentage points. Such overstatement is large enough to make the apparent winner statistically fragile. Thus, we use the same bootstrap to further quantify the intervals of plausible rank positions for each model and group models into tiers. We establish sufficient conditions for consistent recovery of population tiers. On SWE-bench Verified, this procedure places 13 of 173 models in the leading tier. We combine the score adjustment and rank intervals in the Rank Uncertainty and Leaderboard Evaluation Report (RULER). We validate its finite-sample performance using sample splitting and calibrated simulations, and advocate publishing RULER alongside raw rankings so readers can assess both reported performance and the precision of the ordering. RULER also provides guidance for benchmark design that accounts for selection effects and ranking uncertainty.

#15 A Cherry-Picking Approach to Large Load Shaping for More Effective Carbon Reduction
著者: Bokan Chen, Raiden Hasegawa, Adriaan Hilbers, Ross Koningstein, Ana Radovanovi\'c, Utkarsh Shah, Gabriela Volpato, Mohamed Ahmed, Tim Cary, Rod Frowd
公開日: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500

要約:
Shaping multi-megawatt flexible loads such as data centers alters generator dispatch on the electric grid, affecting system-wide CO2 emissions and wholesale electricity costs. However, evaluating prevalent load-shaping signals - such as grid-average carbon intensity, locational marginal price, renewable generation, or commercial marginal emission estimates - has remained difficult because full-scale market-clearing models and generator bid data are rarely accessible outside system operators. Here we conduct year-long causal dispatch counterfactuals using a calibrated, nearly 8,900-bus replica of ERCOT's day-ahead Security Constrained Unit Commitment and Security Constrained Economic Dispatch market-clearing process across renewable-rich and fossil-dominated zones. We find that while LMP-based shaping outperforms other static signals in annual grid CO2 reductions, no single signal performs best across all operating conditions. Extending this framework to coordinated spatial and temporal shifting across two grid nodes increases CO2 reductions by over 70% compared to temporal-only shifting. Finally, because shaping load against region-wide average carbon intensity can backfire and raise physical grid emissions in fossil-heavy zones, our findings highlight the need for Scope 2 carbon-accounting frameworks to incorporate locational, price-aware, and congestion-aware signals.
#16 Measuring and Decomposing Mode Separation via the Canonical Diffusion
diffusion
著者: Shaul Tolkowsky, Ori Meidler, Or Zuk
公開日: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08777
要約:
Mode separation, namely how sharply a distribution fragments into barrier-separated clusters, is a fundamental geometric property of densities, difficult to quantify in high dimensions. Modes are often the meaningful alternatives in the data, such as the conformations of a molecule or the distinct semantic types among a generative model's outputs. Mode separation is distinct from dispersion, which is what standard label-free tools capture: differential entropy rises with spread regardless of fragmentation, and PCA orders directions by variance regardless of barriers. Mutual information does capture it, but only given the latent component labels, which are usually unavailable. We measure mode separation through a single stochastic process intrinsic to the density, namely the unique reversible diffusion with $f$ as its stationary distribution and a constant scalar diffusion coefficient. From its autocovariance matrix we extract two readouts. The first, SSA (Sum of Squared Autocorrelations), is a scalar barrier-sensitive measure. The second, DA (Dominant Autocorrelation directions), gives linear projections ordered by metastability rather than variance. Under an isotropic-Gaussian null, we derive the limiting spectrum of the empirical autocovariance in closed form, generalizing the Marchenko--Pastur law. Its upper edge gives a noise floor that separates metastable directions from sampling noise. Both readouts use only samples and a score function, scaling to high dimensions through pretrained score-based generative models via Tweedie's identity. We apply our framework to three settings: (i) synthetic Gaussian mixtures, where SSA tracks mutual information; (ii) SDXL text-to-image generations, where SSA and DA capture structure that entropy and PCA miss; and (iii) molecular dynamics of alanine dipeptide, where DA recovers the known slow backbone dihedrals from static samples alone.
#17 Sharp feature-learning transitions and Bayes-optimal neural scaling laws in extensive-width networks
著者: Minh-Toan Nguyen, Jean Barbier
公開日: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10395
要約:
We study the information-theoretic limits of learning a one-hidden-layer teacher network with hierarchical features from noisy queries, in the context of knowledge transfer to a smaller student model. We work in the high-dimensional regime where the teacher width $k$ scales linearly with the input dimension $d$ -- a setting that captures large-but-finite-width networks and has only recently become analytically tractable. Using a heuristic leave-one-out decoupling argument, validated numerically throughout, we derive asymptotically sharp characterizations of the Bayes-optimal generalization error and individual feature overlaps via a system of closed fixed-point equations. These equations reveal that feature learnability is governed by a sequence of sharp phase transitions: as data grows, teacher features become recoverable sequentially, each through a discontinuous jump in overlap. This sequential acquisition underlies a precise notion of \textit{effective width} $k_c$ -- the number of learnable features at a given data budget $n$ -- which unifies two distinct scaling regimes: a feature-learning regime in which the Bayes-optimal generalization error $\varepsilon^{\rm BO}$ scales as $ n^{1/(2\beta)-1}$, and a refinement regime in which it scales as $n^{-1}$, where $\beta>1/2$ is the exponent of the power-law feature hierarchy. Both laws collapse to the single relation $\varepsilon^{\rm BO}=\Theta(k_c d/n)$. We further show empirically that a student trained with \textsc{Adam} near the effective width $k_c$ achieves these optimal scaling laws (up to a small algorithmic gap), and provide an information-theoretic account of the associated scaling in model size.

#19 Batched Stochastic Linear Bandits with 1-Bit Communication Constraints
著者: Ivan Lau, Daniel McMorrow, Kevin Jamieson, Jonathan Scarlett
公開日: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30976
要約:
We study stochastic linear bandits under a natural combination of batching and communication constraints: the time horizon is partitioned into batches of equal size $B$, and during each batch the learner sends $B$ requested arm pulls to an agent, who then observes the corresponding $B$ rewards and responds with a single bit of feedback to the learner. For each batch, the learner specifies the 1-bit quantization rule the agent uses, which may depend on all previously received bits but not on any past rewards directly. This setting addresses a significant yet unexplored ``middle ground'' between previous models having per-round quantization only or total bit budgets only. We establish a minimax lower bound showing that $\Omega(B\min\{d,\log\lvert \mathcal{A} \rvert\})$ regret is unavoidable due to the 1-bit communication bottleneck, even in the absence of noise. Combined with standard statistical limits, this yields a general lower bound of $\widetilde{\Omega}(B\min\{d,\log\lvert \mathcal{A} \rvert\} + \sqrt{dT \min\{d,\log\lvert \mathcal{A} \rvert\}})$. We develop two phased-elimination algorithms based on $G$-optimal designs and 1-bit mean estimation. The first achieves $\widetilde{O}(dB + d\sqrt{T})$ regret, matching the lower bound up to logarithmic factors when $\lvert \mathcal{A} \rvert = \exp(\Omega(d))$, and the second incorporates a safe-arm identification and warm-start procedure to obtain $\widetilde{O}(B\log\lvert \mathcal{A} \rvert + d^{3/2}\sqrt{B} + \sqrt{dT\log\lvert \mathcal{A} \rvert})$ regret, which is near-optimal in broad scaling regimes of $(\lvert \mathcal{A} \rvert, B, d, T)$. Together, our results demonstrate that a single bit of feedback per batch suffices to nearly match the minimax regret of unconstrained linear bandits in broad scaling regimes, even for batch sizes as large as $\Theta(\sqrt{T})$.
