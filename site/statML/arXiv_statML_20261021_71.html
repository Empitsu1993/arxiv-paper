#1 Quadratic Sample Complexity for Strictly Replicable Realizable Learning with Unknown Marginals
著者: Guangjian Zhang
公開日: Wed, 21 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26944
要約:
Under the quantifier $\exists A\,\forall D$, where the realizable distribution and its marginal are unknown, we determine the finite-class sample-complexity exponent for strict replicability: two independent samples and one shared seed must produce identical functions. For $\varepsilon=\delta=\rho=10^{-4}$, the worst case over $|H|\le M$ is $\Theta((\log M)^2)$, without iterated logarithms. For $d\ge1$, $0<\varepsilon\le1/16$, and $0<\delta,\rho\le10^{-3}$, every randomized, possibly improper learner valid throughout an explicit anchored realizable family requires $\Omega(d^2/(\varepsilon\rho^2))$ samples. For the full Boolean class $P_d$, $d\ge2$, in the same parameter range, we prove the matching characterization $\Theta(d^2/(\varepsilon\rho^2)+\varepsilon^{-1}\log(1/\delta))$ using a proper Poissonized ball-sampling algorithm. We also give an explicit proper Gibbs upper bound for every finite measurable Boolean class. Our lower bound uses square-root probabilities and volumetric packing; the Gibbs proof builds on established coupling and generalization identities. Finally, we characterize the known-marginal worst case for $P_d$, showing why that interface must be kept distinct.
#2 Dynamic Dual-Feedback Conformal Inference for Time Series Forecasting
著者: Songlin Du, Ling Luo, Uwe Aickelin
公開日: Wed, 21 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26987
要約:
Quantifying uncertainty in time series forecasting is particularly demanding because sequential data exhibit temporal dependence and are prone to distributional changes. Conformal inference has emerged as a powerful uncertainty quantification approach through the construction of prediction sets. Recent advances have introduced online conformal methods that adaptively adjust prediction thresholds through feedback mechanisms. However, the existing feedback mechanism typically relies solely on miscoverage indicators (actual feedback), whether the true label falls within the interval at each time step, while overlooking the empirical prediction threshold (estimated feedback) that is derived from the oracle conformal method. In this paper, we propose Dynamic Dual-feedback Conformal Inference (DDCI), which incorporates a dual-feedback mechanism consisting of actual feedback and estimated feedback. The former drives the primary adjustment of the intervals based on true observations, while the latter dampens excessive expansions or contractions by leveraging empirical thresholds from conformal inference during updates. By balancing these two signals, DDCI achieves more stable and narrower prediction intervals in sequential settings while maintaining near-nominal coverage in practice.
#3 Robust Latent Variable Graphical Models: Exact Recovery and Fundamental Limits
著者: Chuansen Peng, Xiaojing Shen
公開日: Wed, 21 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27010
要約:
We study robust estimation of a Gaussian precision matrix that decomposes as a sparse matrix minus a symmetric, possibly indefinite, low-rank matrix, a structure that arises when latent-variable marginalization is combined with conditioning on linear measurements. The proposed convex estimator replaces the sample covariance by the centered Gaussian measure-transformed (MT) covariance, which preserves the signed graph of the sparse component under the nominal model, and penalizes the low-rank component by the nuclear norm of a congruence transform, an atomic norm that encodes structural side information. Our main result is a primal--dual witness theorem: under restricted invertibility and irrepresentability conditions on the Fisher information, the raw convex optimizer is unique, has no false edges, recovers the signs of all sufficiently strong edges, and has exactly the true rank and inertia. These conditions reduce to the graphical-lasso conditions when the low-rank part is absent, and we verify them from primitive incoherence and near-isotropy assumptions. Over a class on which they hold uniformly, we prove information-theoretic limits for Frobenius estimation, signed graph recovery, and inertia recovery. In particular, inertia recovery requires $N\gtrsim p/\nu_{\min}^2$ samples, where $\nu_{\min}$ is the smallest nonzero eigenvalue modulus of the low-rank part; the spectral beta-min condition of the witness theorem is necessary up to a $\sqrt{\log p}$ factor; and the Frobenius risk attains the minimax rate up to a factor $\sqrt{\mu(1+\min\{d,r\})\log p}$, where $\mu$ is the incoherence parameter, $d$ the maximal degree, and $r$ the rank. We further quantify robustness under Huber contamination, with a bias bound that is sharp for the MT functional and vanishes for remote outliers, and give a globally convergent linearized ADMM with closed-form updates.
#4 Gaussian Approximation of Besov Functions for Logistic Classification on Manifolds
著者: Guanhang Lei, Lei Shi, Xiaoming Yuan
公開日: Wed, 21 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27022
要約:
We study Gaussian kernel approximation and regularized logistic classification on a connected compact smooth embedded manifold $X\subset\mathbb R^d$ without boundary and of intrinsic dimension $\varrho$. Two main challenges are to derive a fast oracle inequality without assuming a bounded Bayes function and to construct Gaussian approximants that exploit both the intrinsic dimension and the target's regularity without saturation. We address the first challenge through a Bernstein-type inequality whose constant depends linearly on the supremum norm of the predictor. Combined with localized complexity estimates, this yields an oracle inequality over separable normed function spaces. To address the second challenge, we establish a uniform Sobolev operator expansion and cancel its lower-order bias terms using a signed combination of Gaussian kernel integral operators at different bandwidths. Real interpolation then gives an $L_2(X)$ approximation error of order $\gamma^\alpha$ for functions in $B^\alpha_{2,\infty}(X)$, where $\gamma$ is the bandwidth. By constructing the approximants directly on the manifold without ambient extension, we obtain a squared Gaussian RKHS norm bound of order $\gamma^{-\varrho}$. This construction thus overcomes second-order saturation while retaining norm control governed by the intrinsic dimension. When the marginal distribution has a density $g\in L_p(X)$, these results yield the logistic excess-risk rate $\bigl((\log n)^{\varrho+1}/n\bigr)^{4\alpha(1-1/p)/(4\alpha(1-1/p)+3\varrho)}$. We further show that clipping predictions yields the faster clipped logistic excess-risk rate $\bigl((\log n)^{\varrho+2}/n\bigr)^{2\alpha(1-1/p)/(2\alpha(1-1/p)+\varrho)}$. For bounded densities, its polynomial exponent matches that of the minimax optimal rate for squared-loss regression, and its corresponding excess misclassification risk also matches the minimax optimal rate.
#5 Diffusion Score Estimation with Shallow Neural Networks: Regularity, Approximation, and Finite-Sample Rates
diffusion
著者: Fan Liu, Yahong Yang, Wei Zhu
公開日: Wed, 21 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27206
要約:
We study the sample complexity of estimating Ornstein--Uhlenbeck diffusion scores in $\mathbb R^d$ using denoising score matching (DSM) with finite-width shallow ReLU networks, and its implications for reverse-process sampling. Under weak differentiability and polynomial-growth conditions on normalized derivatives of the initial density, we combine complementary estimates from the Gaussian transition kernel and the initial density to obtain explicit Sobolev bounds that are uniform in time on each fixed ball. Combining these bounds with existing shallow-network approximation theory gives explicit control of the approximation error and a sufficient path-norm budget. We then use truncation and localized Rademacher complexity to establish high-probability risk bounds for path-norm-constrained empirical DSM minimizers with unbounded inputs and labels. Under additional bounded-density and sub-Gaussian assumptions, suitable choices of network width and path-norm budget yield squared $L^2(P_t)$ score risk $\widetilde O(n^{-(d+3)/(2d+3)})$ at each fixed time $t>0$, where $n$ is the number of independent training samples and $P_t$ is the forward marginal. The nonasymptotic bounds retain explicit dependence on diffusion time and the regularity and tail parameters of the initial distribution. Aggregating the score errors over a time grid and applying existing sampling theory together with an early-stopping bound yields a high-probability total-variation error $\widetilde O(n^{-(d+3)/(4d+6)})$ between the generated and original data distributions under suitable sampling parameters. Here $\widetilde O$ suppresses logarithmic factors for fixed dimension, confidence level, and initial distribution.
#6 Communication Complexity of Universal Features

リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27402
要約:
Motivated by the notion of features from machine learning, we study the complexity of representing data that allows for evaluation with a given family of functions. Concretely, we consider a deterministic encoder that observes $X\sim D$ and produces a finite encoding before a task $f\in\mathcal{F}\subseteq\{0,1\}^{\mathcal{X}}$ is specified. For each task, a decoder must predict $f(X)$ with error at most $\varepsilon$ under $D$. We construct an encoder that outputs a representation with $O(d^{*}\log(1/\varepsilon))$ bits, where $d^{*}$ is the dual VC dimension of $\mathcal{F}$. Our encoder greedily selects $O((d^{*}/\varepsilon)\log(1/\varepsilon))$ functions from the class and uses their joint label pattern to produce the encoding. The key idea in our upper bound is a novel potential function argument, where we compare the entropy gained from each greedy selection with the number of patterns allowed by the dual VC dimension. We present lower bounds showing that the representation length achieved by our encoder is worst-case optimal for every $d^* \ge 1$ and $\varepsilon < 1/4$ up to a universal constant factor, and instance-wise optimal for every family $\mathcal{F}$ up to an $O(\log(1/\varepsilon))$ factor. We give two ways to learn the representation from unlabeled samples, with different guarantees on the representation size and sample complexity. Experimentally, we verify the greedy construction's accuracy-size tradeoff and explore its connection to neural sparse autoencoders.
#7 Beyond Scalar Leaderboards: Adaptive Sampling for Pareto Frontier Identification in Multi-Objective LLM Arenas

リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27447
要約:
The evaluation of large language models (LLMs) is inherently multi-objective, as different models may offer different tradeoffs across performance dimensions such as math, code, and writing. Because such evaluations are often based on costly and time-consuming human feedback, it is important to draw reliable conclusions using as few comparisons as possible. In this paper, we present Vector-Borda Empirical-Gap Elimination (VB-EGE), a fixed-confidence adaptive sampling algorithm for efficiently identifying models on the Pareto frontier in arena-style evaluation, where each comparison evaluates a pair of models on a prompt with respect to a selected performance dimension. VB-EGE progressively eliminates models from an active set once sufficient statistical evidence has been accumulated to certify their Pareto status. Under a multi-objective Bradley-Terry model, we show that VB-EGE returns the correct Pareto frontier with high probability and derive an instance-dependent upper bound on its sample complexity. We further establish a worst-case lower bound showing that VB-EGE is minimax-optimal up to logarithmic factors. Simulations and experiments on three real-world Arena datasets (Arena, SearchArena, and VisionArena) demonstrate VB-EGE's empirical effectiveness.
#8 What Moved the Alarm Score? Causal Attribution with Exact Computation and Scarce-Incident Estimation
著者: Masaaki Takada, Ryusei Shingaki
公開日: Wed, 21 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27534
要約:
Alarms in industrial systems and scientific instruments often demand immediate diagnosis despite scarce incident data. To guide intervention, an explanation must trace how changes in the data-generating process propagate through the causal structure to alter the alarm score, rather than merely flagging shifts that may be downstream effects. Yet exact attribution across interacting causal components appears combinatorial, while existing methods often either assume an unchanged causal model or require enough incident data to estimate the changed system reliably. We address this setting by formulating the alarm-score change as a Shapley game over hybrid structural causal model (SCM) states. Treating structural functions and exogenous laws as distinct players yields a finer-grained, function/law-resolved report of how each type of change propagates to the alarm score. The same framework defines realized-event and population reports, with an event--population coherence identity linking their attributions. For ordered linear SCMs and quadratic scores, a causal first- and second-moment recursion combined with quadrature computes the full Shapley vector exactly in polynomial time, eliminating combinatorial computation. To estimate this target from scarce incident data, we adapt a well-observed reference SCM to the incident regime using cross-fitted estimates of mechanism changes and residual moments, and derive attribution-error bounds and top-k recovery guarantees. Experiments on controlled and measured shifts illustrate the diagnoses and assess scaling to hundreds of variables and estimation from small incident samples.
#9 Bayesian Neural Networks via Dirichlet Process Priors on the Data-Generating Distribution for Randomized Value Functions in Deep Reinforcement Learning
著者: Sumit Vashishtha
公開日: Wed, 21 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27570
要約:
Bayesian neural networks (BNNs) offer a principled framework for quantifying epistemic uncertainty. However, standard weight-space priors yield complex, high-dimensional neural network posteriors that are computationally prohibitive to sample from, and popular approximate inference schemes often fail to capture epistemic uncertainty effectively---a failure mode particularly acute in reinforcement learning (RL) \citep{osband2018randomized}. To resolve this challenge, we introduce a new class of BNNs that quantify uncertainty by placing Bayesian nonparametric Dirichlet Process (DP) priors directly on the underlying data-generating distribution rather than on network parameters. #10 Ask to Learn: Query-Based Collaborative Reinforcement Learning

要約:
Offline reinforcement learning is fundamentally limited by the coverage of the available data, while collecting additional trajectories through online exploration may be costly, unsafe, or infeasible. We study whether this limitation can be overcome without directly interacting with the underlying environment. Motivated by practical settings such as collaborator-mediated simulation, where a collaborator can provide information complementary to the offline data, we introduce query-based collaborative reinforcement learning to study how to acquire this information efficiently. The learner starts with a fixed offline dataset and adaptively queries state-time pairs; the collaborator samples an action according to a fixed, possibly suboptimal policy, and the simulator returns the corresponding reward and next state. Experiments on multiple synthetic and semi-synthetic environments demonstrate the empirical efficiency of the proposed method.
#11 Annealed correlation and quenched noise sensitivity in biased deep neural networks
著者: Johan Jonasson
公開日: Wed, 21 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27983
要約:
In earlier work by Jonasson, Steif and Zetterqvist (2023), fully connected deep neural networks with layers of width $n$, i.i.d.\ standard Gaussian weights, sign activations and no bias terms were shown to be annealed and quenched noise sensitive with respect to any joint probability distribution $\mathbb{Q}^{(n)}$ on the input $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ and its perturbation $\boldsymbol{\eta}$ satisfying a mild positive-correlation condition and $\mathbb{Q}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{\eta} \in \{\boldsymbol{\omega},-\boldsymbol{\omega}\}) \to 0$. Here we add independent Gaussian biases of the natural order and show that quenched noise sensitivity is retained over a broad range of depths, indeed under the slightly weaker assumption that $\mathbb{Q}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{\omega} = \boldsymbol{\eta}) \to 0$. Thus, subject to the positive-correlation condition, quenched noise sensitivity holds under the minimal requirement that the perturbed input is asymptotically unlikely to coincide with the original input. At the same time, the annealed covariance remains bounded away from zero and is entirely accounted for by fluctuations in the network-dependent output means.
#12 Near-Optimal Regret Bounds for UCB-Style Gaussian Process Bandits via Data Thinning

要約:
Gaussian process upper confidence bound (GP-UCB) has been widely studied, though its known regret upper bound is suboptimal. To obtain near-optimal regret upper bounds, existing studies mainly focus on non-adaptive methods, such as uncertainty sampling, whose practical effectiveness is often limited. This paper shows that, under a Gaussian noise assumption, UCB-style algorithms can achieve near-optimal regret upper bounds via data thinning, a statistical technique for creating independent datasets from a single dataset. First, we show that data thinning can yield tighter confidence intervals in the bandit setting, and that a direct extension of GP-UCB achieves a near-optimal simple regret upper bound. Finally, we demonstrate the effectiveness of UCB-style algorithms incorporating data thinning via numerical experiments.
#13 Predicting silent failures of learned inference under restricted access: what measurement physics and prior information explain
著者: Mario Gianni
公開日: Wed, 21 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28155
要約:
Learned priors are used to reconstruct regions that measurements barely reach, and to report uncertainty there. They can fail silently: the reported interval excludes the truth while every ground-truth-free diagnostic stays within its noise. We call such errors the blind set and ask whether they can be predicted before the target data are seen. For a correctly specified linear-Gaussian model averaged over the prior, the risk of a silent failure factorises exactly into non-detection and non-coverage, which does not hold for learned systems in general; we build a score that approximates this reference by linearising a VAE prior. In a prospective study on real X-ray CT objects (2DeteCT) under simulated restricted access, with predictions committed by hash before any trial, the score ranked the realised blindness of two learned inference systems (latent Laplace and ensemble Kalman inversion) with Spearman $\rho = 0.79$ and 0.84 over 240 pre-specified errors. Simple structural predictors would also have passed these tests. Sequential post-hoc analyses, each specified before its own computation, show that under bounded reach the learned score gave no detectable improvement over a physics-based baseline, in rankings or trial-level log scores. At the weakest attenuation studied, prior information improved rankings over the physics-based baseline, most clearly for ensemble Kalman inversion, but a Gaussian prior did as well and the gain was not detectable at the trial level. The score is not a calibrated probability. Silent failures can be anticipated here, but their structure follows mainly from measurement access and the decision quantity rather than from the learned prior.

Sparse variational Gaussian processes (SVGPs) are a widely used, principled approach to scalable Gaussian process modelling. Recent work has explored modifications of the posterior conditional distribution and has shown that such extensions can improve upon this well-established framework. In particular, introducing a block-diagonal structured scal- ing matrix leads to a tighter lower bound on the GP marginal likelihood. #15 Debiased Prior-Data Fitted Networks for Amortized Bayesian Semi-Parametric Inference
著者: Valentyn Melnychuk, Emil Javurek, Stefan Feuerriegel
公開日: Wed, 21 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28314
要約:
Prior-data fitted networks (PFNs) have recently gained popularity as an end-to-end approach to amortized Bayesian inference. Yet, their statistical behavior for semi-parametric targets, such as means under missingness or causal effects, remains poorly understood. In this work, we develop theoretical foundations for debiased PFNs to perform inference on finite-dimensional targets in general semi-parametric settings. First, we show that, despite being Bayes-optimal with respect to the target, an idealized PFN can exhibit a posterior concentration rate that implicitly depends on the concentration rates of (latent) nuisance functions and can become slower as the prior becomes more diverse. Then, to speed up the target posterior concentration rates, we introduce two generic debiasing strategies based on efficient influence functions: (i) a label correction and (ii) a prior correction. Finally, we show that the debiased PFNs (i) & (ii) possess concentration rates that asymptotically match a convergence rate given by traditional frequentist bias-corrected estimators. In this way, we establish a novel connection between amortized Bayesian inference with PFNs and frequentist semi-parametric efficiency theory. Under additional assumptions, this allows us to guarantee local and global optimality of PFN-based estimators (namely, the Bernstein-von Mises property and minimax optimality, respectively). Therefore, our work provides guidelines for designing debiased PFNs for semi-parametric inference with principled and provable asymptotic guarantees.

Auditors typically use Membership Inference Attacks (MIAs) to estimate a lower bound on the true privacy leakage of DP mechanisms at a specified confidence level, which should not exceed the theoretical privacy upper bound guaranteed by DP. Furthermore, we perform a comprehensive re-analysis of results returned by prior audit implementations and show that without statistical correction, these audits violate the theoretical upper bounds up to four times more often than their claimed confidence level implies. #63 Service-Induced Congestion in Memory-Constrained LLM Serving
著者: Ruicheng Ao, Jing Dong, Gan Luo, David Simchi-Levi
公開日: Wed, 21 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15555
要約:
In large language model (LLM) serving, each request accumulates persistent graphics processing unit (GPU) memory during service as its key-value cache grows with every generated token. Under high concurrency, aggregate memory usage therefore increases endogenously over time: the service process itself creates future capacity pressure. When memory capacity is exceeded, systems evict active requests, discarding cached state and restarting them later, which wastes computation and reduces throughput. We develop a discrete-time dynamical model of memory-constrained LLM inference that captures admission, memory growth, and eviction under continuous batching. In the saturated-input regime, the system admits both eviction-free fixed points and limit cycles with evictions. For homogeneous workloads, we show that the eviction-free equilibrium is unstable and that, except for a Lebesgue-measure-zero exact-capture set, the system converges to a unique worst-case limit cycle that is asymptotically stable outside this exceptional set, with throughput losses as large as 50%. For heterogeneous workloads, we prove a stability criterion in the two-class common-input setting and explain how the survival-polynomial mechanism generalizes to multiple classes and heterogeneous-input lengths. Under an input-dominated scaling regime, coprime decoding lengths stabilize the eviction-free equilibrium, while non-coprime lengths create synchronized modes that drive instability. These results characterize when workload heterogeneity desynchronizes completions and helps stabilize memory-constrained serving. More broadly, we identify service-induced congestion as a structural instability mechanism and derive scheduling design principles for sustaining high throughput.

We study the numerical resolution of uniformly elliptic Dirichlet boundary value problems (BVPs) by minimization of the PDE residual over a hypothesis space of Neural Networks made to satisfy exactly the boundary conditions through multiplication by a user-defined \emph{distance-to-boundary approximation} $\rho$. While an $L^2(\partial\Omega)$ boundary penalty cannot generally guarantee $H^2(\Omega)$ error control, we show that exact enforcement can, provided $\rho$ is chosen appropriately. More precisely, we identify a sharp sufficient condition on $\rho$ preserving the regularity needed for $H^2(\Omega)$ error control, provide practical constructions of such $\rho$ for general smooth domains $\Omega$, and refer to the resulting networks as \emph{boundary-adapted}. For boundary-adapted Rectified Quadratic Unit (ReQU) and hyperbolic tangent (tanh) networks, we then derive explicit high-probability $H^2(\Omega)$ convergence rates by combining approximation-theoretic and statistical-learning arguments, making explicit the dependence on $\rho$. Numerical experiments support our theoretical results, showing that appropriate choices of $\rho$ improve accuracy and convergence, while poorly chosen distance functions can substantially degrade the solution. Our proof also yields new VC-dimension bounds for hypothesis spaces of higher-order derivatives of ReQU and tanh networks, together with new approximation bounds for shallow ReQU networks in higher-order Sobolev norms, all of which are of important independent interest.

We prove sharp spectral-norm bounds and construct a mean-fitting regularization procedure for sparse random tensors of fixed order $k$. For an $n\times\cdots\times n$ tensor with independentBernoulli$(p)$ entries, the centered norm is $O(\sqrt{np})$ with polynomially high probability whenever $np\ge c\log n$. This removes the logarithmic loss in Zhou and Zhu (2021). We also determine the expected centered norm at every sparsity level: for every $0<p\le1/2$, it has order $\min\{n^kp,\sqrt{d_{\mathrm{eff}}}\}$, where $d_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is an effective degree accounting for unusually large fiber counts. The upper bounds extend to tensors with deterministic entrywise weights and unequal sampling probabilities. Analogous two-sided bounds hold for the Friedman--Wigderson model at every degree; its expected centered norm has order $\sqrt{d_{\mathrm{eff}}}$. We also give an all-sparsity regularization procedure for independent Bernoulli tensors with unknown, possibly unequal means in $[0,p]$. Our light-fitting regularization estimates the original mean with minimax expected spectral-norm error of order $\min\{\sqrt{np},pn^{k/2}\}$ for every known $0\le p\le1$. The procedure fits light multilinear residuals under an entrywise constraint on the candidate mean, rather than deleting high-degree fibers.

The rank $r$ in LoRA is widely treated as a capacity control: a smaller rank is assumed to yield a simpler model that generalizes better. We show that, under hard per-factor norm budgets---the idealization of the weight decay and norm control used in practice---this intuition breaks down. The reason is structural: under such budgets, the updates LoRA can reach are exactly the matrices of rank at most $r$ inside a nuclear-norm ball, and every complexity and displacement functional we analyze is maximized over this set by a rank-one update---so the rank cap never binds. The consequences follow directly. The linear-readout model class we study is identical for every $r \ge 1$, its Rademacher complexity carries no dependence on $r$, and the distance the adaptation can move the source distribution obeys a rank-independent upper bound that we show is sharp. If rank does not control capacity, where does it act? We identify two places. Statistically, replacing the per-factor budgets with a joint budget on the product restores a data-dependent, rank-sensitive complexity bound---though the gain appears only for well-spread feature distributions, and the worst case remains rank-free. Spectrally, rank sets the price of adaptation: canceling the leading singular directions of the pretrained weight requires both sufficient rank and sufficient budget. We bound the smallest rank achieving a desired source--target alignment, with upper and lower bounds that match under two-sided spectral decay. Together, these results recast rank as governing which updates are reachable and what cancellation costs---not how much capacity the model has.

要約:
Several state-of-the-art methods for online reinforcement learning in continuous control improve policies using action gradients of a learned critic. However, critics are typically trained to predict returns, and accurate value predictions do not necessarily yield accurate action derivatives, potentially leading to unreliable policy updates. We propose Forward Entropy-Regularized Policy Optimization (FERPO), an on-policy maximum entropy reinforcement learning algorithm that performs policy improvement using critic values without differentiating the critic with respect to actions. FERPO derives an optimal target action distribution from a policy-improvement objective regularized by entropy and Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence. We then fit the actor to this target by minimizing a forward-KL objective, estimated using self-normalized importance sampling (SNIS) with actions drawn from the rollout policy. By limiting the target distribution's deviation from the rollout policy, the KL regularization helps keep these importance weights well behaved. In contrast to reverse-KL objectives, which can favor a subset of the target distribution's modes, the forward-KL objective encourages coverage of multiple high-value modes and thereby promotes exploration. Experiments and ablations on MuJoCo Playground and ManiSkill show competitive performance and sample-efficiency gains. Computational benchmarks also demonstrate faster actor updates than Relative Entropy Pathwise Policy Optimization (REPPO).

#70 Truncate or Not? A Scaling Study of Looped Transformers with Truncated Backpropagation

Looped transformers reuse a shared block of layers to increase computational depth without increasing the number of trainable parameters. They are trained with backpropagation through time (BPTT). To save training compute, BPTT is often truncated to the last few loops, but truncation yields biased gradients that may weaken the benefit of looping. In this work, we study the value of truncation in looped transformer training in a systematic way: we jointly vary model size, training tokens, and forward and backward loop counts across nearly 600 training runs and fit a unified scaling law that captures the effects of looping and truncation through effective parameters. Despite the training-compute savings of truncation, our fitted law favors full BPTT when forward and backward loop counts are jointly optimized. In matched-compute runs, full BPTT through fewer loops consistently outperforms truncated settings of the same compute. The law further shows that at fixed model size, the compute-optimal loop count and training-token budget both grow with compute. When model size is also free, the law identifies two fully trained loops as the best looped setting. Experimentally, this setting achieves lower validation loss than the compute-optimal non-looped model with about 27% fewer non-embedding parameters and only 8% more training compute.

This paper considers the Bayesian top-$k$ identification problem, wherein a decision maker is tasked with selecting $k$ items from a pool of items of unknown quality. To assess the items, the decision maker adaptively allocates a fixed sampling budget, where each sample of an item yields a noisy observation of its quality, with the objective of minimizing the expected opportunity cost of the selected set. We propose an approach that allocates each batch of samples by minimizing a surrogate objective derived from an upper bound on the expected opportunity cost. The approach is general: samples can be allocated in batches, the selection size $k$ is arbitrary, and it applies to many sampling distributions. It is also simple and intuitive. We prove that the method's posterior expected opportunity cost decays at an exponential rate of at least half the best rate achievable by any sampling rule. In simulations across a wide range of settings, the method is competitive with, and often improves upon, the best existing algorithm in each setting.
