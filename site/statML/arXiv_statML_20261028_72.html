#1 Anchored Stein Variational Gradient Descent Algorithm for Non-smooth Sampling
著者: Thanh Dang, Matthew X. Burns, Jiaming Liang
公開日: Wed, 28 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36018
要約:
We introduce a Stein variational framework for sampling from a target density $p\propto e^{-U}$ when the target potential $U$ is possibly non-smooth. The key idea is to introduce a smoother reference potential $U_0$ and the anchor weight $a=e^{U-U_0}$. The resulting anchored Stein variational gradient descent (SVGD) algorithm is able to target $p\propto e^{-U}$ without computing the gradient of $U$ and include standard SVGD as a special case. In particular, we allow the target potential $U$ to be locally Lipschitz while the reference potential $U_0$ is continuously differentiable, along with other suitable assumptions. Under this low regularity regime, we establish a Kullback-Leibler first variation formula for anchored perturbations, identify the corresponding steepest descent direction in the reproducing kernel Hilbert space and prove a nonasymptotic convergence bound for the anchored SVGD algorithm in terms of an anchored kernelized Stein discrepancy. Finally, we also provide guidance on constructing a suitable reference potential $U_0$ based on $U$.
#2 Learning on-the-fly quasi-stationarity diagnostics from low-dimensional surrogates
著者: No\'e Blassel
公開日: Wed, 28 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36078
要約:
Accelerated dynamics methods, such as parallel replica dynamics, require estimates of the decorrelation time, after which a process trapped in one of its metastable states is distributed according to the quasi-stationary distribution (QSD). In a method proposed by Binder, Leli\`evre and Simpson, decorrelation times are defined by coupling a particle approximation of the non-linear conditioned probability flow of the process with MCMC-inspired diagnostics. We explore an alternative to MCMC diagnostics, based on coarse-graining of non-linear probability flows and simulation-based inference. We first develop a coarse-graining framework for killed Markov jump processes conditioned on survival, proving uniform-in-time, non-asymptotic estimates approximating the non-linear probability flow of the true process by the non-linear flow of a surrogate process, defined with a collective variable. The framework does not assume reversibility of the true process, and also gives bounds which scale explicitly in an adiabatic regime. Motivated by these results, we then construct a quasi-stationarity diagnostic using simulation-based inference: a recurrent neural network (RNN), trained on particle approximations of non-linear effective flows, estimates posterior probabilities of decorrelation. The learned diagnostic is then transferred to collective-variable traces of high-dimensional particle approximations. We investigate the behavior and robustness of this diagnostic on a set of numerical experiments, comparing it to the MCMC diagnostic of Binder et al.\ on both synthetic examples and a molecular system, in and out of its training prior distribution. When its prior assumptions hold, the RNN diagnostic outperforms the MCMC diagnostic in ROC analysis, is less conservative, fails less often, and has an interpretable decision threshold.
#3 Symmetry Breaking Is the Price of Finite Capacity
著者: Maggie Chlon, Leon Chlon
公開日: Wed, 28 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36138
要約:
If rotating a problem never changes its answer, one might expect its best model to be rotation-invariant; we show that this fails, predictably and by an exact amount, when capacity is limited. A symmetry groups a model's directions into blocks of equally strong directions, a symmetric model must use each block whole, and when the budget cuts a block every optimal model breaks the symmetry. For a Gaussian problem with a $k$-unit bilinear predictor, the log-loss price of symmetry is exactly $(U_k-I_k)/(2\sigma^2)$, the signal of the best $k$ directions minus that of the best whole blocks. Because an attention head compares tokens through a similarity of rank at most its head dimension, whatever computation precedes it, the same price governs heads fitted to a symmetric similarity by squared error, and heads trained this way on three-dimensional directions break symmetry at exactly the predicted head dimensions, with the predicted rotation sensitivity (RMSE 0.004). Under softmax attention, flatter attention restores the predicted symmetric head where tested, while with peaked attention broken heads beat every whole-shell symmetric head at 16 of 20 head dimensions. We give a procedure that separates capacity-driven breaking from other causes.
#4 Anytime Valid Confidence Bands for Distribution Functions
著者: Bror Hultberg, Dave Zachariah, Ant\^onio H. Ribeiro
公開日: Wed, 28 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36149
要約:
Nonparametric inference often requires characterizing an unknown function, such as a cumulative distribution function (CDF), across its entire domain rather than a specific point. We develop confidence bands for distribution functions of a data stream that are valid simultaneously over all times and all points, even when each observation may depend arbitrarily on the past. We begin by deriving such bands for the time-averaged conditional CDF and show how their widths are determined by the depth of a binary search tree. We then show that this result is an instance of a framework through which one can construct valid confidence bands uniformly over time and over an arbitrary index set for any indexed family of time-averaged distribution functions. We illustrate the framework on importance-weighted and right-censored CDFs, as well as multivariate CDFs.
#5 Approximation Families and the Statistical Efficiency of Denoising Score Matching
著者: Brian Miyatake
公開日: Wed, 28 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36205
要約:
We study score matching over finite-dimensional approximation spaces generated by gradients of eigenfunctions of the infinitesimal generator of a forward diffusion. We compare polynomial approximation under one-dimensional Ornstein-Uhlenbeck flow with trigonometric-polynomial approximation under heat flow on the torus. In the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck setting, we show that for every non-Gaussian initial distribution uniformly comparable to the standard Gaussian, the asymptotic score-estimation variance grows exponentially along a subsequence of polynomial degrees. Averaging the score-matching loss under the diffusion semigroup, which we call operator-informed score matching (OISM), reduces the variance at each fixed degree but does not eliminate this exponential instability at sufficiently small times. A local minimax lower bound shows that the same high-degree dependence arises when estimating the polynomial projection of the score, rather than being an artifact of OISM. In contrast, under heat flow on the torus, OISM over trigonometric polynomials admits finite-sample control of the score error along the forward diffusion and yields the classical minimax rate for estimating sufficiently smooth densities under expected Kullback-Leibler loss.
#6 Second-order Sinkhorn Geometry and $\sqrt{n}$-Inference
著者: Tristan Luca Saidi, Florian Gunsilius, Gonzalo Mena
公開日: Wed, 28 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36488
要約:
The Sinkhorn divergence, a debiased form of entropic optimal transport, has been widely used in various scientific disciplines due to its computational and statistical tractability. Unlike the Wasserstein distance, however, the Sinkhorn divergence is not a metric and it does not naturally admit a formal Riemannian structure. These properties are central to many applications of optimal transport methods that utilize interpolation, barycentric averaging, parallel transport or other geometric operations. Recent work addresses these shortcomings by constructing a formal Riemannian tangent space and a metric tensor on the space of probability measures that quadratically approximates the Sinkhorn divergence. In this work, we extend this framework with two major threads of contributions. For a suitable class of finite-smoothness cost functions, we prove Hadamard differentiability of the Sinkhorn metric tensor and we derive the Levi-Civita covariant derivative in this geometry; we then use the covariant derivative to obtain the geodesic and parallel transport equations, each of which amount to an ODE on a fixed Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS) with smooth coefficients. Our second contribution is to linearize the solutions to the geodesic and parallel transport equations using standard tools from Banach space ODE theory. We show that under standard regularity conditions these linearizations imply $\sqrt{n}$-limiting Gaussian processes centered at population values for the plugin estimators of the exponential map, the logarithmic map, parallel transport and the induced Sinkhorn distance. Moreover, we show that the nonparametric bootstrap is consistent for all objects mentioned. To the best of our knowledge, these are the first $\sqrt{n}$--limit theorems and bootstrap consistency results for canonical geometric operations in a transport-based geometry on the space of probability measures.

Statistical graphical models are representations of the dependences between the components of a random vector. They encode these dependences via graphical separation criteria. We consider the algorithmic problem of checking separation statements for the various graph classes and separation criteria that have been developed by the community. We establish that all criteria we study can be checked by modular variations of reachability. For all separation criteria but $\sigma$-separation, this yields a linear-time algorithm. #9 Boundary Geometry Determines Optimal Shallow ReLU Approximation under Weighted Coefficient Constraints
model extraction
著者: Jia-Qi Yang, Lei Shi
公開日: Wed, 28 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36854
要約:
We study how boundary geometry affects $L^q$ approximation by shallow ReLU neural networks on convex bodies $K\subset\mathbb R^d$. The target classes are unit balls of weighted variation spaces that can enlarge classical spaces by incorporating domain geometry into constraints on output coefficients. We establish upper and lower bounds for arbitrary convex bodies and determine the optimal polynomial approximation exponents for all planar convex bodies and their products with polytopes, including cubes and circular cylinders. For $C^2$ convex bodies, we determine the optimal exponents in a dimension-dependent range of $q$ extending beyond $2$. When all principal curvatures are positive, the result extends to every finite $q\ge2$. For these classes, the dependence of the optimal exponent on the domain is captured by a single geometric growth exponent associated with small boundary caps. We also identify sharp weight thresholds for retaining the classical approximation exponent and improve previous weights on Euclidean balls while preserving the classical $L^2$ approximation rate. When the target is given as a finite ReLU network, the upper bounds can be realized by a compressed network using only the original neurons, without increasing the weighted coefficient sum. These results show that boundary geometry can change the optimal exponent even when dimension, error norm, and weighting rule are fixed.
#10 Intrinsic Information-Theoretic Analysis of ReLU Nets

リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36864
要約:
Mutual information (MI) is fundamental to representation-learning analysis in deep networks, but in high dimensions MI estimators depend on hyperparameters whose bias often dominates the signal. We show that continuous piecewise-linear (CPWL) networks, including ReLU networks, admit an intrinsic information-theoretic analysis that avoids this pitfall: their inherent partition of the input space into convex linear regions assigns to every input a discrete region label $\Pi$ alongside the continuous representation $T$ and label $Y$. We introduce a decomposition of the mutual information $I(Y;T)$ into interpretable geometric terms. We show that the dominant term, the *routing information* $I(Y; \Pi)$, admits an estimable plug-in estimator, that in the well-sampled regime, approximates a population lower bound on the joint information $I(Y; \Pi, T)$. The routing estimator agrees strongly with three established MI baselines applied to the same networks and data. The functional quotient recovers informative estimates in the regime bordering the well-sampled and under-sampled regime. We provide a framework for analysing the internal information geometry of CPWL networks, grounded in their inherent piecewise-linear structure rather than in external discretisation.

We propose a test of whether two interventions induce the same outcome distribution when outcomes are structured and high-dimensional, such as images, text and graphs. #12 Low-degree polynomials lower bounds for clustering in small dimension
著者: Alexandra Carpentier (MISTEA), Nicolas Verzelen (MISTEA)
公開日: Wed, 28 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37272
要約:
We study the computational limits of clustering $n$ observations drawn from a mixture of $K$ isotropic distributions in $\mathbb R^d$, in the regime where the number $K$ of groups is much larger than the dimension $d$. In this regime, tensor-based procedures recover the partition with a polylogarithmic separation between the centers, but they require a sample size that is a large and non-explicit polynomial of $dK$, whereas the existing low-degree lower bounds only show that $n$ must be larger than $dK^2$. We introduce a mixture of $2K$ product Bernoulli distributions on $\{0,1\}^d$, with $K=(d+1)^m$, whose centers are built from a dual BCH code. This construction ensures that the moment tensors of the observations coincide with those of the uniform distribution on $\{-1,1\}^d$ up to the order $2m+1$. For any fixed $m$, we establish a low-degree polynomial lower bound showing that clustering is hard as long as the separation $\Delta$ satisfies $\Delta^2\ll(K^2d^{3/2}/n)^{1/(2m+2)}\wedge\sqrt d$, up to polylogarithmic factors. In particular, with a polylogarithmic separation, low-degree polynomials require $n\gtrsim K^2d^{3/2}$ observations, up to polylogarithmic factors. Through a Gaussianization argument, this hardness carries over, in the sense of reductions, to isotropic Gaussian mixtures. Our proof relies on a family of polynomials of the observations, indexed by bipartite graphs, which is almost orthonormal. The moments of these polynomials are governed by the number of solutions of polynomial systems over the finite field $\mathbb F\_{d+1}$ that are induced by the BCH code, and the crux of our analysis is to connect the combinatorial structure of the bipartite graphs with the structure of the solution sets of these systems.
#13 An end-to-end framework for uncertainty-aware validation of generative models
著者: Giada Badaracco, Sean Benevedes, Christina Reissel, Philip Harris, Gaia Grosso
公開日: Wed, 28 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37398
要約:
Density estimation with generative models is a common task in the physical sciences, for example in background estimation in particle physics. Existing machine learning techniques, however, lack methods to quantify uncertainties that can be propagated into a statistical test. That limits the validation of generative models to standard goodness-of-fit tests, in which the generative model is treated as exact (point-null hypothesis). But a model fitted to a finite training set is never exact, so if its uncertainties are neglected, any sufficiently powerful goodness-of-fit test will eventually reject it once the test set is large enough to resolve this estimation error. We propose SIGMA, an end-to-end framework combining density estimation with principled uncertainty quantification. We use Gaussian mixture models, for which uncertainties are tractable, and further construct a new goodness-of-fit test in which these uncertainties are profiled as nuisance parameters to account for the statistical error of the fit, promoting the conventional point-null test to a composite one. A rejection then signals genuine mismodeling rather than a test set large enough to resolve the error of the fit, provided the propagated uncertainties faithfully cover that error. We demonstrate the framework on a controlled two-dimensional toy model and on a collider physics benchmark modeling the QCD background process. With uncertainties included, the same model can be trusted on test sets up to an order of magnitude larger than the standard test allows. Our work thus provides a statistically grounded basis for a robust use of generative models in the physical sciences.

#16 When Does Data Value Reduce to Class Balance? A Coverage View of Per-Point Data Valuation
著者: Yingjie Bai, Weiming Zhi
公開日: Wed, 28 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34801
要約:
Per-point data-valuation scores -- Data Shapley, influence, leverage -- are routinely used to select training data: keep the points with the top $k$ scores. We argue that top-$k$ by a fixed, mode-blind per-point score is the wrong selection rule precisely in the regime where data selection matters. The reason is that a point's value is not a fixed number but depends on what else has been selected -- a near-duplicate of an already-chosen point adds almost nothing, however high its score -- and a per-point scalar cannot express this interaction. The theory quantifies this failure exactly: in an equal latent-mode model, fixed top-$k$ selection incurs a closed-form excess risk over adaptive coverage because it repeatedly spends budget on already-covered modes. The gap is largest when the budget is tight, the learner representation is redundant, and missing a mode is costly. The practical rule is simple: use balance when the labels available at selection identify the learner's modes, and use explicit coverage when they do not. On standard classification tasks with strong pretrained features, class-balancing the same per-point scores recovers the missing coverage and matches or outperforms generic set coverage across our datasets. Without labels, label-free coverage improves accuracy by up to +0.20 while raw top-$k$ can fall below random; when labels are coarser than the modes, coverage within coarse strata performs best. We turn this distinction into a pre-specified diagnostic and validate it across both favorable and stress-test settings. The limits are part of the claim: the advantage is representation-relative. Coverage fails when the relevant modes are not expressed in the learner geometry, as with spurious subgroups, or when data quality varies independently of coverage -- exactly as the theory predicts.

We present LatentLieODE, a novel neural ODE method to discover linear Lie symmetries and first integrals of the learned dynamics from timeseries observations such as video inputs. It starts with an autoencoder, which learns a low dimensional representation of the observed system and then maps trajectories to decorrelated latent coordinates $z \in \mathbb{R}^d$.
