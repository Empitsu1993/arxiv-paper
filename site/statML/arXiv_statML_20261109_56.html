#1 Drift-admissibility via Optimal Transport
著者: Abigail Langbridge, Enrico Biffis, Jakub Mare\v{c}ek, Robert Shorten
公開日: Mon, 09 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08505
要約:
We introduce structure-aware optimal transport diagnostics for assessing whether empirical time-series dynamics are compatible with a prescribed predictable-drift class. Our approach translates a pathwise property of stochastic processes into a finite-dimensional projection problem over empirical transition laws, encoding admissibility as row-wise moment constraints on local couplings and quantifying deviations via a Kullback--Leibler projection gap $G_{\lambda,k}$. This yields both a principled diagnostic and a scalable Bregman--Dykstra algorithm that retains the efficiency of Sinkhorn-type methods while incorporating structural constraints. Calibrated against a Brownian reference null, $G_{\lambda,k}$ separates process classes that are indistinguishable by second-order statistics alone---including fractional Brownian motion and BSS processes sharing identical Hurst indices---without asymptotic resampling or continuous-time recovery. This positions the method as a general tool for structure-aware model validation of sequential data.
#2 ELPAC: Endpoint-Anchored Latent Progression with Stage-Varying Multimodal Coordination
著者: Shengxian Ding, Yifei Zhang, Xinyuan Tian, Yuanshuang Guo, Yize Zhao
公開日: Mon, 09 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08541
要約:
Aging cohorts increasingly combine imaging, plasma, and cognitive measurements, but most subjects provide only a partially observed cross-sectional snapshot, leaving latent stage, progression subtype, and stage-varying multimodal coordination to be inferred jointly. Existing methods estimate the cohort-level aging process, model covariance under observed covariates, or fuse modalities for prediction. However, they rarely target the joint problem of endpoint-anchored staging and progression-resolved residual coordination across modalities, even though subject staging, subtype assignment, and multimodal interplay are mutually dependent. We introduce ELPAC, a Bayesian framework with two interlocking constructions. The first integrates subtype trajectories backward from clinically reliable endpoint groups along biologically structured velocity components encoding modality-specific monotone direction and optional sequential ordering priors. The second factorizes residual multimodal variation around the inferred stage axis into sparse coordination axes whose loadings and activations expose which multimodal patterns are operative in each subtype and when they emerge, with stage-resolved variation supplying the auxiliary structure for conditional identifiability. We use a scalable amortized variational inference scheme that enables joint posterior inference at cohort scale with single-pass deployment on new subjects. On synthetic data and a harmonized ADNI+NACC/SCAN Alzheimer's cohort, ELPAC demonstrates accurate latent-structure recovery, well-calibrated prediction intervals, and biologically interpretable multimodal coordination programs that differ by subtype.
#3 pESTO: Iterative p-greedy kernel regression with risk-driven Early STOpping
著者: L. Bruni Bruno, P. Massa, E. Perracchione, M. Trombini
公開日: Mon, 09 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08555
要約:
Kernel-based greedy algorithms select suitable approximation nodes by minimizing point-wise error indicators, such as the power function; in this context, they are usually referred to as p-greedy approaches. In the presence of noisy data, deterministic criteria may fail, as the discrepancy between the true solution and its estimate is influenced by both approximation error and noise amplification, and the balance between the two is unknown. This study explicitly separates these two contributions, which are governed by the power function and a Lebesgue-type term, respectively. These bounds motivate the use of the Stein's unbiased risk estimator (SURE) as a proxy of the mean squared error, which in turn provides a principled and computable stopping criterion for the number of nodes to select in the p-greedy procedure. Numerical experiments demonstrate that this approach effectively balances deterministic accuracy and statistical noise contributions.
#4 Blind Noise Equalization for Large Data Matrices under General Variance Profiles
著者: Boris Landa, Francesco Strino, Fabio Parisi, Ruiqi Li, Yuval Kluger
公開日: Mon, 09 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08784
要約:
Low-rank approximation is a basic tool for inference from large and noisy data matrices. However, when noise variances differ across entries, a setting known as heteroskedasticity, the noise can distort the observed spectrum, producing spurious components and obscuring weak signals. To address these challenges, we develop BRINE (Blind Resolvent-based Iterative Noise Equalizer), a data-driven procedure that iteratively rescales rows and columns to equalize the average noise variance across both dimensions. Under suitable conditions, this procedure restores the Marchenko-Pastur law for the noise spectrum and can amplify signal components without prior knowledge of the signal or noise structure. A key idea underlying our approach is to equalize the diagonal entries of the data resolvent as a proxy for equalizing the unknown noise variances. At the population level, we identify a potential function that characterizes this equalization and motivates an iterative minimization procedure. We prove convergence of the iterations to the true equalizer for arbitrary positive variance profiles, with a geometric rate under mild conditions. The updates can be estimated directly from noisy data, and we establish probabilistic error bounds for the scaling factors. Simulations and experiments across diverse biological data modalities and preprocessing pipelines illustrate the effectiveness of our approach.
#5 High-Dimensional Off-Policy Evaluation without Sample Splitting using an Onsager Correction
著者: Saksham Jain, Guillaume Braun, Masaaki Imaizumi
公開日: Mon, 09 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08794
要約:
Reward models used for off-policy evaluation (OPE) are commonly selected using held-out data. When sample size and context dimension grow proportionally, the difference in test risk between models fitted on the full sample and on a fixed fraction of it need not vanish. Held-out evaluation can therefore consistently estimate the test risk of the model trained on the smaller sample, but because the relative ranking of candidate reward models can change after full-data refitting, held-out selection can be suboptimal. We develop an Onsager-corrected, target-policy-weighted criterion that estimates the full-data reward model's out-of-sample risk under the target policy using only its training observations. This enables early stopping along the full-data gradient-descent trajectory without sample splitting. For neural networks with fixed depth and hidden width under Gaussian contexts, single-index rewards, context-dependent multi-index target policies, and known context-independent logging, we prove uniform consistency of our criterion over a fixed finite set of gradient-descent iterates and a near-oracle guarantee for the selected reward model. The same correction yields a doubly robust estimator that consistently estimates the true policy value without sample splitting, even when reward-model prediction error does not vanish. Experiments show decreasing risk-estimation error as dimension grows at a fixed sample-to-dimension ratio, and improved reward-model selection relative to held-out baselines. Empirically, our correction also reduces the root mean squared error of doubly robust value estimates computed from the same data used to fit and select the reward models.
#6 Beyond the Lower-Star Filtration: Alternative Filtration Methods for Persistent Homology
著者: Innocent Abaa, Umar Islambekov
公開日: Mon, 09 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08833
要約:
In the lower-star filtration (LSF), simplices are assigned filtration values according to the maximum-vertex rule, with each simplex entering the filtration when its highest-valued vertex appears. A limitation of this construction is that some higher-dimensional simplices may enter simultaneously with their boundary faces, causing certain topological features to be filled immediately upon formation and thus not appear in the resulting persistence diagram. To address this limitation, we propose three alternative filtration methods: the \(\varepsilon\)-lower-star filtration (ELSF), the \(L_p\) filtration (LpF), and the softmax filtration (SMF). {ELSF introduces a dimension-dependent delay to the maximum-vertex rule,} allowing higher-dimensional simplices to enter the filtration after their boundary faces, whereas {LpF and SMF use aggregation functions that depend on all vertex values rather} than solely on their maximum. By modifying how filtration values are assigned, these methods allow simplices to enter the filtration more progressively, so that topological features such as loops and voids may persist over longer filtration intervals rather than being filled immediately. Consequently, the resulting persistence diagrams may include persistence features that are not observed under LSF, potentially providing useful information for distinguishing data populations. Experimental results on simulated and real-world datasets demonstrate that the proposed filtration methods are computationally efficient and can provide greater sensitivity to structural differences in the data than LSF.
#7 Geometry-Aware Score-Repellent Monte Carlo
著者: Jie Hu, Lingyun Chen, Do Young Eun
公開日: Mon, 09 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08909
要約:
The recently proposed Score-Repellent Monte Carlo (SRMC) method improves MCMC sampling by tilting the target density $\pi$ in $\mathbb{R}^d$ with the factor $\exp(-\alpha\,\theta_n^\top s(x))$, where the score function $s(x) = \nabla_x \log \pi(x)$, and $\theta_n$ is the score average from past samples. However, a single scalar repellence strength $\alpha\ge0$ cannot handle both the steep and the flat directions in anisotropic energy landscapes at once. We introduce \emph{Geometry-Aware SRMC} (GA-SRMC), which generalizes the Euclidean alignment to $\theta^\top M s(x)$ for any symmetric positive-definite matrix $M$. A stochastic-approximation central limit theorem under a trace constraint on $M$ identifies the trace-normalized inverse Fisher $M_{\rm opt}\propto S^{-1}$ as the unique minimizer of the worst-direction covariance bound, elevating the Fisher information $S=\text{Cov}_\pi(s,s)$ from a proposal-side preconditioner (e.g., FisherMALA, natural gradient) to the bound-optimal repulsion-side matrix, with a single online estimate $\widehat S_n$ serving both roles at no additional computational cost. The full-Fisher online metric $M_n$ converges almost surely to $M_{\rm opt}$ and satisfies a CLT, providing a theoretical guarantee for the online implementation of the GA-SRMC framework. On an $8$-target simulation spanning the condition number $\kappa\in[1.3,100]$ and dimension $d\in[2,785]$, GA-SRMC dominates SRMC in ESS by $\boldsymbol{4.3\times}$ on anisotropic Gaussian ($\kappa=100$) and $\boldsymbol{6.8\times}$ on MNIST under the MALA baseline, by $\boldsymbol{16\text{--}32\%}$ on three well-conditioned logistic-regression posteriors, and by up to $\boldsymbol{+48\%}$ under FisherMALA; gains peak on the most anisotropic targets, and posterior-mean MSE is lower than with SRMC in all $16$ full-Fisher implementations.

We study kernel-based learning of analytic operators from finite samples, with a focus on how the regularity of the target should determine the choice of the kernel. We establish embeddings of analytic operators into vector-valued reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces and derive finite-sample, high-probability guarantees that quantify the uncertainty due to random sampling: in the noiseless setting, suitable analytic kernels yield rates exponential in a fractional power of the sample size, while Sobolev-type kernels yield polynomial rates. 要約:
Language-model systems can select prompts and parameter adapters repeatedly during deployment, using feedback to guide later choices. Earlier choices may change the context or environment inherited by later configurations. We study selection among preconstructed candidates and compare cumulative loss with the best fixed prompt--parameter pair used from the beginning. This excess loss separates into three terms: selection regret within the available family, the gap from restricting that family, and the benefit or harm of the actual deployment history. Selection is evaluated using the history each candidate would have produced if used throughout, which separates candidate quality from the effects of switching. When loss functions are fixed independently of selector randomness, past choices affect loss for only a bounded number of rounds, and every candidate's own-history loss is revealed, limiting switches yields sublinear expected regret within the selected family. #10 Machine Learning Beyond Statistical Consistency for Hybrid AI
著者: Jinsook Kim, Jinho Kang
公開日: Mon, 09 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01914
要約:
We propose a new definition of machine learning, according to which a machine learns a function when it computes the function successfully, that is, when the value it returns for each input has a proper derivation in a formal system. We extend G\"odel's Representability Theorem, which equates computation with derivation, to empirical targets in two ways: the theorem is extended to the computable reals, and the data are admitted into the system as empirical definitions that record what has been observed. We prove that a machine learns a true probability if and only if two conditions are met. The Truth Condition is that its estimate converges to the true probability, which is statistical consistency. The Assurance Condition is that the machine properly derives its own convergence inside the system. Learning thus lies beyond statistical consistency, for it requires a derivation of the convergence from the record. A global assumption about the data, such as the i.i.d. assumption, does not supply such a derivation, because it leads only to a circular one, as we show for PAC learning. On the other hand, the two conditions are met for the probability of a sentence over a finite ideal corpus. A real machine can approximate a true probability only if an ideal machine can learn it precisely, that is, only in a domain that meets the two conditions. Modern AI works in such a domain, and we offer this as a clue to its success. Modern AI is hybrid as well, in two senses: First, it performs two kinds of tasks, namely probabilistic ones such as predicting the next word and deductive ones such as proving and coding. Second, it combines two parts of different kinds, a statistical proposer and a deductive verifier, for proving and coding. Our framework explains both senses of hybridity in one formal system.

#12 On Lipschitz Explosion in Deep Neural Networks with Normalization: Consequences for Optimization and Adversarial Robustness
著者: Ashkan Soleymani, Reyhaneh Hosseinpourkhoshkbari, Hadi Daneshmand, Patrick Jaillet
公開日: Mon, 09 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08775
要約:
Can a network look stable at every layer and still be exponentially unstable as a whole? The Lipschitz constant of a neural network is a basic stability quantity appearing in optimization guarantees, generalization bounds, and robustness certificates. Deep networks naturally admit two such notions, input Lipschitzness, measuring sensitivity to data perturbations, and parameter Lipschitzness, measuring sensitivity to weight perturbations. We prove that, for deep networks with normalization layers, with batch normalization as the canonical example, both Lipschitz constants can grow exponentially with depth and hence with parameter dimension, even under strong per-layer norm control such as $\|W_\ell\|_2\le 1$, and already for linear activations. Thus, a mechanism widely used to stabilize training can create severe worst-case instabilities that are invisible locally, i.e., from layerwise norm bounds alone. Parameter-Lipschitz explosion turns Lipschitz-dependent nonsmooth optimization guarantees into exponential-in-dimension bounds for deep normalized networks, while input-Lipschitz explosion makes worst-case Lipschitz-based generalization and robustness certificates vacuous. The latter also yields a concrete rank-separation mechanism for adversarial vulnerability. The theory predicts that perturbations introducing new singular directions should be amplified much more strongly than perturbations at the same noise level that remain within the input's original singular subspace. Experiments on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR-10 support this prediction, showing that rank-creating perturbations cause substantially sharper drops in accuracy, confidence, and margins than same-subspace perturbations.
#13 Reweighting for Transfer Classification: Exact Risk Conversion and Minimax Adaptation
著者: Aoran Chen, Yang Feng
公開日: Mon, 09 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08876
要約:
A source can make a training mixture easier to learn while making its accuracy less valuable for the target. We study how the training problem a source creates and the finite-sample error of learning it jointly determine target-risk guarantees under reweighting. For binary classification, for each pair of laws and each positive target weight, we derive the sharpest target excess hinge-risk bound implied by mixture excess alone, uniformly over clipped predictors. This exact conversion curve is determined by density-weighted local votes. A two-sample oracle inequality for weighted support vector machines supplies the mixture excess, and the composition becomes explicit for Gaussian kernels. On a tilted-boundary class, we establish the all-procedure minimax classification rate $[n+s_+^2/(m^{-1}+\tau^2)]^{-1}$ in the transferable strength $s_+$ and the tilt $\tau$, separating source sampling uncertainty ($m^{-1}$) from relation uncertainty ($\tau^2$). On a separate smooth opposition class, the minimax risk within the family of exact Gaussian SVMs fitted to the original labels with nonnegative weights is exponentially larger than the unrestricted minimax risk whenever the target sample is at most half the source sample, despite arbitrary data-dependent weighting and tuning. The value of a source thus depends on the training problem it creates, on what accuracy there means for the target, and on whether the permitted use of the source can implement the needed correction.
#14 Ordered Policy Optimization
著者: Dongkyu Derek Cho, Pan Xu
公開日: Mon, 09 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08936
要約:
Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) stabilizes policy updates by clipping the importance ratio between the new policy and the data-collecting policy. The standard rule uses one fixed clipping range for all samples. While this choice offers simplicity and effectiveness, it fails to distinguish statistically harmful extreme ratio values from large, yet tolerable changes. We study how to adapt the clipping radius to the reliability of the induced importance ratio distribution. Our case study demonstrates that reliable updates require controlling these extreme ratios, rather than merely verifying that individual ratios fall within a fixed interval. Motivated by this observation, we propose Ordered Policy Optimization (\textsc{OPO}). In each minibatch, \textsc{OPO} sorts the importance ratios, selects the extreme ratios that potentially have the largest effect on the update, and assigns them adaptive sample-wise radii instead of using the fixed clipping radius. These radii are derived from a prescribed tail profile, which specifies desired target values for the selected extreme ratios. The resulting objective remains first-order and requires only minibatch sorting and a modified policy loss. Experiments conducted on MuJoCo and Atari benchmarks demonstrate that this simple modification achieves strong performance relative to established baselines.
#15 Enforcing Symmetries on Pre-trained ML Models for Physics
著者: Wenjie Zhou, Valentino F. Foit, David W. Hogg, Soledad Villar
公開日: Mon, 09 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08992
要約:
Post-hoc group averaging (or test-time augmentation) is a simple way to impose symmetry on a trained predictor without changing its architecture or retraining it. For finite and compact groups, averaging over a normalized Haar measure on the group gives the Reynolds operator: a canonical projection onto invariant or equivariant predictors with known non-increasing risk guarantees under certain assumptions. This paper provides numerical experiments on physics emulation models that demonstrate the effectiveness of the approach in this setting. We then extend this framework to non-compact group symmetries. For non-compact groups, the Haar measure cannot be normalized, so group averaging cannot be defined in the same way. We replace it with three alternatives: the Weyl unitary trick (which requires complexifying the space and extending the predictor), a data-dependent orthogonal projection (which involves a non-trivial optimization on the space of invariant or equivariant functions), and a frame averaging approach (which involves mapping each point to a set of orbit representatives). We illustrate the constructions theoretically and empirically on the unit-rescaling group and the Lorentz group. Our results show that in the non-compact case, the Weyl trick and frame averaging do not provide a consistent improvement over the baseline, except where the test data is out-of-distribution (an orders-of-magnitude improvement in this case) and, for the Weyl trick, in low-data regimes. The orthogonal projection can provide improvements in-distribution and out-of-distribution. We also observe that models trained directly on invariant features matched or beat the projection of an unconstrained one. We conclude that post-hoc symmetrization extends to non-compact groups, but it does not consistently outperform training a symmetric model.
