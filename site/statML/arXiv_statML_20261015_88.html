#1 Bayesian Causal Forests for Zero-Inflated Continuous Outcomes
著者: Hugo Gobato Souto, Eric Wang, Igor Shuryak
公開日: Thu, 15 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18889
要約:
Estimating (heterogeneous) treatment effects is still challenging when the outcome is semicontinuous (i.e., exhibiting both a discrete point mass at zero and a skewed continuous distribution for positive values), despite the strong presence of this data type in real world settings. Existing methods either ignore the zero mass, impose fragile parametric assumptions, or suffer from extreme computational costs and data augmentation noise. This paper proposes Zero-Inflated Continuous Bayesian Causal Forests with Duan's Smearing (ZIC-BCF-Smear), a decoupled two-part nonparametric estimator that fits a Probit BCF on the full sample to model participation and a Gaussian BCF strictly on the active subpopulation to model positive intensity. Subpopulation Propensity Adjustment (SPA) mitigates regularization-induced confounding within the active subpopulation, and Duan's non-parametric smearing estimator re-transforms predictions to the response scale, bypassing the fragile scale multipliers of parametric models. A comprehensive simulation benchmark shows that ZIC-BCF-Smear matches well-specified parametric models without their assumptions and remains stable and calibrated where competitors either diverge or pathologically over-cover for nonlinear data. The method is then applied to individual-participant data from the SPECTRUM randomized phase III head-and-neck cancer trial, estimating the causal effect of assignment to panitumumab plus chemotherapy on the number of days patients spend with severe toxicity, an outcome for which a quarter of patients contribute an exact zero. Panitumumab assignment raises that burden by about ten days per patient under the trial's own differential treatment duration, and the decoupled structure localizes the effect mainly in the positive-intensity part rather than in whether the reconstructed severe-AE burden is positive.
#2 Delay Complexity Dimension: Learning with Delayed and Censored Labels
著者: Madhava Gaikwad
公開日: Thu, 15 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18935
要約:
We study PAC learning of binary classifiers when labels arrive after random delays and are censored beyond an observation window $W$. How many samples does this observation model require? We propose a distribution-dependent answer, the Delay Complexity Dimension (DCD). The DCD combines the VC dimension with the exact label-information rate of the observation channel. Every learner needs $\Omega(\mathrm{DCD}/\varepsilon^2)$ samples. An oracle pseudo-label ERM matches this rate on the instance family of the lower bound, up to constants and logarithmic factors. On that family the DCD is within a constant factor of the classical censored-ERM complexity, and whether the DCD rate is achievable where the two separate remains open. The theory also yields a quantization scheme under which delay information lowers the residual label variance, an expedition policy with a $(1-1/e)$ guarantee for its version-space objective, and a phenomenon with no analogue in the no-delay setting. Under a fixed collection horizon, a moderate window can strictly beat immediate labels. Experiments on synthetic data and the Criteo conversion logs support these predictions. A budget-matched test of a companion labeler-diversity prediction is reported as a negative result.
#3 Wong--Zakai Convergence and FNO Learning for Stochastic Volterra Equations
著者: Wonjae Lee, Jiuk Jang, Hyungbin Park
公開日: Thu, 15 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18939
要約:
This paper studies Wong--Zakai convergence and Fourier neural operator learning for stochastic Volterra integral equations (SVIEs) describing random systems with memory. A Wong--Zakai theorem establishes convergence of pathwise solutions driven by piecewise linear approximations of Brownian motion to the corresponding SVIE solution. This convergence provides a foundation for using mirror-padded Fourier neural operators to learn the solution operator mapping initial conditions and driving noise to entire solution trajectories. Numerical experiments demonstrate competitive prediction accuracy and generalization to temporal grids finer than those used for training, while assessing the influence of padding and computational cost. As a follow-up to \citet{LKP:26}, this study extends their operator-learning framework to SVIEs through separate convergence and approximation analyses tailored to convolutional memory.
#4 Semi-Amortized Bayesian Inference with Reusable Local Posteriors
著者: Niek Stevenson, Paul-Christian B\"urkner, Andrew Heathcote, Stefan T. Radev
公開日: Thu, 15 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18945
要約:
Amortized Bayesian inference uses simulations to perform Bayesian estimation when likelihoods are expensive or analytically unavailable. However, learning the full joint posterior is challenging in high-dimensional models with complex dependencies. We introduce a semi-amortized method that divides parameters into smaller blocks, and uses conditional independence to identify the information needed to infer each block. We train neural estimators for blocks whose posterior updates are computationally expensive or frequently repeated. Each estimator learns from simulations of the relevant model components to approximate the block's full conditional posterior, given the relevant observations and the values of the variables on which it depends. A Markov chain then alternates direct draws from these estimators with conventional updates of the remaining variables. Each neural update uses the current conditioning values and requires neither likelihood evaluation nor an acceptance-rejection step. Where the same conditional structure recurs, one estimator can be reused across blocks and datasets, with an output dimension determined by the block rather than the full model. We illustrate the approach in high-dimensional and computationally expensive hierarchical and spatiotemporal models.
#5 Discrete Diffusion Models are Minimax Optimal Under Sparsity

公開日: Thu, 15 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19020
要約:
Discrete diffusion models have emerged as a leading alternative to autoregressive models for generating text and other categorical data, yet their theoretical foundations remain less developed than those of their continuous counterparts. For continuous diffusion models, a prominent line of theory suggests that these models can adapt to low-dimensional structure in the data, thereby improving statistical efficiency and generalization. In this work, we investigate the discrete analogue of this phenomenon by studying the statistical rates of discrete diffusion models under sparsity assumptions. By analyzing a nonparametric estimator derived from a regularized score-entropy objective, we establish minimax-optimal rates in the sparse regime. The proposed regularization can be viewed as an adaptation of label smoothing techniques introduced for classification, and does not require the knowledge of the sparsity parameter. This shows that discrete diffusion models can adapt intrinsically to sparsity.
#6 Conformalized Physics-Informed Weak Regression for Parameter Estimation in Epidemic Models
著者: Allan Peter Engsig-Karup
公開日: Thu, 15 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19058
要約:
Compartment models of infectious diseases are often linear in coefficients that are physical parameters or products or ratios of them. We exploit this with closed-form least squares and equip the estimates with distribution-free uncertainty. Physics-informed regression (PIR) fits the parameters to finite-difference derivatives of the data; we introduce physics-informed weak regression (PIWR), which integrates the model in time instead, needs no derivatives of the data, estimates the initial state jointly and is high-order accurate. To quantify uncertainty we wrap both estimators in split conformal prediction, with a calibration population generated by the physics-based model itself, giving simultaneous intervals for all parameters with a finite-sample coverage guarantee. A residual-adaptive factor, computed without ground truth, scales the intervals to each data set (PIWR-RACC). On the eleven benchmark models of disease-informed neural networks (DINNs), PIWR or PIR-FD6 is one to four orders of magnitude more accurate than the reported DINN results wherever the sampling resolves the dynamics, in about a millisecond per fit instead of minutes to hours of training, and the 90% intervals achieve 87-92% coverage in all 88 settings. On the S3I3R model of the original PIR study, PIWR lowers the reported PIR errors by about three orders of magnitude. On real data from the 1978 boarding-school influenza outbreak, a variable-projection variant gives calibrated 95% intervals that overlap those of a Bayesian analysis. On a time-varying transmission benchmark, a B-spline expansion of beta(t) keeps PIR and PIWR linear; they are more accurate than physics-informed neural networks in three of four cases and comparable in the noisiest, with simultaneous bands for beta(t). The complete conformal calibration takes less than two minutes for every benchmark.

Learning an individualized treatment rule (ITR) with many treatments requires estimating treatment-specific effects from limited observations per arm. Treatments with similar conditional responses can share information, but linear contrast models may miss nonlinear heterogeneity. We propose nonlinear supervised clustering fusion (NSCF), a neural Q-learning framework with a shared nonlinear covariate representation and low-dimensional treatment embeddings. Pairwise fusion groups treatments with similar learned contrasts, yielding an ITR and an interpretable partition. #9 Structure-aware Bayesian full waveform inversion via diffusion-regularized Stein variational gradient descent
diffusion
著者: Chao Li, Mrinal K. Sen, Sergey Fomel, Yangkang Chen
公開日: Thu, 15 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19187
要約:
Full waveform inversion (FWI) is a powerful tool for high-resolution subsurface model reconstruction, but it remains highly nonlinear and ill-posed, especially when the initial model is inaccurate or the acquired data are noisy and incomplete. Annealed Stein variational gradient descent (ASVGD) provides a particle-based Bayesian framework for FWI by approximating the posterior distribution with an ensemble of velocity models, thereby enabling uncertainty quantification beyond a single deterministic solution. However, conventional ASVGD-FWI commonly relies on simple handcrafted priors, such as Gaussian or smoothing assumptions, which are often insufficient to represent complex geological structures. To address this limitation, we introduce a learned diffusion model (DM) as an implicit regularization mechanism within the ASVGD-FWI framework. Instead of explicitly evaluating the diffusion prior density, the trained diffusion model acts as a learned geological projection operator that guides updated particles toward the distribution of plausible velocity models. In this way, the proposed method combines data consistency from FWI, posterior diversity from ASVGD, and structural realism from the diffusion prior. Numerical experiments demonstrate that the diffusion-regularized ASVGD-FWI produces more geologically consistent mean models, suppresses nonphysical artifacts, and yields uncertainty estimates that are more concentrated around structurally complex and poorly constrained regions. These results suggest that learned diffusion priors can effectively enhance Bayesian FWI by improving both inversion stability and the interpretability of posterior uncertainty.

While causal discovery methods have matured for complete data, such algorithms for handling missing values remain limited. #11 Multi-Sensitive Distributional Fairness: Joint Wasserstein Audits and Shapley Allocation
著者: Agathe Fernandes Machado, Charlotte Jamotton, Arthur Charpentier
公開日: Thu, 15 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19252
要約:
Fairness audits with several sensitive attributes are often performed one coordinate at a time. This can miss purely intersectional disparities: a score may be independent of each sensitive coordinate while depending on their joint profile. We study distributional demographic parity for a real-valued score using one-dimensional Wasserstein geometry. We distinguish two complementary constructions. Conditional and sequential audits characterize joint parity through their zero sets, but their positive values do not generally decompose a fixed joint Wasserstein disparity. By contrast, full-cell conditional quantile functions define a Hilbert-valued random variable whose variance is the joint disparity. Conditional expectations of this quantile process induce a monotone information game: every reveal order yields an exact martingale decomposition of the same joint disparity, while Shapley values provide an order-free attribution across sensitive attributes. Under independent sensitive attributes, the game reduces to a Hoeffding-ANOVA decomposition and Shapley splits each interaction energy equally among its members; under dependence, orthogonality is lost and explicit formulas reveal suppression and redundancy effects that marginal audits can hide. We give fixed-grid estimators and permutation calibration, and illustrate the framework on synthetic examples and ACSIncome. The resulting framework separates three questions that are often conflated: whether joint distributional parity holds, how a fixed joint disparity decomposes along an information path, and how that disparity can be attributed across sensitive coordinates without privileging an ordering.
#12 State-Matched Sequence Conformal Prediction
著者: Ruihang Han, Li-Hsiang Lin

リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19484
要約:
Sequential conformal prediction needs to decide which past errors should calibrate uncertainty for the current target. Recency and online error feedback are useful when recent observations remain relevant, but the recent past can be a poor reference when comparable states recur at nonadjacent times, with the recurrence pattern generally unknown. We propose State-Matched Sequence Conformal Prediction (SMSCP), which constructs an explicit one-sided local-linear state from pre-target information, uses state similarity to select a matched reference set of past calibration scores, and then applies the ordinary unweighted conformal order statistic. In contrast to existing approaches, SMSCP uses state similarity to identify calibration observations relevant to the target score quantile. #13 High-Probability Regret Bounds for Feel-Good Thompson Sampling
著者: Xuheng Li, Qingyue Zhao, Quanquan Gu
公開日: Thu, 15 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19513
要約:
Feel-good Thompson sampling (FGTS) augments the posterior of vanilla Thompson sampling with an exploration term that favors reward functions with high predicted optimal values. This modification achieves minimax-optimal regret up to logarithmic factors in linear contextual bandits, but existing analyses of its two standard variants establish regret guarantees in expectation. In this paper, we derive the first high-probability regret bounds for both cumulative and current-step FGTS in contextual bandits with general realizable function approximation. Our bounds depend on the corresponding decoupling coefficients and the prior mass near the true reward function. In the $d$-dimensional linear setting, both variants satisfy $\mathrm{Regret}(T)=O\big(d\sqrt T\log(2T)+\sqrt T\log(1/\delta)\big)$ with probability at least $1-\delta$, where $T$ is the time horizon. This bound matches the dimension and horizon dependence of algorithms based on the upper confidence bounds, up to logarithmic factors. Relative to the expected-regret guarantees, the cost of controlling the regret with high probability is confined to additive terms with at most linear dependence on $\log(1/\delta)$. The proof ideas are partially developed by GPT-6 Astra under the authors' guidance.

#15 On Inherent Privacy of Posterior Sampling: A Unified R\'enyi-Divergence Framework

著者: Ruyu Zhou, Fang Liu
公開日: Thu, 15 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19573
要約:
Differential Privacy (DP) provides a rigorous framework for quantifying privacy guarantees. While most methods achieve DP by injecting calibrated random noise, the intrinsic randomness of certain procedures such as sampling can yield DP guarantees ``for free''. In this work, we develop a unified R\'enyi divergence framework to characterize the inherent DP guarantees of sampling from a Bayesian posterior distribution. We show that the privacy loss incurred by One-Posterior-Sampling (OPS) is governed by posterior exponential moments of the single-record log-likelihood ratio, and establish explicit DP guarantees under uniformly bounded, sub-Gaussian, or sub-exponential tail regimes. We apply the framework to representative Bayesian models, including categorical likelihoods with arbitrary priors, Gaussian-Gaussian conjugate pair, generalized linear models from the exponential family and linear regression with Gaussian likelihood, both with Gaussian priors on the regression coefficients. Our results elucidate sample size, model structure, neighboring relations in DP (bounded or unbounded), and how prior if applicable, jointly determine the inherent DP guarantees of OPS. Furthermore, our results recover the existing DP guarantees for OPS as special cases -- often with tighter privacy loss bounds -- and substantially broaden the class of Bayesian models for which the inherent DP guarantees of OPS can be rigorously characterized.

Automated materials discovery requires evaluating large pools of candidate configurations, with density functional theory (DFT) as a major throughput bottleneck. We frame this decision as a risk-controlled selection problem and solve it within a conformal risk control (CRC) framework. Each configuration is mapped from atomic numbers and Cartesian coordinates to a fixed geometric descriptor, which a lightweight predictor uses to estimate an MLIP-DFT disagreement score in energy, average force, and maximum force. We consider simulation-based Bayesian inference (SBI) for the parameters of models with intractable likelihoods but tractable forward simulation. Building on Gaussian mixtures-of-experts surrogates, as a first contribution we develop a sequential procedure to estimate the posterior, incorporating an importance sampling step that introduces a locally amortized likelihood-to-evidence ratio estimator. The local amortization allows the ratio estimator to be evaluated at new parameter--data pairs from a local (data-dependent) design, without re-estimation, which is particularly convenient for our second contribution. The second contribution is localized simulation-based calibration (SBC). Localized SBC provides a graphical diagnostic of calibration over neighborhoods that are deliberately broader than the available posterior, at low additional computational cost. Rather than repeatedly running the full inference procedure for each simulated pseudo-observation, localized SBC fits the local surrogate and ratio estimator only once for each local design, and reuses them across all pseudo-observations. This approach is particularly appealing for SBI applications. We evaluate both contributions on three case studies, including a real-data epidemiological application.

著者: Hunter Ng, Yubo Tao

リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07589
要約:
Joint-event forecasts combine an estimated dependence model with current marginal forecasts. Retaining historical transforms at the marginal fits available when each outcome arrived creates overlapping estimation errors. We derive their joint influence with the terminal estimates in a stable Vine Copula VAR with normal innovation margins and a fixed, correctly specified Gaussian or positive Clayton vine. An intercept identity and the stable VAR filter reduce the historical correction to harmonically weighted innovation moments. Terminal slope uncertainty remains in the forecast gradient. A feasible covariance estimator yields asymptotically valid repeated-sample intervals for fixed one-sided event probabilities at the realized forecast state. A same-family terminal refit provides the efficiency comparison. Retaining dated transforms adds a positive semidefinite limiting covariance term, whose event-level cost depends on dependence sensitivity and forecast-state leverage. Monte Carlo comparisons show a small accuracy cost of retaining the transforms and a larger inferential role for current marginal estimation. Allowing Clayton dependence improves downside forecasts relative to Gaussian dependence under each transform rule. A fourteen-variable U.S. real-time forecasting application and simulations with twenty and fifty variables illustrate feasible joint-event estimation and analytic inference for fixed-depth vines.
