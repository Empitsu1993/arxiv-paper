#1 Generalized Hyperrectangular Prediction Sets in Multivariate Conformal Prediction
著者: Nabil Alami, Souhaib Ben Taieb
公開日: Thu, 05 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05543
要約:
In multivariate conformal prediction, many nonconformity scores yield prediction sets with complex geometry (e.g., ellipsoids or density level sets) that are difficult to interpret and visualize. Hyperrectangles offer a practical alternative by providing one interval per output. We extend this notion to Cartesian products of arbitrary one-dimensional sets, which we call generalized hyperrectangles. We introduce a rectangularization framework that turns any continuous multivariate nonconformity score, or conditional density, into one yielding generalized hyperrectangular prediction sets with finite-sample coverage. We establish key geometric properties of our rectangularization framework and provide useful intuitions on generalized hyperrectangles. We further establish a representation theorem showing that a broad class of hyperrectangular conformal methods based on max-aggregated coordinate-wise scores can be interpreted as rectangularizations of implicit conditional densities. Through synthetic and real-world experiments, we quantify the efficiency cost of rectangularization, while highlighting its benefits, especially for practical applications of uncertainty quantification.

In this paper, three operations on Gibbs probability measures are studied: renormalization, normalized log-linear combination, and nesting. The resulting measures are shown to be Gibbs probability measures with explicit expressions for their objective functions, reference measures, and regularization factors. These measures are further characterized as unique solutions to optimization problems involving linear combinations of the original objective functions, subject to either relative entropy regularization or relative entropy constraints. #3 Near-Minimax Sample Complexity for Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning under Generative Models
著者: Yue Wang
公開日: Thu, 05 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05562
要約:
In multi-objective reinforcement learning, preferences may be specified only after data collection, requiring one dataset to support a continuum of reward trade-offs. We study finite discounted Markov decision processes with unknown transitions, $S$ states, $A$ actions, and $m$ known reward functions taking values in $[0,1]$, given access to a generative model. The learner must provide an $\varepsilon$-optimal policy for every convex combination of these rewards without further sampling, with a guarantee that holds simultaneously over all preferences and initial states. Writing $H=(1-\gamma)^{-1}$, we establish the constant-confidence minimax sample complexity $\widetilde\Theta\!\left( \frac{SAH^3}{\varepsilon^2}\min\{S,\log(em)\} \right)$, where the suppressed factors are logarithmic in $S,A,H,\varepsilon^{-1}$ and the dependence on $m$ is explicit. Thus, up to these logarithmic factors, the worst-case statistical cost of supporting all preferences grows logarithmically with the number of objectives and saturates at the number of states. The upper bound uses uniform state--action sampling and empirical planning, with reward rounding in the logarithmic-objective regime. Its analysis chains optimal-value increments over covers of the reward hull. A one-sided Bellman variance bound controls signed value differences under a second transition kernel, preserving the $H^3\varepsilon^{-2}$ dependence. A complementary uniform guarantee for all bounded rewards yields the state-dimension saturation. We establish lower bounds matching up to logarithmic factors through a rare-exit construction with subset rewards and action encoding. These bounds apply to adaptive simulator queries and arbitrary randomized history-dependent output policies, while the upper bounds are attained by deterministic stationary policies.
#4 Generative Modeling for Cascading Extremes via Autoregressive Transformers
著者: Miguel de Carvalho, Myung Won Lee, Jordan Richards, Sotirios Sabanis
公開日: Thu, 05 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05728
要約:
Extreme events often occur sequentially, from earthquake-tsunami cascades to successive extreme losses in financial markets. We introduce an extremal token representation that encodes multivariate observations as a categorical sequence. A quiet token records the absence of threshold exceedances; otherwise, the token identifies the exceeding component with the largest marginal probability-scale score. The representation is invariant to componentwise strictly increasing transformations. It preserves the identity and temporal ordering of dominant exceedances but discards their magnitudes and the identities of nondominant simultaneous exceedances. A decoder-only transformer estimates the history-dependent next-token kernel on this representation, and recursive sampling generates future token sequences from the induced joint predictive law. We amortize our generative model by pretraining across simulated process families with varying parameters and dependence persistence. Next-token predictions are evaluated against exact conditional probabilities for held-out categorical processes and particle filter references for heavy-tailed vector autoregressive processes. Applied to ten-second returns for five semiconductor stocks, the pretrained model supports zero-shot transfer, while a few epochs of adaptation improve agreement between observed and generated token frequencies and transition patterns. These results support task-specific tokenization and simulation-based pretraining for modeling which dimensions dominate multivariate extremes.

We study non-parametric bandits in which arms are evaluated using Cumulative Prospect Theory (CPT) rather than expected reward. We consider continuous action spaces with arm-dependent noise and propose Non-Parametric CPT Bandits ($\mathtt{NPCB}$), an optimistic algorithm that decouples mean estimation from CPT-based decision-making. We prove CPT regret of order $\widetilde{\mathcal O}\bigl(\sqrt{T\,\dim_E(\mathcal F,T^{-2})\,\log N(\mathcal F,T^{-2},\|\cdot\|_\infty)}\bigr)$, where $\dim_E$ is the Eluder dimension of the function class $\mathcal F$, $T$ is the time horizon, and $N$ is its covering number in the sup norm. We also study Kernel-CPT bandits and obtain information-gain-based regret bounds for a broad range of kernels, matching the corresponding risk-neutral kernel rates up to logarithmic factors.

#8 Tight Lower Bounds under Matched High-Order H\"older Smoothness and Uniform Convexity
著者: Cedar Site Bai
公開日: Thu, 05 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05527
要約:
In this paper, we provide tight lower bounds for minimizing high-order H\"older-smooth and uniformly convex functions when their orders match. Specifically, we consider functions whose $p^{th}$-order derivatives are H\"older continuous with degree $\nu$ and parameter $H$, and that are uniformly convex with degree $q$ and parameter $\sigma$. When these orders match, namely, $q = p + \nu$, we show that any deterministic algorithm with access to derivatives up to order $p$ requires $\Omega\left(\left(\frac{H}{\sigma}\right)^{\frac{2}{3(p+\nu)-2}}\log\frac{\Delta_0}{\epsilon}\right)$ oracle calls to reduce an initial optimality gap of $\Delta_0$ to $\epsilon$. This matches the corresponding upper bound and establishes a higher-order uniformly convex counterpart to Nesterov's classical first-order lower bound for strongly convex functions. Rather than directly generalizing Nesterov's classical quadratic-chain construction, we develop a multiscale phase-wise construction that preserves previous oracle responses while maintaining global H\"older smoothness and uniform convexity. Our result resolves the matched case left open by existing lower bounds for the two asymmetric regimes.

要約:
Variational approaches to learning transport maps for multimodal sampling can suffer from mode collapse. One way to mitigate this problem is to learn transport along a prescribed path of intermediate distributions defined by geometric annealing. We show that annealing can take a substantial detour in Wasserstein space: when mass moves between modes, the path can be much longer than the distance between its endpoints. We further link path geometry to transport learning, showing that long annealing paths can slow population optimization, while sufficiently regular paths permit rapid convergence. Experiments on multimodal synthetic distributions and Bayesian inference problems demonstrate improvements in transport learning and sampling efficiency.
