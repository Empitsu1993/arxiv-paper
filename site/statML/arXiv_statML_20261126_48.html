#1 The Symmetry Budget: How Much Group Structure Should a Network Architecture Assume?
著者: Noor Islam S. Mohammad, Mahmudul Hasan, Md. Basim Al Zabir Shammo, Hasan Siddiki, Jakaria Habib
公開日: Thu, 26 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33169
要約:
A network architecture is, in large part, a symmetry assumption: convolution ties weights across translations, attention ties them across permutations, and a fully connected layer ties nothing. When the data are only approximately symmetric, tying trades approximation error against estimation error, and the right amount of tying is a property of the dataset, not of the architecture alone. We study this trade-off in an exactly solvable model: linear maps on signals over the cyclic group $\mathbb{Z}_d$, with architectures indexed by the subgroup lattice of $\mathbb{Z}_d$, so that a single integer $k \mid d$ interpolates between an unconstrained layer ($k=1$, $d^2$ parameters) and a fully equivariant one ($k=d$, $d$ parameters). We derive the finite-sample excess risk in closed form, show that for a generic symmetry defect the leading-order risk is affine in $1/k$, and prove that intermediate architectures are then never strictly optimal: the lattice collapses to an all-or-nothing choice with phase boundary $n^\star = \sigma^2 d(d-1)/\varepsilon^2$. Structured defects break this collapse and restore interior optima, and we give a closed-form defect profile for a graded defect. Measurements agree with the theory on $60/60$ cells of a $(\varepsilon,n)$ phase diagram and $48/48$ cells of a second phase diagram at $d=16$, recover the predicted exponent as $-1.92$ against $-2$, and exhibit the predicted $k^\star: 12 \to 4 \to 1$ ladder under graded defects; a two-layer nonlinear control reproduces the crossover. We also characterize when the affine collapse survives for non-abelian groups.
#2 A Causal Energy Prior for Learning Directed Acyclic Graphs
著者: Marco Scutari, Noriaki Sato, Shuichi Kawano, Rui Yamaguchi, Seiya Imoto
公開日: Thu, 26 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33225
要約:
Bayesian learning of directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) has long relied on non- or weakly informative priors, on the assumption that the information needed for an informative one is rarely available. In genetics and systems biology, annotation databases now make it possible to assemble a prior DAG before learning from data. Existing informative priors are local: they score a DAG arc by arc and cannot encode how interventions propagate along its paths. Thus, we propose the local-global energy prior (LGE), a Gibbs distribution over DAGs whose energy combines a local divergence from the prior DAG, the Structural Hamming Distance, and a causal one, the Structural Intervention Distance. We derive rules to calibrate its decay and the weight of the two divergences from the prior DAG alone. We quantify how much prior mass stays on the true DAG, and how much data overturn the prior, when the prior DAG is partially incorrect. In simulations, LGE improves both the structural accuracy of maximum a posteriori DAGs and the fit of the corresponding Bayesian networks to the true distribution over non- and weakly informative priors, even when half of the arcs of the prior DAG are wrong. We also showcase LGE on a gene regulatory network learned from single-cell kidney data.
#3 Evaluating Ensemble Methods for Algorithmic Stability Under Data Perturbations
著者: Benjamin Edelhertz, Simon D. Nguyen, Tyler H. McCormick
公開日: Thu, 26 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33375
要約:
Standard classification algorithms often exhibit sensitivity near decision boundaries, which can compound instability that already exists from overfitting to a subset of high-influence samples. Ensembling is commonly used to stabilize classifiers, but we show that it can destabilize those that are already stable. The well-established practice of subbagging can yield substantial improvements in stability, as we validate in the cause-of-death classification setting. However, its effect depends on the perturbation level, and for stable classifiers the finite number of bags introduces destabilizing Monte Carlo noise that shrinks only slowly as the number of bags grows. This instability can be mitigated, though, by the recently proposed inflated argmax, which allows for multi-class predictions in ambiguous cases. We further examine the extent to which the Rashomon set, the collection of distinct models that achieve near-optimal predictive performance, can reduce instability by leveraging predictive multiplicity rather than resampling. We find that the Rashomon ensemble can stabilize unstable classifiers, but requires sufficient predictive multiplicity, which is not guaranteed. It is a promising direction, but not currently a general substitute for subbagging. Our results are consistent with an influence equalization interpretation of why these ensemble approaches stabilize unstable classifiers, although their failures on stable classifiers appear to have distinct sources.
#4 Gaussian priors from nonlinear PDEs with the semiclassical approximation
著者: Alex Alberts
公開日: Thu, 26 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33400
要約:
Probabilistic numerical methods for partial differential equations (PDE) combines the governing physics with observations to treat solving the PDE as a Bayesian inverse problem. This allows techniques to quantify the uncertainty in the field reconstruction under model-form error. In the majority of cases, a Gaussian prior is assigned to the field of interest, and Bayes's theorem in the infinite-dimensional setting is used to derive the posterior. While Gaussians have been shown to be well-suited for linear PDEs, a similar treatment for nonlinear PDEs remains open-ended. In this work, study the use of the semiclassical approximation to construct Gaussian priors from variational energies or integrated square residuals for probabilistic numerics. The prior is derived by expanding the PDE energy about a stable solution and uncovering a Gaussian form. We then analyze the different characteristics of the resulting priors as they pertain to the inverse problem. In particular, we find that the covariance operator is given by the inverse of the Hessian of the energy whose associated Cameron-Martin space is the solution space of the PDE. We also find that the prior responds to model-form error by sharpening or flattening itself depending on the distance between the ground truth and the solution of the assumed model.
#5 Centered Kernel Distance: Dimension-Free Error Bounds for Comparing Neural Representations
著者: Shujun Xiong, Sarah E. Harvey, Jacob Zavatone-Veth, Alexander H. Williams
公開日: Thu, 26 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33447
要約:
The asymptotic error of representational similarity metrics such as centered kernel alignment (CKA) and representational similarity analysis (RSA) has been well-characterized, but there are no realistic bounds on how estimates of representational similarity $\textit{simultaneously}$ depend on the number of sampled neurons ($N$) and experimental conditions ($M$). We study the natural metric counterpart of CKA and RSA, which we call the Centered Kernel Distance (CKD). We show that CKD admits an unbiased estimator, whereas several popular similarity measures including CKA have no unbiased estimator. Under mild assumptions, we show that estimation error of CKD decays in proportion to $1/M + 1/N$. Strikingly, the rate in this bound does not depend on the dimensionality of the space of experimental conditions. Thus, similarity estimation does not suffer from the curse of dimensionality, which plagues similar problems in high-dimensional statistics. We verify our results empirically in random shallow neural networks, trained neural networks, and macaque visual cortex data. Ultimately, this paper advances a framework for predicting the error of similarity estimators in experiments by relaxing existing, biologically-unrealistic assumptions. Our theoretical and empirical predictions produce specific prescriptions on how to most efficiently estimate the similarity between multiple neural systems.
#6 PAC Learning to Sample: On Computational Aspects of Language Generation
著者: Alkis Kalavasis, Shuchen Li, Manolis Zampetakis
公開日: Thu, 26 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33464
要約:
The success of language models has motivated theoretical work on learning to sample from distributions on discrete domains. A prominent abstraction is the model of language generation in the limit, introduced by Kleinberg and Mullainathan (NeurIPS 2024), in which the algorithm must generate from the target language after some finite but unspecified number of positive examples. While its positive results are surprisingly general, most guarantees are qualitative. In this work, we seek quantitative guarantees on sample and time complexity, inspired by statistical and computational learning theory. To this end, we study a PAC model of language generation, proposed (in a different context) by De, Diakonikolas, and Servedio (SODA 2015), in which a Boolean concept class $C$ over $\{-1,1\}^n$ defines the target languages: given uniformly random positive examples of an unknown concept $f \in C$, the learner must output a sampler whose output distribution (conditioned on the training data) is close in total variation to the uniform distribution over $f^{-1}(1)$. Their work implicitly gives an efficient algorithm for PAC learning to sample $C$, given three primitives for $C$: an online learner, approximate sampling and counting oracles, and an SQ learner. We ask whether these ingredients, in particular online learnability, are necessary. First, we construct a Boolean concept class $C$ for which online learning is cryptographically hard, yet PAC learning to sample is computationally efficient. Then, we develop new algorithms that use offline ERM oracles instead of online learners, together with approximate sampling/counting oracles for intersections of concepts in $C$ and an SQ learner for $C$. We view our results, together with the work of De, Diakonikolas, and Servedio, as a first step towards understanding the computational complexity of language generation.
#7 On the Impact of Extra Information in Bandit Learning: More is Not Always Merrier
著者: Ramakrishnan Krishnamurthy
公開日: Thu, 26 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33479
要約:
More data is commonly assumed to improve learning performance, however, we show that this intuition can surprisngly fail in sequential decision-making. Considering a stochastic bandits setup with exogenous extra information, we ask if the free and unbiased extra reward samples are guaranteed to reduce the regret of the learner. We introduce a formal notion of `non-degradation under extra information' and demonstrate that the widely used Upper Confidence Bound algorithm (UCB) violates this property, i.e., there exist benign extra-information under which UCB incurs strictly higher regret than when run without extra information. We contrast this behaviour with Thompson Sampling and Explore-Then-Commit, which appear to benefit from the same extra information. Constructively, we propose a meta-algorithm that provably enforces non-degradation for a broad class of bandit algorithms. We also run empirical simulations to study our meta-algorithm.
#8 Adversarial Bandit Convex Optimization with $d^{3/2}\sqrt{n}$ Regret
著者: Alireza Bakhtiari, Kevin Jamieson, Tor Lattimore, Lillian Ratliff
公開日: Thu, 26 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33531
要約:
We study adversarial bandit convex optimization, where in each of $n$ rounds a learner chooses a point $A_t$ in a convex body $\mathcal{K}\subset\mathbb{R}^d$ and observes only a noisy value of a convex loss $f_t:\mathcal{K}\to[0,1]$ at that point. The losses are chosen by an adversary, who may change them from round to round and adapt them to the learner's past actions. We improve the best known regret bound for this problem from $\widetilde O(d^{5/2}\sqrt n)$ (Lattimore, 2020, 2026) to $\widetilde O(d^{3/2}\sqrt n)$, which matches the rate conjectured by Bubeck et al. (2017). Together with the recent lower bound of $\Omega(d^{4/3}\sqrt n)$ due to Rajaraman and Han (2026), it determines the minimax regret up to a factor of $d^{1/6}$ and logarithmic factors. The algorithm is an online Newton step with Gaussian exploration. Unlike earlier methods of this kind, it uses unbiased estimates of the gradient and Hessian of the Gaussian smoothing of the loss, rather than of an optimistic surrogate.
#9 Improved Sample Complexity of Robust Reinforcement Learning with KL Uncertainty Sets
著者: Yudan Wang, Zilong Deng, Nathaniel D. Bastian, Shaofeng Zou
公開日: Thu, 26 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33592
要約:
In this paper, we study distributionally robust reinforcement learning with Kullback--Leibler (KL) divergence-defined uncertainty sets. The goal is to find a policy that maximizes the robust value function, defined as the worst-case value over all transition kernels in the uncertainty set. Assuming access to a generative model, we aim to understand the sample complexity of finding an $\epsilon$-optimal robust policy. The best-known sample complexity results in the literature show a non-trivial gap of $\mathcal{O}\{\max\{p_\wedge^{-1}(1-\gamma)^{-1},(1-\gamma)^{-2}\}\}$ between the upper and lower bounds, where $p_\wedge$ denotes the minimum positive transition probability of the nominal transition kernel, and $\gamma$ is the discount factor. More importantly, existing results on the lower bound only cover a limited range of the uncertainty level. In this paper, we develop tighter and complete upper and lower bounds for robust RL with KL-defined uncertainty sets. Our upper bound is the tightest among all existing studies, and it improves upon the best-known bound by at least the order of $\mathcal{O}(\min\{p_\wedge^{-1},(1-\gamma)^{-1}\})$ for uncertainty level $\delta\in(0,1)$. Furthermore, our lower bound holds for any uncertainty level. Our upper and lower bounds (nearly) match in various regimes, providing improved results for this problem.
#10 Understanding Double Descent through Universal Compression
著者: Jiaxuan Cheng, Addison Spiegel
公開日: Thu, 26 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33759
要約:
Many deep learning models exhibit a double-descent phenomenon, where test loss exhibits a sharp peak near the interpolation threshold and then descends a second time as model size grows further, contrary to the U-shape predicted by the classical bias--variance tradeoff. The double-descent phenomenon has been found and explained in toy classical models, but is not well-understood in modern deep learning despite extensive empirical study. We interpret double descent through the novel lens of universal compression. Specifically, we upper bound the maximum-likelihood excess test loss as a sum of four terms: an approximation-and-shift term, a minimax batch regret, a prior cost, and an MLE-vs-Bayes gap. Under exact posterior sampling, we further show that the expected MLE-vs-ensemble gap lower-bounds the MLE-vs-Bayes gap in the well-specified setting. In our experiments, a Gaussian-prior SGLD approximation to the Bayes predictor, unlike SGD, shows no pronounced interpolation peak. Experiments with uniform random labels support this gap as a major contributor to the double-descent peak; here, the SGD cross-entropy is roughly four times the SGLD ensemble cross-entropy at the peak, while the ensemble cross-entropy remains near $\log 10$.
#11 RHOPE: LLM-Augmented Adaptive Experimental Design with Complex Contextual Information
著者: Bowen Shi, Bo Li
公開日: Thu, 26 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33767
要約:
Experimental design allocates limited measurement effort across candidate treatments to learn their expected outcomes. Adaptive designs refine allocations as evidence accumulates, improving estimation and treatment selection. Contextual information can inform these decisions, but exploiting unstructured or high-dimensional context remains challenging. We use large language models (LLMs) to transform complex context into proxy outcomes and propose Residual Horizon Optimization for Prediction-Powered Experimentation (RHOPE), a principled Bayesian adaptive experimentation framework that combines true outcomes with LLM-generated proxy outcomes and formulates finite-batch design as a posterior Markov decision process (MDP). Our methodology uses proxy outcomes in two ways: sample augmentation improves estimation precision, while observing proxies before each allocation decision provides advance information. Synthetic experiments and semi-synthetic experiments using Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC) data suggest that estimation precision drives most gains, with smaller advance-information gains, and highlight the importance of proxy informativeness and moment-estimation accuracy.
#12 A Graphical Criterion for Linear Causal Bounds
著者: Alexis Bellot
公開日: Thu, 26 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33950
要約:
The values of causal effects and counterfactuals are typically only partially identified from observational data in the presence of unobserved confounding. With discretely-valued variables, under certain conditions, sharp bounds can be derived by solving one linear program. This is then computationally tractable for many causal questions. In this paper, for every diagram in which each confounded component has at most two hidden causes, we give necessary and sufficient conditions for the sharp bounds to be equal to the optimum of one linear program for every (positive) input probability table. This algorithm is implemented in a new Python package, called "partialid", that allows users to compose assumptions, queries, and data to obtain sharp bounds in a computationally tractable manner.
#13 Accelerated Proximal Gradient Methods for Multiobjective Optimization over Probability Measures via Hilbert-Space Lifting
著者: Dai Hai Nguyen
公開日: Thu, 26 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34073
要約:
Many sampling and inference tasks require a single distribution that balances several targets. We formulate this task as multiobjective optimization of Kullback--Leibler (KL) divergences over probability measures, in the Pareto sense. The particle method MWGraD (Nguyen et al., 2025) uses explicit Wasserstein gradient steps, and its accelerated version A-MWGraD (Nguyen et al., 2026) lacks discrete-time guarantees. We propose the multiobjective Wasserstein proximal gradient (MWPG), which uses implicit Wasserstein gradient steps, and its accelerated version, multiobjective Wasserstein accelerated proximal gradient (MWAPG), with rigorous discrete-time guarantees. Our key idea is a Hilbert-space lifting: each measure is represented as the pushforward of a fixed base measure by a map in a Hilbert space. The problem then becomes a constrained multiobjective composite problem over transport maps, to which accelerated proximal gradient methods apply. For convex and smooth potentials, we show that MWAPG decreases a Pareto merit function at the rate $\mathcal{O}(1/k^2)$; for $m$-strongly convex and $L$-smooth potentials, the rate is $\mathcal{O}\big((1-\sqrt{m/L})^k\big)$. We also give closed-form Gaussian updates and particle implementations based on input-convex neural networks, which require no score estimation. Experiments on synthetic and real-world dataset show that MWAPG needs far fewer iterations than MWPG and matches or outperforms A-MWGraD in Bayesian multi-task learning.
#14 B-SLIM: BART Sparse Linear Interactions Model for Heterogeneous Treatment Effects
著者: Yusufhan Balci, Mark A. van de Wiel, Jeroen Hoogland
公開日: Thu, 26 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33246
要約:
Estimating covariate-dependent treatment effects is a central challenge in personalized medicine. Existing approaches often emphasize either fully parametric models, risking misspecification, or flexible machine learning methods, which may require large samples to detect the small treatment effect heterogeneity common in medical settings. We propose B-SLIM (BART Sparse Linear Interaction Model), a semi-parametric model for causal inference that combines a flexible Bayesian Additive Regression Trees (BART) prognostic component with a sparse linear treatment effect component. This hybrid structure combines flexible prognosis modelling with interpretable treatment effects. The treatment effect component includes main effects and pairwise interactions, regularized through a global-local shrinkage prior. The model allows covariates to contribute to both components and provides direct inference on treatment effect modifiers, conditional mean potential outcomes, and conditional average treatment effects (CATEs). In simulations, B-SLIM outperforms a fully non-parametric alternative when main effects and pairwise interactions adequately represent the treatment effect function, whereas the non-parametric model performs better under strong non-linearity. On the nonlinear Infant Health and Development Program (IHDP) benchmark, the two Bayesian methods are competitive in predictive accuracy and ranking, and both achieve lower mean CATE error than the DR-learner, S-learner, and R-learner implementations considered. In the CANVAS trials, both methods yield similar rankings, while B-SLIM identifies several important drivers of treatment effect heterogeneity. B-SLIM enables interpretable inference on treatment effect parameters and patient-specific prediction of conditional mean potential outcomes, while remaining competitive at sample sizes relevant to medical studies.
#15 Two Timescales of Forgetting in Fine-tuning of Language Models: Fast Behavioral Suppression and Slow Representational Drift
著者: Yilin Luo, Jin Hwa Lee, Andrew M. Saxe, Antonio Sclocchi
公開日: Thu, 26 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33302
要約:
Today, a core pipeline of artificial intelligence systems is pretraining on a broad range of text corpora and then fine-tuning on specific tasks or desired behaviors. Yet we still have limited understanding of how fine-tuning on a restricted data distribution modifies representations acquired during pretraining. In particular, it remains unclear whether surface behavioral suppression happens together with the erasure of latent representations that support it. In this work, we study supervised fine-tuning (SFT) by pretraining transformers on a controlled context-free grammar with hierarchical latent structure, the Random Hierarchy Model (RHM), and fine-tuning with a restricted subset of its generative rules. While during pretraining the model's ability to generate with the correct grammar rules coincides with building the internal representations of the corresponding latent, we find that SFT exhibits a very different dynamics: (i) the generation of deleted rules changes on a fast timescale inversely proportional to the magnitude of the token-latent correlation; meanwhile, their corresponding representations stay intact; (ii) the hidden representations of the deleted rules become forgotten on a much longer timescale, associated with a slow drift of the network parameters. Finally, we observe analogous results in a realistic setup with natural language, suggesting that this timescale separation in forgetting is a more general phenomenon of fine-tuning language models.
#16 Differentially Private Policy Optimization in Linear MDPs with Adversarial Rewards

著者: Yi He

リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33469
要約:
Reinforcement learning (RL) in personalized services often relies on sensitive interaction data. Motivated by the need to protect these data, we study differentially private (DP) policy optimization in episodic linear Markov decision processes (MDPs) with unknown transitions and adaptive adversarial linear rewards under full-information feedback. This setting poses two key challenges: past trajectories are repeatedly reused for policy evaluation, and reward choices may depend on previously released private statistics. The proposed algorithm combines batched proximal policy optimization (PPO) updates with optimistic policy evaluation, using private regression statistics and reward estimates to protect both trajectories and reward functions under joint differential privacy (JDP). Thus, privacy incurs only lower-order additive costs in $K$, even under adaptive adversarial rewards.
#17 Amortizing Exact Orthogonality: Drift-Triggered Restoration for Orthogonal Fine-Tuning
著者: Lanxin Zhao (The University of Sydney), Xiaoyu Li (University of New South Wales), Dai Shi (University of Cambridge), Junbin Gao (The University of Sydney), Andi Han (The University of Sydney)
公開日: Thu, 26 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33589
要約:
Orthogonal finetuning (OFT) adapts a pretrained weight by an orthogonal transform and preserves its singular values and row angles, but existing methods construct an exact or approximate orthogonal transform at every optimization step. We measure how far a transform $A$ is from orthogonal by its orthogonality defect $\|A^\top A-I\|_2$. For OFTv2, which truncates a Cayley-Neumann series, we show that the truncation order does not determine the defect. At its default order, block defects in checkpoints from the reference implementation span five orders of magnitude. We propose Restarted Tangent Orthogonal Finetuning (RTOFT), which uses the first-order update $W(I+S)$ of a weight $W$ with a skew-symmetric $S$ and restores exact orthogonality by a polar decomposition only when the drift $\|S\|_2$ reaches a tolerance $\tau$. The defect of $I+S$ is exactly $\|S\|_2^2$. Therefore, reading the drift after every update and restoring before the next forward pass guarantees a defect of at most $\tau^2$ in every forward pass, independently of the loss, data, model, and optimizer. A final restoration makes the returned transform exactly orthogonal. On GLUE, RTOFT reduces the training time of block-diagonal, subspace, and butterfly parameterizations from $1.10$-$1.58\times$ to $0.97$-$1.13\times$ that of LoRA, and its block-diagonal version exceeds LoRA by $1.98\pm0.39$ points on the six-task average over five seeds. At $\tau=0.10$, the largest drift read during training stays between $0.10$ and $0.12$ across DeBERTaV3, Qwen, and diffusion models, whereas it ranges from $0.17$ to $1.65$ when restoring once per epoch. In all seven settings of our ablation, the tangent update is worse without restoration than with it, and restoring after every step yields no measurable accuracy gain over restoring at the tolerance and costs $4.5$-$27.5\%$ more per step.

We study meta learning for statistical inference, in which neural networks learn inference rules from simulated tasks and apply them to new datasets without parameter updates, providing a form of in-context learning. We draw on statistical decision theory to design training objectives whose population minimizers are Bayes rules and exploit their connections to classical inference procedures to establish frequentist guarantees for the learned rules. The structure of classical inference procedures also motivates the design of our neural network architecture, called Residual Recurrent Deep Sets (RRDS), which repeatedly aggregates information across observations in a permutation-invariant manner and refines its internal state through residual updates. Under suitable regularity and training conditions, we establish asymptotic efficiency in parameter estimation, consistency in model selection, and variable selection guarantees in high-dimensional regression models. Numerical experiments illustrate the finite-sample advantages of the learned inference rules over classical methods.

Existing methods typically estimate class evidence and uncertainty for each view and combine the resulting opinions through aggregation rules.
