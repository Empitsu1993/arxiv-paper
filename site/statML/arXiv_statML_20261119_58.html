#1 A Differential Theory of Quality-Resource Trade-offs for AI Model Selection: The Multiresource Frontier and Its Minimax Price
privacy
著者: Dmitry Ignatov
公開日: Thu, 19 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23324
要約:
Comparing AI models across resource budgets yields quality-resource frontiers relating error to latency, parameter count, training compute, and energy. Offset power laws summarize such frontiers, but their fitted parameters reveal neither the price-independent geometry nor the manner in which trade-offs extend across several resource channels. This paper addresses both gaps by developing a differential theory in which this structure is made explicit. Each frontier admits a price-independent plane-curve representation relating the effective number of active resource channels, $N_{\mathrm{eff}} = s^2/\kappa$ (squared slope over curvature), to the remaining log-error depth, $\Delta S = \ln(\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{\infty})$. This representation separates intrinsic shape from the scale and origin of the resource axis. Complete monotonicity and divisibility yield differential inequalities and frontier bounds. When each of $N$ resources contributes one active channel, the theory gives the parameter-free operating rule $N_{\mathrm{eff}} = 1$ in delivered units and derives minimax resource prices $\lambda^{\star}_i = \alpha_i/(N+1)$ with an exact worst-case regret of $\ln(N+1)$ nats. The results make it possible to interpret measured frontiers and compare resource costs without hand-chosen exchange rates. The theory is evaluated on 117 published frontiers and implemented in the open-source package nn-rank, which derives prices from measured quality and cost, ranks candidates, and reports supported choices and minimax regret: https://github.com/ABrain-One/nn-rank
#2 Bounded-Order Non-Identifiability in Latent Structure Block Models: The Fiber in Closed Form
著者: Sie Hendrata Dharmawan, Peter Chin
公開日: Thu, 19 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23547
要約:
A graphon is determined by all of its homomorphism densities, but motif-based methods use finitely many. We compute exactly what that truncation costs in a natural family: latent structure block models whose positions lie on $K$ rays, equivalently degree-corrected block models of rank two. For a given radial law, the configurations that agree on every graph with fewer than $2K-1$ vertices form a one-dimensional fiber, which we give in closed form with explicit endpoints; the friendship graph $F_{K-1}$ is its unique edge-minimal separator, so no fixed motif order serves every $K$. The separating signal is suppressed by a factor of at most $m_1^{2(K-1)}$, where $m_1$ is the mean squared slope, about $10^{-4}$ at $K=4$: detecting it from motif counts would take about $10^{15}$ nodes in a single graph, at any edge density. The information is nonetheless in the graph; the fiber belongs to the statistic, not the model. Common spectral pipelines, which count no motifs, still slide along the fiber by more than a third of its length at ordinary densities, with confidence regions that almost never contain the truth, while model-based fits of the same embedding do not. We give practitioners four checks: whether a fit is at risk, what to run instead, why a confidence interval will not warn, and how wide the remaining ambiguity is.
#3 A Free Lunch: Gradient Averaging Achieves Optimal Statistical Efficiency in Newton Sketch
著者: Haoxuan Wang, Jianliang Ye, Sen Na
公開日: Thu, 19 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23671
要約:
We study statistical inference for online sketched Newton methods, in which each Newton system is approximately solved by an inner randomized sketching solver. Existing work reveals a computational--statistical trade-off: the sketching solver (and its accelerated variants) reduces the per-step cost of Newton methods from $O(d^3)$ to $O(d^2)$, but the randomness introduced by sketching also inflates the limiting covariance of both the last and the averaged Newton iterates. In this work, we address this trade-off via gradient averaging. We show that simply replacing the stochastic gradient with a moving average at each step can mitigate, and in some cases eliminate, the trade-off in Newton sketch. Importantly, gradient averaging requires no additional oracle evaluations and leaves the contraction bound of the inner sketching solver unchanged. In particular, we prove that the last sketched Newton iterate with gradient averaging attains a limiting covariance no larger than that without gradient averaging, while the averaged iterate even attains the minimax optimal limiting covariance, matching that of the exact Newton method. To our knowledge, this is the first analysis showing that randomness introduced to improve computational efficiency need not compromise statistical efficiency as long as proper gradient estimates are used. The key analytical challenge is that gradient averaging destroys the crucial martingale difference structure underlying central limit theorem arguments. We overcome this challenge by analyzing the coupled two-timescale recursions governing the iterate and the gradient estimator. Numerical experiments on linear and logistic regression support our theoretical covariance comparisons.
#4 Improved Leverage Score Sampling for Constrained Active Linear Regression
著者: Aarshvi Gajjar, Syamantak Kumar, Aditya Makkar, Christopher Musco, Joey Zhou
公開日: Thu, 19 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23801
要約:
In this paper we study active learning for constrained linear regression. Given a design matrix $A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}$, a constraint set $C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^d$, and query access to $b\in\mathbb{R}^n$, we seek an approximate solution to $\min_{x\in C}\|Ax-b\|_2^2$ using as few queries as possible. This problem arises in many scientific and engineering applications, and randomized leverage score sampling provides a powerful method to solve it. However, existing leverage score methods ignore the constraint or encode it through ridge regularization, suffering unfavorable query complexity for anisotropic constraint sets. We propose ellipsoid-regularized leverage scores, where we approximate the constraint set by an outer ellipsoid and use its shape matrix to bias sampling toward directions that matter under the constraint geometry. We prove that the query complexity is governed by an anisotropic effective dimension $d_{\text{eff}}(C)\le d$. With appropriate parameters, $O(d_{\text{eff}}(C)\log d/\varepsilon^2)$ queries suffice to return a point in $C$ with objective value at most $(1+\varepsilon)$ times the optimum. This bound can substantially improve on the standard leverage-score bound, since anisotropic constraints can make $d_{\text{eff}}(C)$ much smaller than $d$. For convex constraints, we improve the dependence on $\varepsilon$ from $1/\varepsilon^2$ to $1/\varepsilon$. We also prove two lower bounds: first, the $d/\varepsilon^2$ dependence is unavoidable without convexity assumptions on $C$, and second, in some regimes, the effective dimension dependence $d_{\mathrm{eff}}(C)/\varepsilon$ is necessary for ellipsoidal constraints. Experiments on synthetic regression problems and a parametric heat conduction problem show lower regression error than standard baselines for similar query budget.

要約:
Stability under input perturbations is necessary for deep neural network estimators in many applications involving noisy covariates or estimated features. Weight norm constraints are widely used to control sensitivity to input perturbations by bounding the Lipschitz constant of neural networks. However, existing approximation results under fixed bounds on standard norm products are insufficient to establish optimal statistical rates. In this paper, we establish a sharp approximation theory for deep ReLU networks under a split spectral norm product constraint. This theoretical result shows that sufficiently smooth H\"{o}lder functions can be uniformly approximated to arbitrary accuracy as the network depth increases, with the norm-product bound held fixed. We then propose a penalized least squares estimator for deep nonparametric regression. Numerical studies show that the penalized estimator has well-controlled sensitivity to input perturbations and prediction accuracy comparable to that of classical deep network methods.

#8 NOTREKS: Marginal Independence in Score-based Structure Learning

リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24015
要約:
Score-based structure learning estimates causal graphs by optimizing a data-dependent objective over structural equation models. We study how prior knowledge that selected pairs of variables are marginally independent can be incorporated into such causal discovery methods. Under faithfulness, marginally independent pairs cannot be connected by a trek, so the knowledge becomes a graphical no-trek constraint, which we call NOTREKS information. We introduce a power-series trek (PSTrek) penalty for gradient-based optimization and a trek constraint for order-based search, and we analyze why such information helps in finite samples and how it restricts the search space. #9 Rare-Tail Statistics For Learning Biased Gaussian Halfspaces with Label Noise
著者: Arya Mazumdar, Prateeti Mukherjee, Harsh Vardhan
公開日: Thu, 19 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24181
要約:
We revisit the classical problem of learning general Gaussian halfspaces in the presence of label noise, with particular focus on the large-bias regime, where information-computation gaps are conjectured in several standard label-noise models. Concretely, we present and analyze the rare-tail mean estimator, an extremely simple one-shot statistic that averages only the observed minority class. In the large-bias regime, classical Averaging (Servedio, 1999) and related global first-moment statistics are provably suboptimal, while the rare-tail mean estimator recovers the target halfspace direction at the information-theoretic sample rate (modulo log factors) under a broad class of label noise models, including probit noise and margin-dependent noise with very slow decay away from the boundary. Notably, our guarantees allow labels arbitrarily close to the decision boundary to be nearly random, while still giving strong directional recovery from a small number of samples. The estimator also cleanly interpolates between the noiseless and noisy regimes: without noise, it attains the same sample rate as classical separability-based methods for realizable halfspaces, and under low noise, or noise that decays away from the boundary, it keeps this rate even after linear separability has been destroyed. As an application, we consider a two-stage curriculum in which source samples, whose noise may be arbitrarily close to $1/2$ near the boundary and decay very slowly away from it, precede target samples, whose noise flips every label at the same rate, i.e., random classification noise (RCN). Since both stages share the same Bayes optimal halfspace, our estimator supplies the directional warm start needed by existing refinement routines, and together they learn the RCN target at the information-theoretic sample rate in the large-bias regime, up to log factors.
#10 Stability-compatible kernels: beyond Gaussianity for impulse response estimation
著者: Gianluigi Pillonetto, Mauro Bisiacco
公開日: Thu, 19 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24272
要約:
Kernel-based regularization for linear system identification uses positive semidefinite kernels both to define reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces (RKHSs) and as covariance models for stochastic impulse responses. Sharp kernel conditions are known for RKHS stability, while almost-sure BIBO stability under Gaussian priors requires an even more restrictive covariance condition. These restrictions, however, need not persist beyond Gaussianity, where the covariance no longer determines the full probability law. We introduce the notion of a stability-compatible kernel, namely a covariance kernel admitting at least one stochastic realization with almost-surely absolutely summable sample paths. We show that stability-compatibility holds if and only if the stable elements are dense in the associated RKHS, a condition strictly weaker than RKHS stability, and derive practical sufficient conditions, including a spectral criterion for stationary kernels. We then construct non-Gaussian priors that preserve a prescribed covariance while enforcing almost-sure stability. In particular, this yields a stable prior based on the stationary radial-basis-function (RBF) kernel, separating covariance structure from the mechanism enforcing stability. Numerical experiments show substantial estimation gains for several important classes of impulse responses, with particularly pronounced advantages when relevant dynamics occur at late lags or in separated temporal regions.
#11 Sparse designs via quantization of the uniform measure on high-dimensional hypercubes
著者: Luc Pronzato, Anatoly Zhigljavsky
公開日: Thu, 19 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24507
要約:
While the notions of sphere packing and sphere covering underlie the most widely used criteria for space-filling design in computer experiments, namely, the maximin and minimax approaches, they suffer from major drawbacks in high dimensions. First, when the dimension $d$ is very large, deploying a space-filling design requires an extremely large number of design points, making the very notion of a space-filling design questionable. Second, these two design characteristics are highly sensitive to boundaries, particularly when the design domain is a hypercube (the case considered in this paper), and especially to its vertices. This sensitivity is particularly acute when the design size is less than $2^d$. Instead of considering the maximin or minimax criterion, we consider here the quantization error of the uniform measure on the hypercube. This error has three key advantages: it accounts for the entire distribution of distances from a random point $U$ to the design, is less sensitive to boundary effects than the maximin and minimax criteria, and, thanks to Zador's celebrated theorem, enables a smooth and natural transition to uniform designs as the design size tends to infinity. Building on recent results on the construction of optimal random quantizers for spherically symmetric distributions (arXiv:2610.11772) we propose a method to generate random designs of a given target size $n$ with small $L_s$-mean quantization error. The support of the resulting distribution is then used to construct designs, possibly incrementally, with sizes up to $n$. We demonstrate that, for moderate $n$, designs supported on a fractional factorial subset of the vertices of a small hypercube perform exceptionally well---and significantly better than designs uniformly distributed over the entire hypercube. We also explain how to account for projections onto lower-dimensional subspaces.

Diffusion models have transformed generative modeling through their ability to capture and generate samples from complex data distributions. This success has spurred substantial interest in using pretrained diffusion models as expressive priors for highly ill-posed inverse problems in imaging and sensing. Despite strong empirical performance, theoretical understanding of this approach remains limited: existing guarantees are often asymptotic or entail prohibitive computational costs in the worst case. In this paper, we address this gap by studying sparse recovery from a small number of linear measurements, a classical problem in compressed sensing. These results lay a theoretical foundation for statistically and computationally efficient inverse solvers with diffusion priors.

We study two-layer neural networks trained by stochastic gradient descent (SGD) on a multi-index teacher model, focusing on how first-layer parameters orthogonal to the teacher subspace affect generalization.
