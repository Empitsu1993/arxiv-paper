#1 Borrowing Strength Across Stores: Transfer Learning for Choice-Based Revenue Management
著者: Recep Yusuf Bekci, Stefanus Jasin, Joline Uichanco
公開日: Thu, 22 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28708
要約:
Large retail chains must tailor assortment and pricing decisions to local customer preferences, yet even retailers with extensive sales records often have limited data for estimating demand at the store or local market level, particularly in product categories with short selling windows. Local estimation (fitting a demand model for each store on its own) can therefore be unreliable, while pooling data across stores improves estimation precision at the cost of ignoring local demand heterogeneity. We study how retailers can balance these competing effects through transfer learning under a multinomial logit (MNL) demand model. We propose a shrinkage estimator that combines store-specific demand estimates with information pooled from other stores. Unlike the linear or quadratic settings underlying much of the existing theoretical literature on information transfer in operations, the nonlinear MNL likelihood does not admit a straightforward bias-variance decomposition. By exploiting its self-concordant-like properties, we establish finite-sample performance guarantees that characterize when information transfer improves demand estimation and how the appropriate degree of shrinkage depends on local sample size, feature dimensionality, and cross-store heterogeneity. We further derive regret bounds for downstream assortment and pricing decisions. Numerical experiments demonstrate the operational benefits of shrinkage across different data environments. An empirical study using data from several hundred stores of a European apparel retailer shows that shrinkage improves out-of-sample product prioritization and revenue capture relative to both local estimation and full pooling. Our findings demonstrate how retailers can leverage information across their store networks to improve revenue management decisions while preserving local demand differences.
#2 A Reverse-Gold Theorem: Positive-Data Identification by Substrate-Bounded Contrastive Learners
著者: Rakesh Sengupta
公開日: Thu, 22 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28747
要約:
Gold's theorem shows that no learner can identify an arbitrary language in a superfinite class from positive presentations alone. We prove a positive result of a different shape. Fix an innate \emph{recursive contrast operator} $R$ mapping any string to a finite set of structurally bounded perturbations of it, and a discriminative scorer of bounded capacity trained by InfoNCE-style contrastive estimation \citep{vandenoord2018,macollins2018} on self-generated perturbations. Under a faithfulness condition relating operator, scorer, and target, the trained scorer achieves \emph{PAC conditional identification} from positive data alone: with high probability it ranks all but an $\varepsilon$-fraction of positives above every out-of-language perturbation in their own orbit, with sample complexity polynomial in the capacity, the orbit size, the score bound, and $1/\varepsilon$. The negatives are constructed by the learner, not supplied by an oracle, and no global normalizing constant over strings is ever computed. The misranking bound follows from the geometry of the contrastive loss---per-example loss floors plus a definite loss increment under misranking---yielding an $\varepsilon^{-2}$ rate, together with an explicit approximation gap that quantifies the cost of in-language (``colliding'') perturbations and vanishes for orbit-pure operators. The ranking guarantee lifts to a global membership classifier under a margin condition, and the same operator composed with the learned scorer is a normalization-free Metropolis--Hastings generator. We instantiate the hypotheses for substitutable context-free languages \citep{clarkeyraud2007} and the subregular classes \citep{heinz2010,hayashikamigaito2025}, and state an open conjecture locating the construction in the mildly context-sensitive hierarchy.
#3 Depth Separation for Neural Networks under Gaussian Measure
著者: Cynthia Bortolotto, Francisco Ganacim, Roberto I. Oliveira
公開日: Thu, 22 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28800
要約:
We revisit the problem of depth separation for neural networks, as studied by Eldan and Shamir, Daniely, and others. Specifically, we consider the natural setting of $L^2$ approximation of radial functions under the standard Gaussian measure in $d$ dimensions. We show that there is a radial function in $L^2$ under the standard Gaussian measure that can be approximated to a fixed sufficiently small $L^2$ error by a depth-3 ReLU neural network of polynomial size in $d$ but can only be approximated by depth-2 networks with subexponentially bounded weights if their size is at least exponential in $d$. For that, we prove a general result showing that if a radial function has substantial mass in the high frequencies of its Hermite expansion, then it cannot be well approximated by a depth-2 neural network with small width and bounded weights. All theorems, lemmas, propositions, and corollaries are verified in Lean~4.
#4 Is In-Context Reinforcement Learning A Posterior Sampler? An Empirical Study
著者: Weiqin Chen, Xinlong Du, Gesualdo Scutari, Harsha Honnappa
公開日: Thu, 22 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28977
要約:
In-context reinforcement learning (ICRL) exploits the impressive in-context learning ability of large language models to zero-shot generalize to new, unseen tasks at inference time without any parameter updates. Among many approaches, the Decision-Pretrained Transformer (DPT) is particularly appealing for its theoretical grounding: under idealized assumptions such as a sufficiently expressive model and infinite pretraining data, DPT can perform Bayesian posterior sampling. Whether this characterization survives in practice remains open. Therefore, we ask: \textit{is ICRL, specifically DPT and our proposed DPT variants, a Bayesian posterior sampler in practice?} To approach this question, we establish two necessary conditions that a Bayesian posterior sampler must satisfy: \textit{sufficient-statistic invariance} and the \textit{martingale property}. We then construct statistical hypothesis tests to statistically assess if practically-trained ICRL models satisfy these conditions. Applying these tests in Gaussian, Bernoulli, and linear bandit settings, we find that both conditions are consistently violated, suggesting that ICRL may not behave as Bayesian posterior samplers in practice. Beyond this negative result, our tests provide a principled toolkit for testing the Bayesian behavior of ICRL.
#5 Internal Guidance as a Sampling Method for Tilted Diffusion Models
diffusion
著者: Curtis McDonald, Peter Bartlett
公開日: Thu, 22 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29086
要約:
Reward guidance methods sample an exponential tilt of a base density using a diffusion model trained with only samples from the base distribution. Existing methods use what we call external guidance: additive terms in the drift or multiplicative terms on the transition probabilities. We propose internal guidance which leaves the output of the base denoiser unmodified and instead changes the point at which it is evaluated. For discrete distributions embedded in the hypercube $\{-1,1\}^{d}$, the denoiser of any linearly tilted density is exactly the base denoiser evaluated at a shifted and rescaled input. In practice the base denoiser is a neural network trained on noised samples of the base distribution. The required evaluation points for sampling the tilted density would be out-of-distribution for basic noising of the base distribution. We modify the training algorithm with random diagonal rescalings of the data, so that the denoiser network learns both in- and out-of-distribution evaluations of the denoiser relative to the base distribution. A single denoiser network samples binary reward tilts, class labels, and infilling with no fine-tuning and no external guidance term.

要約:
Barycenter models, based on stochastic and deterministic optimal transport bridges between probability measures, have proven to be useful for supervised and unsupervised learning for (probability) measure-valued data. In this paper, we utilize the directional asymmetry and dual drift-diffusion control arising in the recently proposed Schr\"odinger-Bass Bridge (SBB) between two measures, and propose \textit{SBB-encoder}, \textit{SBB-decoder}, \textit{SBB-symmetric}, and \textit{SBB-Markovian} barycenter models. For each of these novel models, our first theoretical result establishes the existence of the SBB-barycenter under mild assumptions. Finally, we utilize these algorithms for solving distributional-inverse/interpolation problems arising in the context of spatial images and single-cell RNA sequencing data, where we show that the SBB-decoder and Markovian barycenter allow us to interpolate between successive time points. Results show that our method outperforms existing barycenter models for measure interpolation and inference.

We are the first to give a certified rule for whether unseen data belong in a pretrained model's context or its weights, built on an exact law for their information value. That value, the code length saved on held-out queries, equals in expectation the bits stored about the query, plus a prior repair, minus a decoding loss, for any model, architecture, dataset and task. #9 Residual-Based Causal Discovery via Pretrained In-Context Quantile Regression

要約:
Residual-based causal discovery assumes that each variable is generated from its parents and an independent noise source. It estimates this noise and tests its independence from candidate causes. For identifiable functions such as additive-noise models (ANMs) and the broader class of location-scale noise models (LSNMs), this principle identifies the causal direction, as only the causal model admits this independence. Existing LSNM methods rely on parametric assumptions to obtain standardized residuals, risking model misspecification. In contrast, the RESIT-framework allows for nonparametric estimation of residuals to iteratively identify sink nodes, but was proposed for ANMs. To close this gap, we propose Loscar, an extension of RESIT to LSNMs that estimates the conditional mean and scale via quantile regression, enabling pretrained tabular foundation models as plug-in estimators. We compare it with a feasible generalized least squares (FGLS) variant, which is consistent given suitable basis functions. Across bivariate and multivariate causal-discovery benchmarks, in-context quantile regression substantially outperforms FGLS with flexible function classes, particularly for multivariate causes.
#10 Near Minimax Rate for Quantile Distributional Reinforcement Learning
著者: Zijie Cheng, Zhihua Zhang
公開日: Thu, 22 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29595
要約:
In this paper, we study quantile distributional policy evaluation, where the return distribution under a given policy is approximated by the quantile Bellman fixed point $\bm\eta_m$. We investigate the statistical accuracy of estimating $\bm\eta_m$ across quantile resolutions under Wasserstein distances. Assuming access to a generative model, we construct a quantile fixed point estimator $\bm\eta_m^{(n)}$ from an empirical Markov decision process and establish non-asymptotic error bounds under Wasserstein distances. When reward densities are uniformly bounded away from zero, we obtain a root-$n$ high-probability bound simultaneously over all resolutions $m$ and all Wasserstein orders $1\le p\le\infty$. When reward densities vanish at the support endpoints, we show that continuity and endpoint monotonicity suffice to preserve the same resolution-independent rate for $1\le p\le2$. For $p>2$, we establish a root-$n$ bound with coefficient $m^{1/2-1/p}$, together with a slower, resolution-independent $n^{-1/p}$ bound for every finite $p>2$. We further obtain resolution-independent $W_\infty$ consistency governed by the continuity modulus of reward quantile functions. To investigate the sharpness of these bounds, we characterize the exact local asymptotic minimax risk at each fixed resolution as the expected Wasserstein-coordinate norm of the efficient Gaussian limit and prove that our estimator attains it. We then examine the resolution dependence of this optimal local risk. A regular one-state instance has resolution-independent local risk constants, whereas a single endpoint-vanishing two-state instance exhibits growth of order $m^{1/2-1/p}$, up to logarithmic factors along a resolution subsequence, for every $p>2$.

Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is a fundamental tool for statistical dimension reduction, yet no canonical extension to distribution-valued data has emerged within the framework of optimal transport. While Wasserstein barycenters and Wasserstein least-squares regression provide canonical liftings of Euclidean averaging and linear regression in the Wasserstein space, an analogous principle for PCA has remained unexplored. In this paper, we introduce Lifted PCA, a canonical extension of Euclidean PCA to Wasserstein space, obtained through the framework of lifting functionals in the space of probability measures by convex extension. Starting from the classical variational formulations of PCA on the Stiefel and Grassmann manifolds, we derive two equivalent minimization problems over probability measures supported on these manifolds that correspond to Wasserstein barycenters with generalized costs. Whereas Euclidean PCA summarizes vectors through projections onto an optimal low-dimensional linear subspace, Lifted PCA summarizes probability distributions through an optimal probability measure supported on multiple low-dimensional subspaces. Numerical experiments on distributional datasets illustrate the practical performance of the proposed methodology and its benefits for distributional data analysis.

#13 The Price of Current-Effect Inference in Nonstationary Multi-Armed Bandits
著者: Sharan Sahu, Martin T. Wells
公開日: Thu, 22 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29982
要約:
Adaptive experiments use bandit algorithms to favor interventions with higher expected rewards. However, decision-makers also require reliable inference on current treatment effects: differences between intervention and reference-arm mean rewards at the present time, rather than historical averages. These objectives conflict: reward maximization favors better interventions, whereas current-effect inference requires continued exploration of potentially suboptimal interventions as outcomes change over time. We characterize this tradeoff for $K$-arm bandits with H\"older-smooth mean rewards of exponent $\beta\in(0,1]$ and constant $L$. Requiring high-probability simultaneous coverage of all current effects by confidence intervals of width at most $w$, we establish matching minimax bounds, up to logarithmic factors, on expected dynamic regret. Over horizon $T$, our algorithm TRACE (Tracking Rewards and Current Effects) achieves regret of order $\widetilde{O}_{\beta}\left(\sqrt{KT}+K^{\beta/(2\beta+1)}L^{1/(2\beta+1)}T^{(\beta+1)/(2\beta+1)}+Kw^{-2}(1+L^{1/\beta}w^{-1/\beta})\right)$, where the notation hides logarithms and constants depending only on $\beta$. This regret--precision tradeoff separates reward-learning regret from the cost of refreshing current-effect estimates, which remains unavoidable even when one arm is uniformly optimal. An empirical-Bernstein e-process extension adapts exploration to realized prediction errors while preserving worst-case guarantees. When within-block drift is negligible relative to sampling error, all observations, including reward-learning observations, can be reused for asymptotically normal pointwise inference and stronger local stationarity yields local asymptotic efficiency. Finally, we show that regret constraints can prevent honest adaptation to unknown smoothness and support our theory with extensive numerical experiments.

要約:
RLVR improves language-model reasoning, but commonly relies on independent stochastic rollouts for exploration, which can repeatedly sample similar failed strategies. Prior work has seen success with approaches that guide exploration by introducing dependence into the sampling process by including incorrect solutions in the context for subsequent attempts. Still, these methods introduce the additional question of how to assign credit to each attempt. We propose to view the credit assignment through the lens of Stochastic Shortest Paths, a framework in classical reinforcement learning that seeks to minimize the expected cumulative cost to reach a terminal state (in our case, a verified successful response). Taken together, our results suggest that ReAPO is a simple yet effective approach for improving LM reasoning performance through improved exploration and retry-aware credit assignment.

Conformal prediction (CP) for language models is typically evaluated under a matched protocol, in which calibration and deployment use the same post-training inference configuration, such as quantization mode and inference temperature. In deployment, these choices often differ from those used at calibration to meet memory or throughput constraints or serving policies. We argue that this exposes a limitation in current CP evaluation protocols for language models. We formalize the underlying phenomenon as inference-time model shift: a change in the conformal score distribution induced by inference configuration rather than by the input--label distribution, and theoretically characterize how it breaks split-conformal exchangeability even when the task distribution and trained model are unchanged. 要約:
Many problems in signal processing, machine learning, and neuroimaging provide several jointly observed measurements of one process, each mixing a component common to all of them with a component private to itself, and require the common component to be recovered without supervision. This problem has been studied in several communities, largely independently. Statisticians proposed canonical correlation analysis (CCA), introduced by Hotelling in 1936. Information theorists proposed two notions of common information, due to G\'acs and K\"orner and to Wyner. Engineers developed subspace methods for sensor arrays and multiuser communications to design multiview receivers. Negative guidance of diffusion models generates samples that avoid an undesired condition, specified either by reference samples or by a conditional score alongside an unconditional model. Existing methods inject repulsive terms derived from local information at each sample, so the resulting sampler does not target an explicit density, and its bias cannot be systematically reduced by increasing computation. We instead present a negative-guidance method that samples from an explicit target density, which uniformly handles both reference-sample and score-based specifications and suppresses probability mass near the undesired condition with an avoidance strength that parametrizes the target density itself. Realizing this target requires estimating, at each diffusion timestep, how likely each sample is to belong to the undesired condition. We cast this estimation as a Positive-Unlabeled learning problem that admits lightweight online training along the diffusion trajectory, using condition samples as positives and diffusion particles as unlabeled data. Because the guided particles drift from the unconditional marginal, we further correct the residual mismatch via Sequential Monte Carlo, yielding a sampler that is asymptotically exact in the particle limit and that turns any existing negative-guidance method into its proposal kernel. For nonlinear models, equivalent weights can be defined locally through the sensitivity of a poststratified population estimator. Diffusion models have emerged as the foundation of modern generative systems, yet their high memorization capacity raises privacy concerns. While differentially private (DP) training provides formal guarantees, it comes at a cost in sample quality. In this work, we study a complementary question: how much privacy loss is incurred during the sampling process itself? Directly analyzing modern neural denoisers is challenging because their sensitivity to a single training example is generally intractable. To overcome this challenge, we construct and analyze a clipped privacy-accountable empirical denoiser, a training-free estimator of the Bayes-optimal denoiser, whose per-step sensitivity can be computed explicitly, allowing each denoising step to be a Gaussian mechanism. This perspective enables privacy accounting under Gaussian DP when each training example contributes to each denoising step, and under $f$-DP trade-off functions when each generated sample uses a single randomly subsampled block of the training data. We report every $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ guarantee as a certified upper bound from a privacy loss distribution accountant, and use a cumulant-based Edgeworth approximation as a fast diagnostic, characterizing where it is reliable and where it becomes anti-conservative. We also analyze the privacy loss along the denoising trajectory and show that it grows steeply near the critical window in which semantic content is formed. This observation motivates hybrid samplers that use the private empirical denoiser through the formative steps where it remains a faithful proxy for the neural denoiser, then switch to a public neural denoiser for the remaining refinement. Our results on CIFAR-10 and CelebA-64 show that the privacy of diffusion models depends not only on the training procedure, but also on sampling-time design choices such as subsampling, stopping time, and hybrid denoising.
