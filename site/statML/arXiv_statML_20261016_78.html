Zhang [Zha26] recently posted a manuscript announcing that the list-replicability number of every class of $M\ge 2$ binary concepts on $N\ge 2$ domain points is $O(\log^2 M\log N)$, which would be the first polylogarithmic upper bound on this quantity. #2 Latent Annealed Posterior Sampling with a Variance-Preserving Bridge for Sparsely Observed Random Fields
著者: Jiankun Lei, Tian Qi
公開日: Fri, 16 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20659
要約:
Posterior inference for random fields from sparse observations presents two major challenges: accurately characterizing complex spatial uncertainty and preventing conditional sampling costs from growing substantially as the spatial grid is refined. To address these challenges, we propose latent annealed posterior sampling (LAPS). LAPS first maps the random field into a latent space with reduced dimensionality, while a coordinate decoder composed of Branch and Trunk networks enables field evaluation at arbitrary spatial locations. Flow matching is then used to learn the latent prior distribution. Based on this learned prior, posterior sampling is performed entirely in the latent space, with the observation likelihood incorporated progressively through an annealing procedure. To control local covariance inflation, we introduce a variance-preserving (VP) bridge that allocates a covariance budget between the transported latent state and a Gaussian perturbation. Numerical experiments demonstrate that LAPS accurately approximates posterior distributions, preserves distinct posterior modes, and reduces the dependence of conditional sampling costs on spatial resolution. Across two Gaussian benchmarks, LAPS attains small errors against exact posterior references, with sliced Wasserstein distances only 2 to 3 percent higher than the reference values obtained from exact posterior samples. In a bimodal posterior problem, LAPS reduces the sliced Wasserstein distance from 0.2693 to 0.1499 and consistently recovers both modes across all test scenarios. For a two dimensional Navier-Stokes random field, LAPS substantially improves marginal posterior calibration, while its evaluation time remains nearly constant as the output grid is refined from 64^2 to 512^2. These results demonstrate that LAPS achieves a favorable tradeoff among posterior accuracy, uncertainty calibration, and computational efficiency.
#3 Graph Additive Models disentangling feature, topology, and interactions
著者: Clara Alvarez-Rodriguez, Steve Azzolin, Vincenzo Marco De Luca, Filippo Maria Bianchi, Andrea Passerini, Antonio Longa
公開日: Fri, 16 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20662
要約:
Graph Neural Additive Networks (GNANs) have emerged as effective, interpretable-by-design architectures for graph learning. They achieve transparency by combining per-feature shape functions with simple distance-based reweighting. However, GNANs have three limitations. First, node-wise relevance scores aggregate information from all graph nodes, allowing irrelevant nodes to inherit spurious relevance from relevant ones. Second, topology is represented only through shortest-path distances, so structurally distinct graphs may share identical distance profiles. Third, feature and distance information are combined multiplicatively, entangling feature, distance, and interaction effects in a single rescalable term. We introduce AMIGA, which represents topology through a vocabulary of mined motifs and decomposes each node contribution into three additive channels: feature, structure, and feature-structure interaction. The channels are centered around a graph-balanced training baseline, making the decomposition well-defined and unique under the chosen reference distribution. Across ten graph classification and regression benchmarks, AMIGA outperforms GNAN on most datasets and narrows the gap with standard black-box GNNs. Because each channel is centered and assigned to a single node, AMIGA produces node-local explanations that reconstruct the model output exactly and support interpretation at multiple levels of granularity.
#4 On the Stability and Convergence of Flow Map Training
著者: Ishan Nath, Tom Stanic, Avishek Joey Bose, Gauthier Gidel, Quentin Bertrand
公開日: Fri, 16 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20674
要約:
Flow maps are a recent method of probabilistic generation that amortize diffusion trajectories into one-step samplers, unlocking efficient generation. While progress has been made in improving their empirical performance, there is still a lack of understanding behind the mechanisms underlying their success. Specifically, many flow map learning methods employ stop gradient operators as a crucial part of their training target. Adaptive weighting also drastically improves the generation capabilities of flow maps. Although many interpretations have been proposed to support the inclusion of these heuristics, none have analyzed the impact of these assumptions on the training dynamics of flow maps themselves. In this work, we address this issue by providing grounded theory explaining why such tricks are required. Analyzing the functional gradient flow induced by the MeanFlow and Lagrangian losses, we show their convergence to the true flow map, settling the validity of these objectives. For finite updates, we highlight a severe roadblock to the usage of these losses in practice: the possible unboundedness of the update. Adaptive normalization not only provides bounded updates, but, combined with a specific weighting, additionally provides convergence guarantees towards a neighborhood of the solution. On synthetic data, we show that our functional gradient flow analysis exactly describes the true training dynamics for flow maps. Experiments on standard image datasets demonstrate that a proper reweighting scheme is essential for strong empirical performance, which is only achieved for a specific range of reweighting strengths.
#5 Complete or Sparse: A Tale of Two Identifiabilities
著者: Junze Zhou, David Klindt
公開日: Fri, 16 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20851
要約:
Understanding when neural networks learn interpretable representations is central to mechanistic interpretability. We study this through identifiability: when can supervised learning recover the true latent variables underlying the data? We introduce two data generating processes distinguished by their causal direction where either labels cause latents (generative) or latents cause labels (discriminative). When the representation has at least as many dimensions as the latent space, we prove that the optimal encoders are linear in the latents. Conversely, when dimensions are insufficient, identifiability generically fails and encoders become nonlinear. We then show that additional constraints yield recovery of individual latent variables, not just linear mixtures. In the generative setting, non-negative activations and energy efficiency force each neuron to encode exactly one latent, providing a precise account of selective grandmother cell-like responses. In the discriminative setting, when latents are sparse and the true measurement matrix satisfies the spark condition, the learned encoder inherits compressed sensing structure, enabling sparse dictionary learning to perfectly recover individual latents. This constitutes, to our knowledge, the first identifiability result for undercomplete nonlinear independent component analysis. Our framework unifies nonlinear ICA, compressed sensing, and neural network interpretability, delineating exactly when representations transition from nonlinear distortions to linear representations and, under additional constraints or steps, to clean, axis-aligned codes. Theoretical predictions are validated on synthetic benchmarks and on activations drawn from vision models, large language models, and macaque inferotemporal cortex.
#6 Time-partitioning Gaussian Process Mixture Model
著者: Guilherme Piantino, Hedibert F. Lopes
公開日: Fri, 16 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20860
要約:
We propose the Time-partitioning Gaussian Process Mixture Model (TPGPMM), a Bayesian nonparametric framework for identifying persistent regimes and modelling nonstationary dependence in time series. The model combines an adaptive Pitman--Yor prior on contiguous segmentations with regime-specific Gaussian processes, without imposing Markovian within-regime dependence. Learning the Pitman--Yor discount parameter allows adaptation to homogeneous and heterogeneous regime lengths. A split--merge Metropolis-within-Gibbs sampler with exact Gibbs boundary updates jointly learns change points and model hyperparameters, quantifying uncertainty over segmentations and within-regime dynamics. With an RBF kernel and a fixed, conservative observation-noise level, TPGPMM favours sparse segmentations that distinguish persistent shifts from transient shocks. Simulations demonstrate recovery of the regime structure. Applications to the CBOE Volatility Index (VIX) and the 10-year United States Treasury yield identify regimes aligned with major market and monetary-policy events. Variants that estimate observation noise improve predictive accuracy relative to the main specification, but can produce less sparse or less event-aligned segmentations. A variant with regime-specific means fits better than a fitted stationary Gaussian process and forecasts about as accurately. These results highlight a trade-off between interpretable regime identification and predictive accuracy.
#7 Flexible Doubly Robust Proximal Causal Inference for Censored Outcomes
著者: Dhanajit Brahma, Ruolin Meng, Lawrence Carin, Ricardo Henao
公開日: Fri, 16 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20893
要約:
Estimating causal effects on right-censored time-to-event outcomes is challenging under unmeasured confounding. Proximal causal inference addresses unmeasured confounding through proxy variables, but existing proximal survival methods rely largely on restrictive parametric bridge models. We introduce Proximal Survival Bridge learning (PSB), a flexible doubly robust proximal framework for censored outcomes. Building on existing proximal identification of treatment-specific survival curves, the framework yields outcome-bridge (OB), treatment-bridge (TB), and doubly robust (DR) estimators, with two flexible bridge learners: PSB-K, a kernel estimator with a low-rank time decomposition, and PSB-N, a neural estimator trained by a projected maximum-moment-restriction loss. On a standard SEM benchmark, a proximal semi-synthetic ACTG study, and the real-world Framingham study, PSB-K performs strongest on SEM and ACTG, whereas PSB-N is the most stable on Framingham. Additional results with modern causal survival baselines, weakened or invalid proxies, and heavier censoring assess robustness across challenging settings.
#8 Stochastic Gradient Descent: A Nonlinear Time Series Perspective
著者: Jiaqi Li, Zhipeng Lou, Stefan Richter, Wei Biao Wu
公開日: Fri, 16 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20894
要約:
This paper investigates the statistical behavior of Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) with constant step size under the framework of iterated random functions. Compared to previous studies establishing the convergence of SGD in probability measure, e.g., Wasserstein distance, our approach directly provides the convergence in Euclidean distance by showing the Geometric Moment Contraction (GMC) of SGD. This new convergence can address the non-stationarity of SGD due to fixed initial points and can provide a more refined asymptotic analysis of SGD. Specifically, we prove quenched central limit theorem, invariance principle and $\alpha$-stable convergence for averaged SGD (ASGD) regardless of initialization. Furthermore, we provide a novel perspective to understand the impact of step sizes in SGD by studying its derivative with respect to the step size. The existence of stationary solutions for the derivative processes is shown, enabling the use of interpolation to recover the full-trajectory of SGD. Subsequently, we utilize multiple step sizes and show an enhanced Richardson-Romberg extrapolation with improved bias representation, which brings ASGD estimates closer to the global optimum. Finally, we propose a new online inference method and a bias-reduced variant for the extrapolated ASGD. Empirical confidence intervals are constructed and the coverage probabilities approach the nominal level as $n$ grows.
#9 Ranked Right, Sized Wrong: Ensemble Spread Fails Silently at Streamflow Extremes
著者: Justin Gallant, Tyler Wilson
公開日: Fri, 16 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20947
要約:
Deep learning models, particularly Long Short-Term Memory networks, are increasingly used for operational streamflow forecasting, with ensemble spread providing a signal of predictive uncertainty. As climate change increases the frequency of hydroclimatic extremes, these models will increasingly operate under flow conditions that are rare in their historical records. We therefore evaluate whether ensemble spread remains informative under increasing flows, examining both its magnitude relative to error and its ability to rank errors. Prediction error is approximately $2\times$ the corrected spread at ordinary flows and up to $4\times$ at the highest flows, with the discrepancy increasing with flow (log-log slope $1.30$, 95% CI: $[1.20,1.41]$). Under-dispersion is strongest in observed extreme flows beyond the training record, reaching an $18\times$ discrepancy. This is pervasive and not explainable by conditional mean bias. Area Under the Sparsification Error, a standard distribution-free metric of uncertainty ranking, describing how well a model knows when it is uncertain, indicates near-oracle ranking for ensemble spread ($0.071$), yet ordering by predicted discharge alone nearly matches it. Informative uncertainty ranking can coexist with severe under-dispersion. In deployment, error ranking cannot be verified, yet the spread is available, and it is relied on as the uncertainty signal under increasingly extreme conditions, while systematically understating risk, most severely for the extreme events that matter most.

#11 Acceleration and its Limits in Gaussian Variational Inference
著者: Jaesung R. Park, Uijeong Jang, Yifan Chen
公開日: Fri, 16 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21020
要約:
We study whether the classical momentum-based acceleration that improves the condition-number dependence of gradient descent from $\kappa$ to $\sqrt{\kappa}$ for strongly convex, smooth objectives extends to Gaussian variational inference (GVI) for strongly log-concave, log-smooth target distributions. With exact Gaussian expectations, a convex composite formulation of GVI yields the classical accelerated $\sqrt{\kappa}$ convergence rate. However, when only pointwise derivatives are available, we construct a family of strongly log-concave targets for which any randomized algorithm requires $\kappa^{1-o(1)}$ queries to achieve a fixed objective accuracy, even when the initial objective gap is bounded independently of $\kappa$. Thus curvature bounds alone cannot guarantee uniform accelerated total-query complexity. We then ask which additional structure permits acceleration and give positive examples: under a Hessian-Lipschitz assumption, variance-reduced stochastic methods yield accelerated guarantees when the Hessian variation is sufficiently small. Finally, experiments on real datasets show substantial empirical speedups from acceleration across a broad range of methods.

#14 Dimension-Free PAC-Bayes Generalization for Deep Neural Networks via Dirichlet Processes
著者: Sumit Vashishtha
公開日: Fri, 16 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21340
要約:
Classical PAC-Bayesian generalization theory places prior distributions over Euclidean parameter space $\Theta \subseteq \mathbb{R}^D$, causing relative entropy to scale with network dimension $D$. For modern overparameterized neural networks where $D \gg n$, this parameter-counting dependency yields vacuous generalization bounds ($> 1{,}000\%$). In this work, we resolve this barrier by shifting prior specification from internal parameters to the non-parametric space of data distributions $\mathcal{M}_1(\mathcal{Z})$ via a \textbf{Dirichlet Process (DP)}, $\mathcal{DP}(\alpha, F_0)$, inducing parameter distributions through push-forward measures under the learning algorithm. By the Data Processing Inequality, parameter-space relative entropy is strictly bounded by the data-space DP divergence, which we evaluate in exact closed form using Digamma functions. The resulting PAC-Bayesian certificate is strictly invariant to network parameter count $D$, width, and depth. On intrinsic $d^*$-dimensional submanifolds, optimal partition scaling achieves the minimax non-parametric rate $\mathcal{O}(n^{-1/(d^*+2)} \sqrt{\ln n})$. Across vision architectures scaling up to 4.2 million parameters ($D = 7{,}850$ to $D = 4{,}203{,}522$) on the full MNIST benchmark, while classical weight-space bounds scale past $940\%$ (becoming vacuous), our data-space DP-PAC-Bayes certificate remains stable, non-vacuous, and certified at \textbf{$8.8\%$--$9.4\%$}.
#15 When Is Muon Optimal? Radial Fragility and Minimax Geometry
著者: Ao Xu
公開日: Fri, 16 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21549
要約:
Muon replaces a matrix gradient by an approximate polar factor and has shown strong empirical performance in neural-network training. The Isotropic Curvature Model (ICM) provides a curvature-based explanation for this update by asking when gradient orthogonalization minimizes a one-step local-loss surrogate. We investigate the stability of this explanation when the fixed input radius of the ICM is replaced by a general radial law. We introduce the Random-Radius Isotropic Curvature Model (RR-ICM) and the Polar-Factor Admissibility Set, which encodes exact scaled-polar optimality as a geometric condition on the positive polar factor of the gradient. We derive an exact characterization of this set for arbitrary radial laws and show that all nonscalar admissible responses arise from radial atoms aligned with curvature kinks. Consequently, every atomless radial law exhibits zero-capacity rigidity: exact scaled-polar optimality is possible only when the gradient is already a scaled column isometry. To connect the curvature-based framework with the established spectral geometry of Muon, we introduce the Minimax RR-ICM by replacing directional averaging with worst-case optimization over probability laws on the sphere. We prove that every nonzero global minimizer lies on the polar ray of the gradient, while its scale is determined by a one-dimensional convex problem. The corresponding admissibility geometry is a positive-semidefinite trace slab, in sharp contrast to the spherical-zonoid geometry of the RR-ICM. Together, these results expose the radial fragility of average-curvature explanations and provide a new minimax variational characterization of Muon's polar direction.

Flow matching has emerged as a powerful generative family for simulation-based inference (SBI). However, sampling and density evaluation require numerical integration at inference time. This makes posterior sampling in neural posterior estimation costly. More importantly, it can make repeated likelihood evaluation prohibitively expensive and impractical for Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling, which may require thousands of repeated density evaluations. Robust covariance estimation under entry-wise contamination and missing data is challenging in various signal processing applications. Tabular foundation models based on prior-data fitted networks (PFNs) predict on a new data set in a single forward pass, but they cannot report which inputs their predictions depend on. We propose SelectPFN, a PFN that performs Bayesian variable selection and prediction jointly for sparse nonlinear regression. In one forward pass it returns posterior inclusion probabilities for every variable, and its predictions are computed from sampled subsets only, so excluded variables provably cannot affect them; we show this is exactly what the Bayesian model prescribes. The network is pretrained once on synthetic tasks from a meta-prior built around a group spike-and-slab sparse-input neural network.
