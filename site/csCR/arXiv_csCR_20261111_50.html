#1 Useful Work Can Increase Energy Use: Demand Elasticity and Proof of Useful Work
著者: Isaiah Hull, Or Sattath
公開日: Wed, 11 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13131
要約:
A serious criticism of Bitcoin's Proof-of-Work is its enormous electricity consumption. Proof-of-Useful-Work (PoUW), introduced by Ball, Rosen, Sabin, and Vasudevan (2017), in which mining performs useful computations, was suggested as an alternative to reduce electricity expenditure, with matrix multiplication (MatMul) as a recent concrete instantiation by Komargodski and Weinstein (2026). Perhaps surprisingly, we show that switching to PoUW may increase the total electricity consumption, echoing Jevons' paradox. Intuitively, the PoUW mechanism acts as a subsidy that lowers the effective price of the useful work. Recall that demand is elastic if a discount of x percent in the price leads to an increase of more than x percent in the quantity demanded. If demand is elastic, the resulting increase in quantity demanded leads to a net increase in total electricity consumption; if inelastic, total electricity consumption decreases. Even then, the electricity consumption due to PoUW never vanishes, since the mining subsidy is spent on useful work that would not have been produced without the subsidy. This result is proved in a simple model with electricity-only costs, no PoUW overhead, and an unchanged mining subsidy. The analysis extends to less restrictive settings with adjusted thresholds.
#2 Splitting for Stability: a Modified Reward Scheme for Transaction-Fee-Only Regimes
著者: Eric Brigham, Baigang Chen, Nicholas Hopper
公開日: Wed, 11 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13252
要約:
As Bitcoin's block subsidy declines over time, miner revenue will come to depend increasingly on transaction fees. In fee-dominated regimes, winner-take-all reward structures can create incentives for undercutting and fork-based mining strategies, leading to instability and reduced transaction throughput. To prevent this instability, we propose a reward-splitting mechanism in which the winning block producer receives an $\alpha$-portion of the block reward, while the rest is distributed among other miners; this incentivizes mining on previous blocks with higher rewards, while preserving the incentive to produce blocks with high rewards. We evaluate the proposed reward structure through analysis and simulation. Our analysis derives a local profitability condition for one-step Function-Fork deviations, showing how the winner-side reward, loser-side subsidy, and honest hash power jointly determine whether undercutting is profitable. Simulations show parameter regions in which honest behavior dominates fork-based deviations. In the cases where forking becomes dominant, the learned strategy is the least aggressive variant studied. The analysis and simulations suggest that suitable reward-splitting parameters can reduce the honest participation needed to suppress aggressive forking strategies relative to the winner-take-all structure studied in prior work. Finally, we show that the modified reward scheme does not appear to worsen selfish mining incentives. Together, these results suggest that reward redistribution can mitigate fee-driven fork incentives without introducing stronger selfish mining incentives, providing a possible direction for incentive design in fee-era proof-of-work systems.
#3 Early Rug Pull Detection on Solana
著者: Saba Heydaridoost, Kasra Kashani, Mohammad Mowlavi Sorond, Behnam Bahrak
公開日: Wed, 11 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13342
要約:
Web3 platforms that help users discover and trade newly launched crypto tokens, including launchpads, decentralized-exchange (DEX) interfaces, wallets, and token-analytics sites, must assess tokens before their legitimacy is clear. On Solana, where token creation is inexpensive and tokens use standard programs rather than custom per-token smart contracts, rug pulls can unfold within minutes. Effective warnings must therefore rely on limited histories. Although graph neural networks (GNNs) have improved rug-pull detection on EVM chains, it remains unclear whether transfer structure adds predictive value over non-graph models at minute-scale decision points, and how that value changes as history accumulates. We study 2,700 tokens sampled from SolRPDS, collecting one-minute market histories from Birdeye and token transfers from Solana RPC. We define a rug pull operationally by requiring a liquidity drain, price collapse, and persistent liquidity depletion over a nominal 24-hour period after token creation. All features use only information available at or before each prediction cutoff. We evaluate observation windows of 12.5, 35.5, and 93 minutes, selected in advance from the published lifespan distribution of Solana rug pulls, and represent each token as a temporal transfer graph. Within the first 12.5 minutes, rug-pull and non-rug graphs already differ in size, density, reciprocity, and receiver-side centralization. We compare GNNs against matched non-graph models using the same token-level and graph-summary features on identical samples. A fixed panel of tokens observed in all three windows further isolates the effect of longer observation from changes in token population. Our findings inform the design of early-warning mechanisms for newly launched Solana tokens in Web3 interfaces.
#4 Stinging the Swarm: Extracting Attacker Intelligence from a Twin-Controller OT Honeypot
著者: Canyon Grey Dell'Omo, Joao Pedro de Carvalho Voltani
公開日: Wed, 11 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13406
要約:
Autonomous language-model agents can drive industrial controllers over fieldbus protocols, probing a plant at machine speed and in coordinated swarms. We present Talos, an inline gate that judges every agent request to a controller in milliseconds and routes agents it flags as hostile to a device-faithful decoy -- a mirror twin of the real plant -- where their attack continues harmlessly and is read for intelligence. We evaluate the full pipeline (detect -> quarantine -> mirror -> extract) against 2,400 autonomous attacker agents, 600 in each of four tiers that escalate the attacker's effort to stay hidden, from no concealment to an explicit non-disclosure rule and a trained cover story. No write reached the real plant in any tier: the gate denies the damage path and a device-level envelope check backstops it, while unmapped registers are denied without exception and the gate still permits safe-direction stop commands. Decisions are model-driven in every tier with no inference timeouts, closing a common objection to small on-device judges. Beyond blocking, the pipeline reads the attacker. We find a behavioral structure along two axes -- how persistently an agent attacks and how much it discloses -- and show that the system has a capture channel for three of the four resulting regions: hardened, silent attackers escalate most and are read on the mirror; compliant, talkative attackers volunteer their identity to a diagnostic form before they are ever quarantined; only the paranoid, low-yield region resists both. Attacker identity fields are self-authored cover stories, but involuntary tells -- the self-reported toolchain fingerprint and the attacker's own list of targeted registers -- accumulate across the swarm into a high-confidence profile drawn from agents under explicit orders to stay silent. The gate, mirror, and harness are released under Apache 2.0 with a synthetic device family.
#5 5G CATS: 5G-Based Cryptographic Authorization Against Trafficking of Semiconductor Devices
著者: Sreevikram Rajaraman, Derek Ladd, Nicholas Heinrich-Barna, Srinivas Shakkottai, Jeyavijayan Rajendran
公開日: Wed, 11 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13437
要約:
Enforcing export controls on AI accelerators requires verifying where a chip operates after deployment. Round-trip time measurements can bound a chip's distance from a trusted landmark, but any jitter in honest replies forces the verifier to loosen its timeout, giving an attacker slack to relay traffic over fiber from a restricted region. Prior approaches either cross the untrusted host stack or rely on noisy power-line channels that inflate this slack. We present 5G CATS, a location certification system that pairs a co-packaged 5G radio, cryptographic engine, and hardware interlock on the GPU package with 5G Ultra-Reliable Low-Latency Communication (URLLC) base stations. Terminating the radio link inside the package removes the host software, PCIe fabric, and baseboard management controller from the verification path. We map an authenticated challenge-response protocol onto 5G's slot structure, replace permanent key erasure with short-lived execution leases, and specify a cross-check of the chip's reported serving cell against the carrier's own attachment records. In ns-3 and 5G-LENA simulations of an unloaded cell, our URLLC-based radio profile narrows the 99.9th percentile round-trip spread to 2.60 ms, more than ten times narrower than default 5G. A tracer rejects every round of a fiber relay from 300 km, against 2,000 km for default 5G. Against a 3,000 km attacker, a tracer stays safe at up to 1,410 km from the chip, against at most 619 km for PowerCATS's published model, while default 5G is never safe.
#6 Data-Efficient Unlearning-Based Backdoor Detection in Federated Learning
privacybackdoor
著者: Dowon Kim, Junhao Cai, Sung Il Choi, Dohun Kim, Juhyun Park, Chengjun Jin, Changhee Joo
公開日: Wed, 11 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13491
要約:
Detecting backdoor attacks in federated learning (FL) is challenging because malicious clients can manipulate the global model while preserving main-task performance. Static model-update statistics may therefore fail to clearly distinguish malicious from benign clients. Unlearning-based detectors instead probe client models through loss ascent on clean server data, but detection performance degrades with scarce proxy data. We propose Screening via Unlearning Response Analysis (SURA), a data-efficient detector. SURA reuses the same small clean proxy across multiple train-mode loss-ascent steps, strengthening client responses without additional clean data. For federated vision models with batch normalization (BN), repeated probing also changes BN running statistics and can confound endpoint measurements. SURA addresses this by restoring the shared pre-probe BN state only at readout while preserving the parameter changes induced by probing. This separates repeated probing from response measurement and removes BN drift and the interaction between parameter and BN-state changes from the readout. Across six backdoor attacks, SURA uses only 10 clean server images, outperforms methods under the same data budget, and approaches the robustness obtained with much larger proxy sets.
#7 TERM: Target-Free Jailbreak Defense via Entropy-Guided Residual Modulation
著者: Yunkai Dang, Wanyu Zhang, Meiyi Zhu, Qi Fan, Tianyu Ding, Wenbin Li
公開日: Wed, 11 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13577
要約:
Large language models (LLMs) have shown strong capabilities across diverse applications, making both safety and utility essential for reliable deployment. Jailbreak attacks use diverse forms of adversarial prompting to bypass alignment safeguards and induce LLMs to generate harmful responses. Some representation-based defenses regulate internal activations using predefined steering directions or learned safety regions. However, adapting intervention to each input without a predefined representational target while limiting over-refusal remains a challenge. In this paper, we propose Target-free Entropy-guided Residual Modulation (TERM), an inference-time defense that uses prompt-calibrated attention entropy deviations to guide adaptive modulation of the model's own residual updates. Attention patterns vary across prompts and decoding steps, making raw entropy values difficult to interpret as a consistent signal for intervention. We therefore calibrate attention entropy against each prompt's own prefill baseline to establish an input-specific reference for reducing unnecessary intervention on benign requests. Adaptive scale normalization and temporal accumulation then account for differences in deviation scale and persistence to determine intervention strength. The resulting signal controls bounded scaling of the model's own residual updates, without relying on a predefined safety direction. We compare our method with seven defenses on seven jailbreak attacks and two over-refusal benchmarks across Llama, Qwen, and Gemma models, including Qwen3 models from 0.6B to 32B. Results show our method improves jailbreak robustness while maintaining low over-refusal on benign safety-sensitive requests with modest inference overhead.

#9 MOAI: Module-Optimizing Architecture for non-interactive secure transformer Inference
著者: Linru Zhang, Xiangning Wang, Jun Jie Sim, Zhicong Huang, Jiahao Zhong, Huaxiong Wang, Pu Duan, Kwok-Yan Lam
公開日: Wed, 11 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13641
要約:
Privacy concerns have been raised in Large Language Models (LLM) inference when models are deployed in Cloud Service Providers (CSP). Homomorphic encryption (HE) offers a promising solution by enabling secure inference directly over encrypted inputs. However, the high computational overhead of HE remains a major bottleneck. To address this challenge, we propose MOAI, an efficient HE-based, non-interactive framework for secure transformer inference. MOAI gains significant efficiency improvement from: (1) a novel evaluation flow that combines column and diagonal packing with consistent strategies across all layers, eliminating expensive format conversions. (2) rotation-free algorithms for Softmax and LayerNorm that significantly reduce the number of costly HE rotations, removing 2,448 HE rotations in BERT-base inference. (3) Column packing removes rotations in plaintext-ciphertext matrix multiplications and interleaved batching further reduces the rotations in ciphertext-ciphertext matrix multiplications. MOAI uses at least 1.7x fewer HE rotations compared to the state-of-the-art works across all matrix multiplications of BERT-base. As a result, we achieve a $52.8 \%$ reduction in evaluation time compared to the state-of-the-art HE-based non-interactive secure transformer inference, THOR (Moon et al., CCS 25). We then apply MOAI on the Powerformer's framework and achieve a $55.7 \%$ reduction in evaluation time compared to Powerformer (Park et al., ACL 25), which approximates Softmax and LayerNorm with simpler functions in transformer and proposes HE-based non-interactive transformer inference. We report an amortized time of 2.36 minutes per input on a single GPU environment. We show the extendibility by applying MOAI in LLaMA-3-8B. Our implementation is publicly available as open source.
#10 BypassGym: A Benchmark for Evaluating AI Agents' Endpoint Defense Evasion Capabilities

公開日: Wed, 11 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500

要約:
Artificial intelligence agents increasingly automate cybersecurity tasks, including offensive operations. Frequent attacks across the web make endpoint detection and response (EDR) a critical defense, but whether AI agents can evade it remains unclear. Benchmarks evaluate the offensive capabilities of large language model (LLM) agents in areas such as penetration testing and vulnerability discovery. One important capability that these benchmarks do not address is defense evasion, specifically whether an agent can develop a program that executes a payload without being blocked or terminated by a deployed EDR. We introduce BypassGym, to our knowledge the first benchmark to systematically evaluate this capability on real endpoints. Given command-and-control (C2) shellcode, an agent must generate and compile a loader that executes the payload without being blocked or terminated. A supplementary evasion knowledge skill provides domain knowledge and supports comparisons with and without this input. BypassGym is built on Proxmox VE(PVE), so the evaluation environment runs real Windows systems and EDR products inside virtual machines and returns structured detection feedback to the agent. We evaluate 5 lightweight model backends against 6 EDR products and 3 payloads, and find that even these simple models generate successful submissions, with DeepSeek-V4.1 Flash reaching 17/18 successful runs and Bitdefender allowing only 5 of 15 runs to pass. These results provide a systematic measurement of current AI agents' endpoint defense evasion capabilities and identify areas for defenders to investigate.
