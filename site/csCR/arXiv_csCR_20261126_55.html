#1 Demystifying Real-world LLM Service Freeloading
著者: Xinyi Hou, Shenao Wang, Wenhui Pan, Yanjie Zhao, Haoyu Wang
公開日: Thu, 26 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33218
要約:
Third-party projects repackage free inference from LLM web applications and dedicated clients into independent APIs. We refer to such use beyond providers' permitted access, allocation, or redistribution conditions as LLM service freeloading (LSF). However, its scale and underlying mechanisms remain poorly understood. We present a systematic measurement study of 513 open-source projects, tracing 1,588 inference paths across 64 upstream LLM services and characterizing their development, dependencies, and operating mechanisms. We find that 93.0% of repositories were created in 2026. Direct HTTP or WebSocket requests account for 83.2% of paths, making reproduction of product protocols the dominant invocation method. Projects adapt messages, reconstruct conversation state, and convert responses to support compatible APIs. Account selection or rotation appears on 304 paths (19.1%), allowing projects with multiple eligible accounts to distribute requests across their separate allowances. Our policy analysis finds that 58 of the 62 services with available terms explicitly prohibit at least one examined practice, including automated access, account sharing, or service redistribution. These restrictions cover both downstream API provision and the means of obtaining upstream inference. Our findings inform defenses against unauthorized consumption and redistribution of free LLM resources. We have responsibly disclosed our findings to the relevant providers.
#2 Real Package, Wrong Controller: Static Auditing of Vendor--Protocol Mismatches in LLM-Generated PLC Code
著者: S M Zia Ur Rashid, Sazidul Islam Hira, Suman Rath
公開日: Thu, 26 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33227
要約:
Language models can generate Python that drives industrial protocols on programmable logic controllers, yet existing code-grounding checks ask whether an imported package or symbol exists rather than whether it suits the declared industrial target. We define \emph{metadata-conditioned, import-level vendor--protocol incompatibility} and build \textsc{Proteus-OT}, a label-free static verifier over a version-pinned graph of documented bindings. Across 883 generated scripts, 180 carry an import-level mismatch against the declared target (20.4\%); restricting to single-vendor targets gives 16.3\%, and additionally requiring the offending library to reach a protocol communication call gives 14.2\%, which we read as conditional on the corpus metadata rather than on verified intent. None of the 28 adjudicable human control files carries one, a sample too small to estimate a prevalence. Within the balanced subset, 96.6\% of mismatching scripts import packages and call methods that exist, so an existence baseline does not represent them, and every positive ships with an inspectable witness. We also audit machine-versus-human classification on the same corpus, where character $n$-grams reach macro-F1 1.000 under task, model and vendor-disjoint partitions and survive comment removal, an AST round trip and identifier anonymisation. The trailing newline present in 95.2\% of human files and in none of the generated ones separates the classes on its own. Vendor--protocol consistency is a useful static audit target, whereas provenance classification on repository-labelled corpora is not authorship detection.

Physical fault injection can flip stored weight bits, degrading LLMs and VLMs. Existing work largely overlooks how supply-chain manipulation affects fault vulnerability. Self-evolving defenses improve LLM-agent security by learning reusable policies from observed attacks. However, we demonstrate that this learning process itself can become an attack surface. Using SED (Self-Evolving Defense) in the AgentDojo benchmark, we construct a single prompt-injection episode that causes the defense to learn an over-broad policy and subsequently refuse legitimate requests. #10 Merkle Trees Minimize Verifier Complexity in the ROM
著者: Miranda Christ, Oliver Korten, Tal Malkin, Noam Mazor, Shyamal Patel
公開日: Thu, 26 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33508
要約:
Nearly five decades after their introduction, Merkle trees remain the most efficient hash-based vector commitments. They compress a vector $x \in \{0,1\}^n$ into a commitment whose size is independent of $n$, such that a verifier can open any bit $x_i$ using $\log n$ hash evaluations. The security of the scheme (intractability of finding two distinct openings for a given bit using the same commitment) follows from the collision resistance of the hash function. The verifier's runtime complexity is a bottleneck in many key applications of vector commitments, including SNARKs, credentials, and authenticated dictionaries. As such, a longstanding open question has been whether it is possible to construct hash-based vector commitments where the verifier makes a sublogarithmic number of hash evaluations. We prove that sublogarithmic verifier complexity is impossible to achieve through black-box use of a hash function; we not only rule out constructions from collision resistant hash functions, but in fact show that commitment schemes with sublogarithmic verifier complexity are unachievable in the random oracle model. In particular, any vector commitment with deterministic verifiers has verifier query complexity $\Omega_{\theta}(\log n)$, where $\theta$ is the input length of the random oracle. This bound is matched up to constant factors by high-arity Merkle trees. The key technical lemma in our proof is a disjoint product lemma, which can be viewed as a computational analog of the van den Berg-Kesten-Reimer (BKR) inequality. We believe this lemma is of independent interest, and in particular may be useful for showing other lower bounds and separations in the random oracle model.
#11 PRECEPT: Security Self-Improvement of Coding Agents via Evolving Principles
agent
著者: Keyan Guo, Rupam Patir, Haipeng Cai, Hongxin Hu
公開日: Thu, 26 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33519
要約:
AI coding agents increasingly write and submit code with limited human review, and much of it passes functional tests while remaining insecure. We show that these failures persist. Across 14 task sequences, Codex, Claude Code, and Qwen Code fixed a vulnerability 85.7% of the time when shown the failing security test, then reintroduced the same vulnerability class in a new session 26.7% of the time, about as often as agents that had never seen it (27.5%). The capability is present; the retention is not. Neither recording the repair in the agent's persistent context nor supplying published secure coding principles closed the gap: the recorded fix stayed tied to the family of the task it came from, and the principles, written for human developers, remained too general to apply to the code at hand. The knowledge exists; it is not activated. We call the missing ability security self-improvement: an agent learns from its own security failures so that each lesson persists across sessions, applies to new code, and does not break what works. We present PRECEPT, which equips coding agents with this ability through an evolving library that starts from authoritative secure coding principles and revises them against the agent's own failures, so that each principle comes to fire on the code where that agent actually goes wrong. A revision is kept only if fresh executions show it makes code more secure while keeping functional success within a small tolerance of the agent without the library. Improvement continues across evolution steps rather than saturating after the first. On task families never seen during evolution, PRECEPT's weakest agent reaches 97.1% secure working code, above the strongest unaided agent at 81.9% and the strongest baseline at 87.7%.

要約:
Modern cryptographic protocols such as WPA2-PSK, Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE), and Bluetooth Classic (BC) tolerate low-entropy keys for user-friendliness, backward compatibility, or resource-constrained deployment, yet existing symbolic protocol verifiers rely on the perfect-encryption assumption and therefore cannot reason about brute-force attackers. Prior work addresses this gap only through protocol-specific, manually encoded reveal rules, which neither generalize across protocols nor systematically explore compound-key decomposition. We introduce the first protocol-agnostic, automated symbolic methodology for low-entropy keys, built on three abstractions: the message-derivation fact MDerive, which captures brute-force prerequisites independently of concrete function symbols; a three-tier breaking-oracle design (value-breaking, relationship-breaking, message-derivation) that composes with the standard Dolev-Yao attacker; and a graded bit-level attacker model that bounds the key length an attacker is allowed to exhaust. Across three real-world protocols and six BC device configurations, our results show that entropy-aware symbolic analysis is both feasible and necessary.

公開日: Thu, 26 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33672
要約:
Node-differentially private algorithms enable learning on network-structured datasets in which records reflect interactions among individuals. They protect the privacy of each individual's information, including all their associated records. A key challenge in the design of such algorithms is to exploit the structure of realistic graphs---where most nodes have low degree---to achieve high accuracy, without compromising on the worst-case privacy guarantee. In this work we give two new tools for designing accurate, computationally efficient node-differentially private algorithms. First, we give a nearly linear time, randomized, node-stable graph projection: the projection leaves graphs of maximum degree $D$ unchanged, maps arbitrary graphs to subgraphs of maximum degree $\hat D \approx D$, and ensures that changing one node of the input graph has only a small effect on the distribution of the edges selected among the remaining vertices. Unlike prior approaches, which abstain when the input violates the degree bound, our projection acts as degree clipping: even when $D$ is set well below the true maximum degree (trading truncation bias at high-degree outliers for lower privacy noise), it retains at least as many edges as a maximal $D$-bounded subgraph. Second, for graphs with bounded edge weights, we give a deterministic nearly linear time Lipschitz extension for the total edge weight that agrees with the original sum on graphs of maximum degree $D$ yet is $D$-Lipschitz on all graphs. Finally, we show how to apply these methods to design node-private algorithms for high-dimensional vector summation and gradient descent on datasets where records are associated with graph edges, such as email threads.
