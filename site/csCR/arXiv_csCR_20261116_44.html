#1 Beyond Attack-Based Accuracy: Rethinking Adversarial Robustness Evaluation Through Structural Gradient Alignment
著者: Hanrui Wang, Yu Luo, Shuo Wang, Xingbo Dong, Zhe Jin, Isao Echizen
公開日: Mon, 16 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17377
要約:
Visual adversarial robustness remains a fundamental security property as vision systems evolve from conventional classifiers to vision-language models (VLMs) and large vision-language models (LVLMs), with visual inputs remaining an attack surface across generations. Yet existing evaluation methods often depend on prescribed attacks, in-distribution data, extensive probing, or classifier-specific formulations, limiting external auditing of diverse models. We introduce Structural Gradient Alignment (SGA), a complementary robustness metric measuring the structural alignment between input content and objective-conditioned local model sensitivity. SGA uses a few randomly sampled, unlabeled public probes that need not follow the downstream task distribution and only one input-gradient computation per model--probe pair. Across 38 distinct DNNs, VLMs, and LVLMs, SGA produces rankings consistent with attack-based references. Without robustness references, uncalibrated SGA achieves $83.72\%$ average pairwise agreement across eight public-probe conditions, outperforming the strongest overall baseline in all eight by $9.38$ percentage points on average. With known robustness orders of anchor models, calibrated SGA increases agreement to $85.36\%$. These results demonstrate an attack-agnostic, computationally efficient robustness signal across conventional DNNs, VLMs, and autoregressive LVLMs, downstream tasks, threat norms, probe distributions, and evaluator-knowledge settings. Our implementation is available at https://github.com/azrealwang/SGA.
#2 Evolutionary-based Transfer Learning for Physical Attack on End-to-End Autoencoder Communications
著者: Ho Viet Duc Luong, Lang Hong Nguyet Anh, Huynh Thi Thanh Binh, Trinh Van Chien
公開日: Mon, 16 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17393
要約:
End-to-end autoencoder communication has emerged as a promising means of achieving efficient data transmission across various domains. Nevertheless, vulnerabilities have recently been revealed in these systems by adversarial attacks, wherein malevolent entities manipulate the transmitted signals to deceive the autoencoder. This paper leverages the applications of evolutionary algorithms to solve a challenging optimization problem of launching physical attacks against end-to-end autoencoder communication. For white-box scenarios, we introduce PAGA (Physical Attack based on Genetic Algorithm), which combines a genetic algorithm with elite population initialization to craft attack signals that degrade autoencoder performance. To gauge the efficacy of these attack signals, we employ a fitness function that assesses their capacity to induce substantial reconstruction errors and misclassification rates. A Multi-Factorial version of PAGA, referred to as PAMFGA, is proposed to deal with practical scenarios where the attackers have limited knowledge about the receiver, also known as a black-box attack. Our experimental results show that the proposed attacks can substantially degrade the reliability of the considered autoencoder communication systems across the evaluated settings. This study also underscores the critical importance of incorporating security considerations into the design and implementation of end-to-end autoencoder communication systems.
#3 Can LLMs Write Backdoors? An Empirical Security Study of Weight-Space Programming
backdoor
著者: Zhaojia Wang, Jiucui Lu, Zheng Li
公開日: Mon, 16 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17395
要約:
Recent advances in weight-space programming (WSP) have demonstrated that large language models (LLMs) can directly inspect and modify neural network weights without conventional gradient-based training. While this capability opens new possibilities for model improvement, it also raises an important security question: Can LLMs exploit weight-space programming to inject backdoors into neural networks? To answer this question, we present an empirical security study of LLM-driven weight-space programming for backdoor injection. Specifically, we investigate whether general-purpose LLMs can directly modify the weights of target models according to natural-language instructions, without accessing task-specific data, performing forward passes, or computing gradients. We evaluate five LLMs as weight editors across three target architectures: an MLP on MNIST, ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10, and Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct on AG News. Our experiments reveal three key findings. First, LLM-driven weight editing can successfully inject backdoors across different architectures while largely preserving clean performance. Second, successful attacks can require remarkably few parameter modifications, including only six parameters for the MLP and 0.02% of the weights for a fine-tuned Qwen model. Third, editing capabilities vary substantially across LLMs and target architectures: although all five LLMs successfully backdoor the MLP, only GPT-6 Astra succeeds across all three targets. These findings highlight an emerging security risk of weight-space programming and underscore the importance of safeguarding LLM-driven weight editing.
#4 I2C-GRPO: Insight Importance Checklist for Reinforcement Learning in a Multi-Agent DDoS Alert Triage Workflow
agent
著者: Eyal Zloof, Shaun Shuster, Aviram Zilberman, Chen Frydman, Inderjeet Singh, Andr\'es Murilo, Asaf Shabtai, Yuval Elovici, Rami Puzis
公開日: Mon, 16 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17534
要約:
Large language model (LLM) agents are increasingly used for investigative tasks over incomplete evidence, such as cybersecurity alert triage, but reinforcement learning (RL) for such tasks is difficult: intermediate steps have no verifiable labels, rollouts are costly, and outcome rewards are sparse. We propose Insight Importance Checklist (I2C)-GRPO, a checklist reward for intermediate investigation steps. For each decision point, a panel of expert LLMs proposes investigative actions, their atomic forensic insights are extracted, and a separate three-model ranker panel orders the insights by importance. A reward model checks which ranked insights a candidate action targets and returns their consensus-weighted coverage. Because actions are scored without being executed, the reward is a proxy for expected investigative value, and training reduces to a one-step contextual bandit over fixed investigation prefixes. From a soft-computing perspective, the reward is an additive fuzzy measure whose rank-exponent weights act as an ordered weighted averaging (OWA)-type operator; one exponent sets its orness, i.e., how strongly the reward concentrates on the top of the judges' consensus. We train an open-weight Qwen3-4B Triage Agent within a fixed multi-agent Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) alert-triage workflow on DARPA 2000 LLDOS 1.0 and test it on an independent CSE-CIC-IDS2018 DDoS trace. Across three training seeds, the agent reaches a mean F1-score of 0.869, 5.9 and 9.7 points above uniform insight weights and DIVA, and a false-positive rate of 4.9% versus 16.4% for GPT-5-mini. Within the weighting family, false positives fall only at the highest-orness weighting evaluated. All claims are limited to DDoS alert triage; the code, task contexts, and ranked insight dataset will be released.
#5 Online Allocation with Differential Privacy
privacy
著者: Jianyi Yang, Xingyu Zhou, Mostafa Mushsharat, Shaolei Ren
公開日: Mon, 16 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17554
要約:
We study private online allocation problems, where allocation decisions must satisfy differential privacy (DP) to protect sensitive user information while optimizing performance under limited resources. We first formalize differential privacy notions suited to different privacy requirements in online allocation and establish the fundamental performance limits of any algorithm under these definitions. Based on these insights, we propose PPOA, a privacy-preserving meta algorithm for online allocation, and establish sufficient conditions under which PPOA preserves Joint DP (JDP) or Local DP (LDP) while achieving asymptotically near-optimal performance. Building on these conditions, PPOA can be instantiated into a variety of concrete algorithms. In particular, we present the specific designs, which include PPOA-DMD that preserves JDP and LDP and PPOA-FTRL that preserves JDP. We analyze their performance under both adversarial and stochastic settings and characterize the fundamental trade-offs between DP and allocation performance. Finally, we demonstrate the superior performance of the proposed algorithms via numerical experiments on AI model routing in battery-powered edge systems.
#6 Tractable Importance Sampling for Monte Carlo Privacy Accounting
privacy
著者: Galen Andrew, Arun Ganesh
公開日: Mon, 16 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17596
要約:
Evaluating tight Differential Privacy (DP) parameters for DP matrix factorization with privacy amplification by sampling requires analyzing high-dimensional, correlated Gaussian mixtures. Because integrating these mixture distributions to find the exact privacy failure probability ($\delta$) is analytically intractable, these mechanisms are typically analyzed using Monte Carlo accounting. However, standard sampling suffers from prohibitive variance when estimating small $\delta$ associated with strict privacy guarantees, which in turn requires a large sample complexity, making Monte Carlo accounting inefficient. We demonstrate that Monte Carlo accounting can be made much more efficient via importance sampling. We give a shifted importance sampling proposal distribution parameterized by a stretch factor $\alpha > 1$. We prove a closed-form bound $B(\alpha) < 1$ on the support of the importance-weighted estimator that holds identically for both directions of the hockey-stick divergence, show that $B$ is log-convex so that the optimal $\alpha$ is a one-dimensional root-finding problem, and show that the resulting estimator seamlessly plugs into the KL-Hoeffding verification protocol used by past work, with a sample complexity reduced by exactly a factor of $1/B(\alpha^*)$.
#7 Binarization as an Attack Surface in Weightless Neural Networks
著者: Lucas Senos Coutinho, Matheus Vargas, Pedro Teixeira, Heudson Tosta Mirandola, Ori Rottenstreich, Priscila Machado Vieira Lima, Leandro Santiago, Daniel Sadoc Menasche
公開日: Mon, 16 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17646
要約:
Weightless Neural Networks (WNNs) are lightweight classifiers that binarize their inputs and learn by storing binary patterns in lookup memories, which makes them attractive for resource-constrained tasks such as intrusion detection. Because a small perturbation that crosses no binarization threshold leaves the binary input unchanged, WNNs may appear naturally robust to adversarial examples; a perturbation that does cross a threshold, however, can still change the prediction. We study this tension in WiSARD, the canonical RAM-based WNN, and in two variants that replace its memories with Bloom filters, Bloom WiSARD and BTHOWeN. A continuous perturbation reaches these classifiers only by crossing an encoding threshold, changing at least one binary tuple, and redirecting a memory query; we formalize this mechanism with a threshold-crossing bound and a response-margin certificate for standard Boolean WiSARD. Controlled MNIST experiments compare linear and data-driven thermometer encodings under model-agnostic sensitivity probes, a score-based Simulated Annealing attack, and surrogate-transfer attacks. Changing only the encoding while keeping each model otherwise fixed shows that encoding granularity can trade a small clean-accuracy loss for a large decrease in attack success. On the Bot-IoT, CICIDS, and UNSW-NB15 intrusion-detection datasets, adversarial perturbations lead WNN detectors to classify many attacks as normal traffic and to raise false alarms, even when their aggregate accuracy remains high.
#8 iGRAIL: Blockchain-Based Generative Federated Learning for Trustworthy Healthcare Intelligence
著者: Debashis Das, Sourav Banerjee, Saraju P. Mohanty
公開日: Mon, 16 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17703
要約:
Healthcare organizations collect huge amounts of patient data from clinical records, medical tests, monitoring systems, and healthcare services. Such data can underpin intelligent healthcare analytics but remains distributed across institutions and cannot be shared directly due to privacy, security, and trust concerns. Federated learning (FL) enables collaborative learning without sharing raw patient records, but the exchanged model information remains vulnerable to privacy leakage, tampering, replay, and unauthorized access. In this paper, we propose iGRAIL, a blockchain-enabled generative FL framework for trustworthy healthcare intelligence. iGRAIL combines local generative learning for data augmentation with differential privacy to protect sensitive information during local training. Model communication is secured using cryptographic mechanisms, and blockchain-based verification and smart-contract access control ensure that only authorized, authentic, fresh, and integrity-preserving updates are accepted for federated aggregation. Increasing the strength of differential privacy reduces the privacy-loss parameter $\epsilon$ by about 72.5% for the cancer dataset and 87.5% for the diabetes dataset, with only a limited loss in prediction performance. These results indicate that iGRAIL supports secure and scalable federated computing while preserving privacy, model integrity, and controlled participation, at the cost of additional computational overhead.
#9 Evaluating the Robustness of PRNU Source-Camera Attribution to the Fingerprint-Copy Attack: An Open Toolkit with Triangle-Test Detection
著者: Zeno J. M. H. Geradts
公開日: Mon, 16 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17747
要約:
Photo-Response Non-Uniformity (PRNU) is the de facto standard trace for sensor-level source-camera attribution, and a high Peak-to-Correlation-Energy (PCE) between a questioned image and a camera reference fingerprint is routinely treated as strong evidence of common origin. The fingerprint-copy attack of Goljan, Fridrich and Chen (2011) shows that this signal can be forged: an adversary who estimates a target camera's fingerprint from that camera's publicly available images can inject a scaled copy of it into an unrelated photograph, producing a forgery that clears a PCE decision threshold despite never having been taken by the target device. We present prnucf, an open, auditable toolkit that pairs a reference implementation of the (additive) fingerprint-copy attack with the triangle-test detector and a cohort-calibrated decision procedure, so that practitioners can quantify how fragile a PRNU attribution is against this attack on their own pipelines. On a controlled multi-camera Monte-Carlo benchmark the attack reaches an arbitrary target PCE at a mean PSNR of 59.2 dB (visually lossless), yet the triangle test separates forgeries from innocent images with an area under the ROC curve of 0.999, detecting every forgery at a cohort-calibrated 5% false-positive operating point. We discuss why a fixed significance cut-off is not safe in casework, the limitations of synthetic validation, and the open problem of extending explainable detection to the modern, GAN-based generation of fingerprint-transfer attacks. The toolkit is released under the MIT license with an explicit dual-use statement.
#10 Inferring Sensitive Events in Multi-Mode Cyber-Physical Systems via Scheduling Side Channels
著者: Yifan Cai, Linh Thi Xuan Phan
公開日: Mon, 16 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17761
要約:
Many cyber-physical systems (CPS) are multi-mode: they switch between operating modes with different tasksets and timing parameters in response to physical events or user activity. We show that these mode changes create a timing side channel: by altering the interference experienced by co-running tasks, they let an unprivileged co-resident observer infer sensitive events, such as a homeowner's arrival or a vehicle's route, from its own execution timing and, on multicore platforms, its core assignment. Existing schedule-based attacks and defenses miss this channel because they assume single-mode systems or target a specific, known victim task. We present STAMP, a lightweight attack that needs no knowledge of task parameters or scheduling policy, and works whether or not the system exposes its modes. On a ZoneMinder home-security deployment, STAMP identifies the modes with 94.7% accuracy on unseen hardware, using under 0.5% of one core. On ROS 2 AMCL with the Oxford RobotCar dataset, it identifies which of seven route segments a vehicle traversed with 86.0% accuracy on unseen platforms. A schedule-randomization defense lowers detection recall to 0.53, at which setting only 53.9% as many jobs finish on time. At higher noise ($\epsilon=10$), on-time job completion reduces to 14.4%, while introducing new leakage in 14.1% of the tasksets.

Jailbreak attacks can circumvent the safety mechanisms of Large Language Models (LLMs) and lead to the generation of harmful or policy-violating content. We first examine eight jailbreak methods against an LLM-based filter and observe that requests resembling normal user queries tend to have higher filter pass rates than those containing obvious anomalous patterns. This observation motivates the hypothesis that resemblance to normal requests may contribute to jailbreak success. We introduce \textbf{Detail Guidance Attack (DGA)}, a framework that uses an auxiliary LLM to construct detailed requests and interacts with the victim model in a single turn. The survey documents differing expectations about whether models should respond; the usability costs of the filter remain to be measured.
#12 Securing Multi-Agent Systems by Design
agent
著者: Xilong Wang, Xiaoxing Chen, Neil Gong
公開日: Mon, 16 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17883
要約:
Multi-agent systems solve complex tasks by decomposing them into subtasks, delegating the subtasks to agents, and aggregating the agents' responses using an aggregation rule. Malicious agents can manipulate the final solution by providing malicious responses to their assigned subtasks. In this paper, we first propose response manipulation attacks (ReMA), which iteratively optimize malicious responses to maximize attack effectiveness. Specifically, we formulate untargeted and targeted attacks as optimization problems and develop an approach to approximately solve these problems by iteratively refining malicious responses based on feedback from a surrogate multi-agent system. To defend against such attacks, we further propose SecMAS, a secure aggregation rule that leverages the semantic similarities among agent responses to select the response that is most consistent with a sufficiently large subset of the other responses. We theoretically establish security guarantees for SecMAS against both untargeted and targeted attacks. Extensive evaluations demonstrate that existing aggregation rules are vulnerable to ReMA, whereas SecMAS effectively defends against all evaluated attacks, including adaptive variants of ReMA, while maintaining utility comparable to that of existing aggregation rules.

#15 Condensing Law of Security Information
著者: Qi Tan, Tianyu Cui, Qi Li, Ke Xu
公開日: Mon, 16 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17918
要約:
Scaling laws indicate that the generalization error of a large language model (LLM) decreases monotonically with model size once data is sufficient, and the corollary that larger models outperform smaller ones is now widely accepted. We argue that this corollary fails in security: decisions there hinge on anomalous inputs, which are long-tailed (e.g., zero-day attacks) and therefore poorly served by likelihood fitting. Worse still, the capability brought about by scale (e.g., inferring the intended whole from a corrupted part) leads a LLM silently \emph{repair} the anomaly before any downstream security head ever sees it. We formalize this as a tradeoff between two quantities: the cognitive capability $K(W)=I(W;P)$ that a model holds about the world distribution, and the security-information retention $R(W)=\frac{I(Z;S)}{I(A;S)}$ that survives in its intermediate representation. We derive three information-theoretic bounds to connect them: an upper bound on restoration accuracy in terms of $K(W)$, an upper bound on $R(W)$ in terms of the restoration error, and a Fano lower bound on security-task error in terms of $R(W)$. These theorems approximately imply that usable security information is a product of how much security information survives and how well it can be extracted, indicating that optimal usable security information can be achieved at a certain model size. We state this as the \emph{condensing law of security information}: usable security information is unimodal in model size, so security generalization error is U-shaped and is minimized at a finite $N^{\star}$ rather than decreasing without bound. The experiments on various real-world LLMs validate the condensing law, indicating that there is an optimal model size to solve security issues.

#17 Hello Again: Exploiting Replay Protected Monotonic Counters to Brute-Force Windows Hello PINs
著者: Christian Werling, Matti Finder, Hans Niklas Jacob, Julian Prein, Niclas K\"uhnapfel, Jean-Pierre Seifert
公開日: Mon, 16 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18126
要約:
Windows Hello is the primary authentication mechanism on over one billion Windows 11 devices, supporting PINs as short as four digits. Such low-entropy PINs are viable only because the TPM's anti-hammering protection mitigates brute-force attacks. Most modern x86 platforms rely on firmware TPMs (fTPMs) that persist their non-volatile (NV) state, including the lockout state, to SPI flash. On newer platforms, rollback prevention of this state is delegated to the flash chip via Replay Protected Monotonic Counters (RPMCs), a JEDEC-standardized mechanism operating over a physically accessible bus. We present the first practical security analysis of RPMC in shipping x86 systems. Using low-cost interposer hardware, we identify multiple implementation flaws, two of which fundamentally undermine RPMC's security model: First, Winbond SPI flash - the leading NOR flash vendor with over 25% market share - leaks its HMAC key under voltage glitching, enabling arbitrary counter forgery. Second, AMD's NV controller does not verify RPMC increments, so an interposer can suppress them unnoticed. Moreover, AMD platforms without RPMC flash appear to lack NV rollback protection altogether: on two AMD desktops, reflashing an old image resets the fTPM's lockout counters. Both flaws enable rollbacks of the fTPM's NV state (AMD, Intel, or Pluton), including its lockout counters. We demonstrate this end-to-end on locked, encrypted hardware: our setup finds a 4-digit Windows Hello PIN in 40 hours instead of 69 days. A compromised PIN also grants access to device-bound keys and Hello-based enterprise SSO. The same primitive defeats BitLocker's TPM+PIN pre-boot protector, reducing the worst-case time for a default-length 6-digit PIN from 19 years to 20 days.
