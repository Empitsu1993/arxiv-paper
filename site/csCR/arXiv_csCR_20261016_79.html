#1 Forensic Analysis of the HackRF One with PortaPack Mayhem Firmware
著者: D\"uzg\"un K\"u\c{c}\"uk
公開日: Fri, 16 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20647
要約:
Software-defined radios (SDRs) have become an inexpensive, portable platform for replay attacks, location spoofing and jamming. The HackRF One can operate stand-alone when fitted with a PortaPack interface board running the open-source Mayhem firmware. No peer-reviewed study has addressed the forensic analysis of this device family. This work establishes, at source-code level, where forensically relevant data reside on the microSD card of a device running Mayhem v2.0.1, in which format, when they are written and how they should be interpreted. The methodology is applied to an SD card image that the author acquired from their own device after four months of real use, and every inference is drawn from the image alone. The workflow covers timestamp rules that depend on the real-time clock (RTC) battery state, traces of on-device trimming and text editing, cluster-level recovery of deleted in-phase/quadrature (IQ) captures, and decoding of recorded signals cross-checked with an independent decoder. In the example image, the final part of a deleted IQ capture and a trimmed-away capture segment are recovered, and six captures are attributed to a single rolling-code on-off keying (OOK) transmitter sharing one 32-bit identifier. The study provides practitioners with an artifact catalogue, interpretation rules and acquisition recommendations.
#2 Unmasking the Secret Code: Using Canaries to Show How Masked Context Controls Memorization in SFT
privacy
著者: Avi Schwarzschild, Om Thakkar, Sahra Ghalebikesabi, Congzheng Song, Vinnie Monaco
公開日: Fri, 16 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20656
要約:
Canaries, or synthetic training records containing known secrets, are widely used to assess whether language models reveal information from their training data. But how much do these measurements depend on the canaries themselves? We study this question through controlled fine-tuning experiments with language and vision-language models, spanning synthetic tasks and tasks where training improves useful performance. Whether a secret is provided in the training input strongly changes what the model learns; for example, models can reproduce a secret in the input yet fail to recall it when it is removed, even after extensive repetition during training. When training instead requires predicting the secret from the surrounding context, later recall can become reliable. Repetition alone is therefore insufficient to characterize memorization. We also find that changing when a canary appears, or which other canaries accompany it, can substantially change measured memorization without increasing that canary's repetition count. These results show that canary measurements depend on how secrets are presented during training and tested afterward. Audits should test whether their conclusions hold across these choices before extending them to other training records.

Classical machine learning models remain among the most widely used approaches for cloud cryptojacking detection. This paper presents an empirical evaluation of six widely used classical machine learning models for AI-based cloud cryptojacking detection under different preprocessing and evaluation settings. Because no public cloud-native cryptojacking dataset is currently available, experiments were conducted using two publicly available proxy datasets, DS2OS and NSL-KDD. #5 CascadeLedger: Counterfactual Auditing of Information and Resource Integrity in LLM Cascades

要約:
We introduce CascadeLedger, a counterfactual execution-audit framework for budgeted language-model cascades. The protocol enforces call-before-read semantics, binds successful reads to service and cache identities, records availability and component-wise budget transitions, and freezes the event trace before any target or task scorer is joined. The central object is aggregate-equivalent trace validity: two executions can share a final answer, declared resource projection, and service histogram while differing in whether their routing decisions used only legitimately observable information. We complement single-trace invariants with counterfactual prefix non-interference (CPNI), which couples a visible prefix and policy randomness while changing only a declared hidden family. The online recorder and offline CPNI audit have separate overhead measurements. Learned routing and semantic verification provide downstream compatibility studies under the same execution contract.
#6 Foundations of Hardware-Software Leakage Contracts
著者: Jan Reineke
公開日: Fri, 16 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20754
要約:
Microarchitectural attacks such as Spectre and Meltdown undermine security by exploiting software-visible side effects of microarchitectural optimizations like caches and speculative execution. To use modern hardware securely, programmers must take into account the security implications of these optimizations. Yet instruction set architectures (ISAs)---the conventional abstraction layer between hardware and software---are ill-suited for secure programming: by design, they abstract from microarchitectural details and therefore fail to reflect their security consequences. A new security abstraction is emerging to fill this void: hardware-software leakage contracts. To support the principled development of secure software on modern hardware such contracts characterize side-channel leakage at the ISA level. Thereby they enable programmers and compilers to generate code that is provably resistant to side-channel attacks---without having to explicitly reason about the microarchitecture. On the other hand, they provide a specification for hardware designers to ensure that microarchitectures do not introduce unintended and undocumented side channels. This paper introduces the foundations of this emerging topic, discussing recent contributions and unresolved challenges along the way.

要約:
LLM-based agents increasingly execute long-horizon, end-to-end workflows involving shell commands, code modification, dependency installation, and interaction with remote services. This growing autonomy expands their exposure to indirect prompt injection (IPI), where adversarial instructions embedded in external content are interpreted as legitimate task directives. Existing evaluations, however, often focus on isolated malicious strings, single-step tool calls, or narrowly scoped agent configurations. We introduce INFUSE, an end-to-end benchmark for evaluating IPI throughout the lifecycle of long-horizon agent workflows. INFUSE comprises 40 realistic tasks and a structured taxonomy of 56 attack scenarios spanning five threat categories. Attacks are injected through realistic interaction surfaces encountered during task execution, extending evaluation beyond attacks placed only in initial prompts or isolated tool interactions. We evaluate 19 LLMs from the GPT, Claude, Gemini, Llama, Gemma, and Qwen families, producing 41,824 adversarial trajectories (26,220 without defenses) and 720 benign reference runs. Across the benchmark, mean attack success reaches 19.6% under LLM-based evaluation and 15.4% under heuristic verification, with per-model ASR-L ranging from 3.2% to 41.9%. Existing guard models provide limited protection, while prompt-level instructional hardening reduces mean attack success from 58.2% to 14.3%, revealing substantial but model-dependent security-utility trade-offs. Our code is available at https://github.com/judydnguyen/infuse.

#10 GhostLock: Learning-Assisted Blind GPS Signal Nullification Without Victim-Side Information

要約:
The Global Positioning System (GPS) provides critical positioning, navigation, and timing (PNT) services for numerous safety- and mission-critical applications, making it an attractive target for physical-layer attacks. While GPS spoofing and jamming have been extensively studied, GPS Signal Nullification (GSN)-a physical-layer attack that suppresses authentic satellite signals through destructive interference prior to acquisition-remains largely unexplored. Existing wireless signal nullification techniques target conventional communication systems and typically require victim-side information, such as channel estimates or synchronization parameters, making them impractical for GPS. Furthermore, many GPS spoofing defenses assume authentic satellite signals remain available as trusted references, an assumption invalidated by GSN. To address this gap, we present GhostLock, a learning-assisted blind GSN framework requiring only an approximate target location estimate. GhostLock integrates a Blind Adaptive Filter (BAF) with a deep learning-based synchronization module to generate and align an anti-signal using only the attacker's locally received GPS signal, eliminating the need for victim-side observations. We implement GhostLock on a real-time software-defined radio (SDR) platform using GPS-SDR-SIM and GNU Radio. Experimental results demonstrate successful suppression of GPS signal acquisition, establishing the feasibility of blind GSN and motivating new detection and mitigation techniques for this emerging GPS threat model.

LLM coding agents such as Claude Code and OpenAI Codex can drive penetration-testing tools, but they reason about an engagement as a flat list of findings in a context window. Prompt-injection defenses are commonly evaluated as binary detectors, although deployed LLM applications must make operational decisions about whether to allow, block, or escalate potentially malicious inputs. #13 Screwtape: Adversarial Transcript Editing for Monitor Red-Teaming

要約:
We present Screwtape, a pipeline that converts real agent transcripts into attacks that evade action-only monitors. An LLM attacker edits a transcript so that the agent causes a specified harm, such as leaking an API key or spawning an unmonitored agent. The edited actions are never executed, so we use an LLM verifier to reject attacks that are inconsistent with the original transcript or below a severity bar. The attacker revises the edit until the monitor no longer flags it. We applied Screwtape to 94 Claude Code and Codex transcripts from Redwood Research staff, optimizing against our own monitors. Across 252 runs, it produced 35 attacks that passed the verifier and evaded these monitors. We expect to find more attacks when the pipeline runs inside the developer's infrastructure, with frontier attacker models that lack refusal safeguards and direct optimization against the production monitor. Developers can use these attacks to fix monitor blind spots, and evaluators can use them as counterexamples to safety claims about the monitor.

The architecture also introduces hardware-assisted page migration: driven by access counters, the system dynamically promotes frequently accessed pages from host CPU memory to GPU DRAM to improve memory locality. When an available registry omits the declared seller ID, the authorization remains visible and machine-readable, but its public verification path breaks. We call this failure the seller-identity gap. We measure the gap using 233.4 million normalized advertising-transparency records and an absent-only, coverage-aware evidence rule. A declaration enters the numerator only when the applicable registry is available and omits its seller ID; unavailable, malformed, confidential, and other non-evaluable states remain separate. The resulting row-level floor is 24.25% in a high-popularity, low-removal reference population and 30.71% among publishers associated with at least 100 copyright-removal requests, a 6.45 percentage-point increase. The gap then remains near one third at stricter cumulative thresholds. Standardizing the populations to common weights over 1,469 shared ad systems reduces the primary contrast to 4.65 points: about 72% remains within systems, while 28% forms a composition-associated residual. We connect these population estimates to recurring seller-account coordinates, byte-identical declaration configurations, and provenance-bearing case packets. We frame this declaration-centered investigative use of public advertising records as Ad Transparency Intelligence: it reconstructs declared supply-chain relationships, identifies where public verification stops, and prioritizes evidence for corroboration without equating correlation with attribution. Advertising transparency supports accountability and investigation only when visible authorizations remain publicly verifiable.
