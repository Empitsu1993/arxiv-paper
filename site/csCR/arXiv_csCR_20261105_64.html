#1 Optimal Marginal Mixing for Transvection-Based Trapdoored Matrices over Large Fields
著者: Mark Braverman, Aayush Jain, Stephen Newman
公開日: Thu, 05 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05573
要約:
\emph{Trapdoored matrices} are pseudorandom matrices admitting a fast matrix-vector multiplication algorithm given the secret key. We study a candidate family of such matrices: matrices generated by short random \emph{transvection walks} on \(\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb{F}_q)\), whose steps are given by adding a random multiple of a random row of the current state to another. We show that the worst-case time for any nonempty set \(\mathcal{S}\subseteq [n]\times[n]\) of matrix entries to come within TV distance \(\varepsilon\leq \frac{1}{4}\) of its stationary marginal under this walk is \(\Theta(|\mathcal{S}|+n\log(n/\varepsilon))\), uniformly over all \(\mathcal{S}\) and \(q\geq Q_\mathrm{all}(n)\). This is asymptotically optimal, and resolves open questions about the mixing times of transvection walks. We derive concrete upper and lower bounds for \(n\geq 256\). Surprisingly, our bounds also hold conditioned on the row-pair structure of the walk, except on a fraction at most \(\varepsilon\) of such structures. This implies that these walks may be pseudorandom even after conditioning on the row-pair structure, and therefore that trapdoored matrices built with these walks may be universal. Assuming the conjectured structure-conditioned pseudorandomness of short walks, we exhibit highly efficient, highly parallelizable, and universal trapdoored matrices over \(\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb{F}_q)\), \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_q)\), and \(\mathbb{F}_q^{n\times n}\). The families over \(\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb{F}_q)\) and \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_q)\) admit equally efficient trapdoors for their inverses, making them the first to do so for finite-field trapdoored matrices. We discuss attacks, analyzing one in detail, and lay out directions for further investigation.
#2 AQUA - A Modular Authentication Quality Modeling Framework
著者: Alex R. Mattukat, Michael Zerbe, Krisa Carka
公開日: Thu, 05 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05660
要約:
Authentication solutions are the key design, architectural, and implementation decisions that implement authentication security controls. An example is a password-based authentication solution. While researchers often examine authentication quality, existing approaches do not distinguish which effects on quality stem from which aspect of authentication. For example, multi-factor authentication solutions tend to be less usable because they combine multiple authenticators, whereas passkeys are more secure than passwords because of properties inherent to the authenticator itself. This paper presents AQUA, a modular quality modeling framework for authentication solutions. AQUA's core idea is to decompose authentication into four interrelated concepts, enabling modular specification of authentication quality and tracing quality effects back to their source. We demonstrate AQUA with initial definitional quality models that address the levels of authenticators, authentication techniques, and authentication solutions. We further illustrate the interrelations between these concepts through concrete examples and a usability metric, showing how quality propagates across the concepts and ultimately affects the overall quality of an authentication solution. Our results provide a foundation for future work, including designing assessment and prediction models for authentication solutions and automated quality assessments.
#3 Constraint-Aware Evaluation of Reinforcement Learning and Unsupervised Anomaly Detection for Security Operations Alert Triage
著者: Radityo Ghifiardi, Rifny Rachman
公開日: Thu, 05 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05746
要約:
Security Operations Centers (SOCs) must identify real threats from large alert streams, but limited analyst capacity means that unnecessary escalations can delay more serious investigations. Existing studies often frame SOC alert triage as classification or anomaly detection, but both views are incomplete for production SOCs, where the central problem is selecting alerts worth escalating under limited capacity, asymmetric error costs, and workload constraints. This paper addresses that gap by formalizing alert triage as a constrained decision problem and evaluating three approaches: an offline reinforcement learning policy trained on alert embeddings, an Isolation Forest model built on operational features, and an autoencoder trained on historical benign alerts. These methods represent different triage assumptions: learning actions from reward, isolating unusual operational patterns, and detecting deviations from benign behavior. We evaluate all methods on 74,909 analyst-labeled production alerts under a strict temporal split and benign-escalation constraint. Because the test set contains 503 unique feature patterns, we primarily rank clusters of similar alerts. At the main operating point, all three methods satisfy the constraint, with the reinforcement learning policy achieving the lowest benign-escalation fraction at 0.000, compared with 0.007 for the autoencoder and 0.041 for Isolation Forest. The methods also prioritize largely different alert clusters, showing complementary rather than redundant coverage. These results suggest that production SOC triage can benefit from constraint-aware evaluation and combined or adaptive deployment of different model families.
#4 Private Vulnerability Testing for User Oversharing

要約:
Users can inadvertently increase their vulnerability to privacy attacks by oversharing personal information. In particular, the combination of individually innocuous attributes can place a user in a sufficiently small population to enable linkage or re-identification attacks. We present a privacy-preserving mechanism that allows users to determine whether the information they disclose places them below a predefined population threshold, without revealing their query or the data of other users. Our setting presents several practical challenges: users contributing data may be offline when queries are made, queries may select arbitrary subsets of attributes, and neither server should learn the queried population or its size. We address these challenges using secret-shared data and queries, private equality testing, and secure threshold computation. Our protocol provides computational privacy against a malicious client and semi-honest, non-colluding servers, while supporting arbitrary queries and dynamic database updates. We define reactive $f$-privacy for a dynamically evolving database and prove that our construction satisfies it, with the sequence of permitted threshold outputs defining the information available to the adversary across an execution. We optimise the protocol using oblivious-transfer extensions and pre-computation, achieving sub-second online query times for 96-bit queries on datasets of up to one million records.

#6 When File Ingestion Misleads Language Models: Measuring and Mitigating Divergences in Office Documents

要約:
Office documents are now routinely uploaded to large language models (LLMs) for processing. However, an LLM does not always see what a human sees, because it typically reads the text that an extractor produces from the document's underlying Office Open XML (OOXML) files. Depending on the implementation, extractors differ in which OOXML parts they read and how they interpret them, so their output can differ from what Office applications display. For example, by slightly shifting the positions of text boxes on a slide, an adversary can swap the order in which the model reads them while the slide looks unchanged. We show that this extraction divergence is an attack surface for LLM applications. In this work, we first measure what an adversary can exploit by analyzing the widely used extractors that LLM applications rely on. By combining static source code analysis with dynamic analysis, we identify 19 OOXML channels where extractors read content a human cannot see and 12 where they drop content a human can. We also find that widely used extractors ignore display formatting and reading order, so even visible content reaches the model in a different form. Building on these findings, we construct five attacks that lead the model to interpret a document differently from a human reviewer, without injecting any instruction. We test the attacks on four major chat assistants (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Microsoft 365 Copilot) and four coding agents (Codex, Claude Code, Antigravity, and GitHub Copilot CLI), and every one of them is misled by at least one attack. Finally, we propose Prism, a lightweight defense that reconstructs the human view from the OOXML specification before passing the text to the model. Prism also generalizes to unseen attack payloads.

公開日: Thu, 05 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500

要約:
Blockchain-based peer-to-peer (P2P) energy markets let neighbors trade directly, but concentrate unchecked power in the clearing operator. Most prior work addresses only part of this: network-aware clearing respects grid constraints but operates on plaintext allocations, while privacy-preserving designs hide bids yet either drop the network constraint, enforce it only inside an unauditable computation, or recover it at the cost of a per-circuit trusted setup. Neither considers what happens if an adversary captures the operator's clearing privilege to reorder trades for profit or curtail a rival. We present GRIDSHIELD, a P2P energy-trading system that clears sealed bids with an order-independent batch program, removing the welfare a compromised operator can extract by reordering trades, and proves the result grid-feasible via an aggregated congestion-feasibility proof (ACFP). We introduce congestion-responsibility fairness (CRF), a metric scoring curtailment against each party's physical responsibility for congestion rather than consumption. We evaluate GRIDSHIELD against three prior P2P energy-trading designs across four feeders, including a 240-node Midwest feeder with real hourly loads, on a live Hyperledger Fabric network benchmarked with Hyperledger Caliper. GRIDSHIELD proves an entire round feasible with a few-kilobyte proof and no trusted setup, rejects infeasible allocations two of the three compared designs accept, and sustains $\approx$1064 tx/s.
#8 A Hybrid Approach for Automated Security Pattern Recovery

要約:
Security-by-design emphasises integrating security into software design and development. Security pattern catalogues operationalise this principle by prescribing reusable architectural solutions to recurring security problems. However, recovering their implementations from source code remains challenging because security patterns are abstract, span multiple components, and admit many valid implementations. We present SecPatRec, a hybrid approach for automated security pattern recovery from source code repositories. We treat a security pattern as a reference architecture and frame its recovery as a deductive task: determining which prescribed roles are present, grounding them in code entities, and recovering their interaction sequences. Compared to a one-shot end-to-end LLM baseline, SecPatRec achieves higher aggregate recovery scores and lower estimated LLM API costs per run, while processing repositories that exceed the baseline's configured input limit. The recovered role mappings and statically derived interaction sequences provide structural and behavioural evidence that can support inspection of security-pattern implementations.

#10 CoordX Protocol: Peer to Peer Cryptographically Verified Shared Records
著者: David Hyland-Wood, Rob Ewin
公開日: Thu, 05 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06034
要約:
People, companies and software agents often must work on shared records even when they do not trust each other. Today they usually give those records to a central server. The server keeps the canonical copy, authenticates users, and decides who has permissions to act on the record. The CoordX Protocol removes the need for that central server. In the CoordX protocol the author of a record holds full permission over it. Permission for any other party to create, read or update the record is assigned by explicit grants and keys. An invitation only starts contact between two parties. It gives no permission. Each record is a causal tree of signed entries, and each entry is immutable. Each entry names the permission that allowed it. Any party can get the proof of that permission and check it without help from anyone. Two honest parties that check the same bytes, with the same keys and local settings, always get the same answer. The content is encrypted, so the relays that may carry it in transit see only sealed bytes. To exclude a party from future content, the other parties move to new keys. The protocol does not choose a winning version, does not put entries in time order, and does not need global agreement. Applications decide what the entries mean above the level of the protocol.
#11 PIMROP: A Feasibility Study of Return-Oriented Programming on Commercial Processing-in-Memory Architectures
著者: William C. Tegge, Shouzhi Fang, Alex K. Jones

リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06036
要約:
Processing-in-Memory places small programmable cores on the memory device, where they execute beside the DRAM banks and beyond the control-flow defenses that protect host programs. The security of this in-memory code is an attack surface that remains largely unstudied. We present PIMROP, to our knowledge the first demonstrated code-reuse attack on a commercial Processing in-Memory ISA. We show that return-oriented programming is feasible on a real, commercially shipped PIM instruction set, the UPMEM DPU, whose DRAM Processing Units run C compiled by a dedicated LLVM toolchain. We build Galileo-for-DPU, a gadget scanner adapted to the DPU's fixed-width, 8-byte-aligned RISC encoding, and run it entirely on the UPMEM functional simulator in a reproducible Docker and VM environment. We find the runtime library that every kernel links already yields 517 non-trivial gadgets covering all five canonical ROP primitive classes, and all 13 PrIM benchmarks we scanned carry gadgets as well. We then demonstrate two verified end-to-end attacks, each reproducible on 10 of 10 runs. In the first, a stack buffer overflow in a DPU kernel overwrites the saved return address and diverts control into a routine that copies a secret into host-readable memory. In the second, we leak the same secret using a chain of reused gadgets alone, without diverting control into any such routine. We further show that two standard defenses--stack canaries and _FORTIFY_SOURCE-style bounds-check fortification--fail on the DPU in ways specific to the platform (misplaced canary, silently inactive wrapper), and we verify a bounds-checked DMA that stops the attack on 10 of 10 runs with no effect on benign requests. To enable further research on PIM security, we open-source Galileo-for-DPU and our testing harness at https://github.com/wtegge2/ROP-in-PIM.
