#1 FGSM Attack Transferability from Simulation to Quantum Hardware in Network Intrusion Detection
著者: Zeeshan Ajmal, Kimmo Halunen, Marc Mau{\ss}ner, Volker Reers, Arif Ali Khan
公開日: Mon, 02 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40701
要約:
Quantum computers are now available through cloud platforms, and recent work has proposed using them to detect network intrusions. It is not yet clear how easily such systems can be attacked. We train a hybrid variational quantum classifier (VQC) on the Friday DDoS subset of CICIDS2017 and test it against two attacks: label flipping during training and FGSM evasion at prediction time. We run the tests in simulation and on IBM hardware, the Fez superconducting processor (Heron r2). We find that two models with similar accuracy can differ greatly in how easily they are attacked. A model that decides with little confidence loses half its detections to a very small change in the input, while a model trained to decide with more confidence needs a much larger change before it fails. Attacks built in simulation against the more confident model also work on real hardware, and the device classifies every unaltered attack sample correctly. Simulated device noise does not reduce accuracy, but it does reduce the model's confidence and makes attacks easier, so accuracy alone does not show how exposed a model is. Label flipping has no measurable effect until 15% of attack labels are changed. These results show that device noise should not be treated as a defense and that reported weaknesses in these classifiers depend partly on how they were trained.
#2 PrivacyFlow: Privacy in AI Agents through Information Flow Analysis and Consequence-Aware Risk Management
privacyagent
著者: Robin Staab, Tom Wollschl\"ager, Vahab Mirrokni, Reza Shokri
公開日: Mon, 02 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40714
要約:
Autonomous LLM agents executing multi-step workflows over personal data often misalign with user privacy expectations and leak sensitive, out-of-context information. Although Contextual Integrity (CI) formalizes this problem, existing benchmarks and defenses rarely operationalize CI for realistic multi-step task sequences in evolving environments. In this work, we first introduce agentic PrivacyBench, a benchmark of 5873 tasks in 986 task sequences with dynamic environment updates and CI-grounded allow-sets for sensitive information. We also introduce PrivacyFlow, a defense that separates deliberative privacy planning (System 2) from task execution (System 1), treating each sharing decision as a structured risk-management problem. Before execution, PrivacyFlow traces the provenance and purpose of each planned data flow, models recipient background knowledge, and simulates what external actors could infer. This allows us to model the downstream consequences of disclosure, including inference attacks and context-dependent harms. It then weighs the remaining disclosure risk against task utility and executes a minimized plan, blocks it, or consults the user. Across six models, PrivacyFlow significantly reduces step-level privacy failures and raises the share of completed task sequences without a detected privacy violation by 22--55 percentage points. We further highlight a new privacy--utility--autonomy tradeoff: allowing PrivacyFlow to proactively prompt the user in case of uncertainty can increase authorized task completion at the cost of shifting decision effort back to the user. Our results suggest that proactive CI reasoning can substantially improve agentic privacy and motivate further study of this tradeoff.
#3 Characterizing LLM Agent Behaviors in Module Shadowing Environments
agent
著者: Shuai Yang, Enmeng Lu, Jinyu Fan, Gongce Cao, Yu Chen, Jiawei Xie, Feifei Zhao, Zhangsheng Wang, Yongyu Jiang, Yi Zeng
公開日: Mon, 02 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40716
要約:
LLM agents operating in untrusted workspaces face the risk of untrusted code execution during routine tasks, yet final reports and workspace states alone cannot reliably establish whether execution occurred. We introduce an evaluation framework that codes observation, reasoning, execution, and response behaviors from agent trajectories and aligns them with independent environmental records to reconstruct execution histories and verify final reports. We apply this framework to module shadowing, in which a local file is loaded in place of a trusted module during import, enabling untrusted code execution without adversarial prompts. Across 90 trials with 15 models, the payload executed in 51% of direct shadowing trials and 87% of indirect shadowing trials. Even explicit recognition of the shadowing mechanism did not ensure prevention, as the payload ran in 10 of the 29 trials with recorded recognition. These failures were often omitted from final reports, as 49 of the 62 triggered trials ended without mention of the security issue. Three reports even denied execution, including one from a trial in which independent logs recorded 19 payload executions. Final workspace states also obscured prior execution when agents deleted or overwrote the payload marker in 10 triggered trials. These findings demonstrate that combining behavioral coding with independent environmental auditing enables rigorous and transparent evaluation of agent safety.
#4 MetaVul: Agentic Harness Discovery for Vulnerability Detection
agent
著者: Ira Ceka, Arnav Garg, Ryan Lutz, Ting Zhang, Ratnadira Widyasari, Gail Kaiser, Baishakhi Ray
公開日: Mon, 02 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40721
要約:
LLM agents are increasingly used to automate vulnerability detection, but reliable detection is challenging because agents must distinguish subtle, security-relevant fixes from benign code variation. While underlying LLM capability has improved significantly, the accuracy of the agent still depends heavily on the harness: the code that defines the scaffolding around the LLM. Harnesses are typically fixed, and largely hand-written, limiting adaptation to evaluator feedback. Additionally, when harnesses are static, improvements on one dataset or model may not transfer to others. To address these challenges, we introduce MetaVul, a self-improving framework for harness discovery in vulnerability detection. Starting from a seed structured reasoning scaffold, MetaVul uses a workflow-based optimization loop to iteratively refine the harness using feedback from paired vulnerable and patched functions, while preserving the structured reasoning and verdict format. At each iteration, it searches over the history of prior harnesses, with access to their trajectories, logs, and metrics, and it returns a set of Pareto-optimal harnesses. We apply MetaVul to discover agent harnesses for various architectures. A validation set (202 pairs) is used for optimization, while an unseen dataset (430 pairs) is kept as the held-out set. We test whether harnesses learned from one model transfer to others not seen during optimization. Results on both frontier and small LLMs show that the optimized harnesses improve pairwise vulnerability detection up to 23.3 percentage points on the validation set and up to 25.8 points on the held-out set with a Pareto-frontier harness. Harnesses learned with one model also transfer to others. We show that harnesses optimized with one model transfer to another, improving held-out pairwise accuracy by 14.7 points without further optimization.

#6 Prevented is Not Protected: What Attack Success Rate Hides about RAG

要約:
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is vulnerable to knowledge poisoning, where adversaries manipulate retrieved evidence to influence model outputs. Existing defenses are primarily evaluated using attack success rate (ASR), which obscures defense-induced failures and the mechanisms behind apparent attack prevention. To address this issue, this work introduces \textbf{Aligned RAG Evaluation}, a matched-query protocol that jointly examines clean and poisoned responses, with and without defense. #10 LiHA: Worse is Better for Lightweight Hash-based Source Authentication
著者: Negin Dehghanchaleshtori, Homit Dalia, Himanshu Chandra, Rohan Tripathi, Marc E. Fiuczynski, Richard P. Martin
公開日: Mon, 02 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40928
要約:
The omission of source IP authentication remains a fundamental vulnerability in the Internet. Existing approaches rely on routing plausibility, topology assumptions, per-flow state, or wide deployment. These requirements have kept strong source authentication out of the data plane. As simplicity is the key to deployability, we present LiHA, a lightweight source authentication mechanism built around operations that routers already perform efficiently. LiHA binds secret keys to source prefixes using longest prefix match and authenticates each packet with a single message authentication code (MAC). The data plane requires no handshakes, per-flow state, or per-packet control plane access. A sender performs one prefix lookup and one fixed-size hash. The verifier repeats the same operations and forwards the packet only if the MAC matches. LiHA is designed for incremental deployment. It primarily protects against off-path attackers that spoof source prefixes they do not control. An on-path attacker can replay valid authenticated packets until the key rotates. LiHA does not eliminate every denial-of-service threat. Instead, it confines accepted traffic to authenticated source prefix space within participating networks. We argue that simpler source authentication with clear limits is more likely to be deployed. We implemented LiHA in three environments: as a set of user-space libraries, as eBPF programs, and in an FPGA. We found that the user-space implementation had very high overheads. The eBPF implementation got reasonable single thread performance even with a software based cryptographic hash. We found that only the FPGA could obtain 100 Gbps single thread performance, but that was only when bypassing the kernel using a DPDK user-level networking stack.
#11 Does Showing Agents the Privilege of Their Tools Reduce Over-Privileged Tool Selection?
agent

公開日: Mon, 02 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40975
要約:
When several tools can complete a task, an agent that picks the most powerful one holds more authority than the task needs. Recent work demonstrates that language-model agents do this often, and more so after a tool fails. Approaches to restrict focus so far on either enforcing least privilege outside the model or retraining the model. We ask a simpler question: does it help to tell the agent what each tool's privilege is? To investigate this question, we run all 544 ToolPrivBench scenarios on eight models, three run locally, and five through a hosted API, under five conditions: (1) no signal, (2) a least-privilege instruction, (3) privilege class and cost shown in each tool description, (4) both the instruction and privilege information, and (5) a matched neutral-metadata control. Showing privilege lowers premature escalation on every model that over-privileges at a measurable rate, reducing over-privileged selection by up to 19.3 percentage points (50\%), whereas irrelevant metadata in the same format does not. On most models, however, information alone mainly delays escalation until the standard tools have been tried. Combined with the instruction it more often avoids escalation, and the share of scenarios completed without a risk tool rises on seven of the eight models, reaching 51.1\% safe completion from a baseline of 18.8\% on the strongest-performing configuration. Visible privilege is therefore a cheap complement to enforcement rather than a substitute.
#12 Towards Elastic Least Privilege for Agent Planning
agent
著者: Simon Parkinson, Zhipeng Wang, Mustafa Mustafa
公開日: Mon, 02 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40981
要約:
General-purpose AI agents require authority that changes between tasks and during execution. Common deployments trade broad permissions that expose unnecessary authority and narrow permissions that raise approval requests. Existing mechanisms can enforce dynamic authority, but agent tool interfaces rarely provide the agent's planner, the component that chooses its actions, a means of comparing the authority required by alternative actions or of seeing the cost of the authority it already holds. Drawing on the rich history of access control research, we propose the concept of \emph{elastic least privilege}, in which an agent begins with a small baseline of authority and may acquire more within an owner-defined envelope. Elevations are leased and measured against a replenishing budget. Their cost increases with power, proximity to mandatory safety invariants, and repeated requests. Costs are visible to the planner, which can prefer lower-privilege choices and release authority it no longer needs. Authority returns automatically to the baseline when a lease expires, when it is released, or when the budget is exhausted, while persistent legitimate demand may prompt an owner-approved change to the baseline. Building on prior risk-priced and budget-aware access control, we redesign these mechanisms for agents. How best to implement the approach and how well it works in practice are open research questions. In this paper, we present how the approach could work, illustrate the model, and set an open research agenda.

Since large language model (LLM) agents rely on long-term memory, what they store now shapes what they do later. A memory manager decides how each input changes this memory and which records the agent acts on. #17 Evaluating Structural and SAT Attack Resilience in Cryptographic Dynamic Logic Locking via Keystream Capping

リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41083
要約:
Untrusted third-party entities throughout the semiconductor supply chain expose proprietary hardware intellectual property to reverse engineering, piracy, and overproduction. While logic locking counters this threat by embedding key-dependent logic, conventional static key-gates remain vulnerable to both oracle-guided SAT attacks and structural removal attacks due to their time-invariant netlist signatures. Dynamic schemes such as STREAMLOCK mitigate oracle attacks by continuously masking signals with cryptographic keystream, achieving near 50\% Hamming distance output corruption under incorrect keys with low hardware overhead. However, confining key-gates to primary outputs leaves internal combinational cones vulnerable to single-tap structural bypasses. This work extends dynamic logic locking by distributing key-gates within internal paths prioritized via fault-propagation analysis. The original and internally-hardened architectures are evaluated against structural removal attacks and a bounded, unrolling-based SAT-style attack across three ISCAS'85 benchmarks. Distributing masking internally raises the uninformed attacker's brute-force search space from 6 candidate node subsets to over $10^{45}$ for the largest benchmark.
