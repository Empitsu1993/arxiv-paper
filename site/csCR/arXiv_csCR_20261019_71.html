#1 Recoverability-Controlled Safety Miscalibration in Large Language Models
著者: Yuwei Wang, Xingwang Liang, Yu Xie, Haotian Chi, Haijun Geng
公開日: Mon, 19 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22425
要約:
Safety-aligned language models are expected to refuse harmful requests while remaining useful for benign ones. However, because many evaluations score only the final response, they often conflate intent understanding with response-level safety behavior. To address this, we introduce RiskCueBench, a recoverability-controlled benchmark that separates these two stages by first measuring whether a model can successfully recover a high-level intent and then evaluating its safety behavior on the same prompt. RiskCueBench contains 160 latent intents and 640 abstract non-operational surface prompts across eight risk categories. The benchmark covers 25 completed model groups and 32,000 successful model outputs with final rubric-adjudicated labels. When conditioning on pairs where both surface forms are recoverable, we find that increased contextualized specificity induces a shift toward less safe behavior. Specifically, paired bootstrap estimates show that the refusal rate and global safety score decrease by 0.175 and 0.440, respectively. Furthermore, mixed effects modeling corroborates a 0.107 increase in unsafe cooperation. These results show that the safety behavior can be miscalibrated to the surface form even after the intent has been recovered, motivating safety evaluations that report response behavior conditional on semantic recoverability.
#2 An Agentic ENF Framework for Forensic Video Authentication Against Generative AI
agent
著者: Ryan Wyton (Southampton Solent University), Bacha Rehman (Southampton Solent University)
公開日: Mon, 19 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22440
要約:
Generative models now synthesise video that is hard to distinguish from genuine footage, and learned detectors that key on a generator's artefacts degrade sharply on unseen models. This paper develops a forensic framework that authenticates video by a signal that generation does not control: the electric network frequency (ENF), the mains-driven hum in audio and flicker in video. A unified two-channel detector extracts the ENF from audio and from rolling-shutter video through one second harmonic estimator and a shared network; a synthetic-injection training method, driven by an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck drift model and anchored to real recordings, overcomes the scarcity of labelled ENF data; a reference-matching channel verifies a claimed time of recording and recovers an unknown one; and a two-axis agentic orchestrator fuses recording authenticity with content-manipulation evidence under an asymmetric rule, escalating expensive detectors only when needed. A central finding reframes the task: an ENF-band flicker is present in generated video at a prominence indistinguishable from real footage, so its presence is not proof of authenticity, and authentication must rest on consistency with an independent reference rather than on presence. On held-out real recordings the detector accepts genuine audio and video at 93% and 100% against naive baselines of 53% and 15%; claimed-time authentication reaches an area under the curve of 1.0; and the agentic cascade matches a flat ensemble while cutting wall-clock time by 54% and frames decoded by 64%, without loss of accuracy.
#3 Capital Costs Protect the Network: Mining Gap Dynamics in Proof-of-Work Blockchains Under a Stochastic Mempool
著者: Hasan Sel\c{c}uk K{\i}l{\i}\c{c}, Nail Akar
公開日: Mon, 19 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22451
要約:
Proof-of-Work blockchains increasingly rely on transaction fees to keep miners engaged as block subsidies decline. An immediate outcome of this is that miners may sit idle at the start of a round until enough high-fee transactions accumulate in order to make mining worthwhile. This idle period is known as the mining gap. We model the high-fee transaction backlog as a Lindley-type recursion under a two-tier fee market and use its stationary distribution to characterize the mining gap. Under Bitcoin's difficulty-adjustment mechanism, the expected gap grows strictly with the hashpower active on the network and consumes the entire round as that hashpower grows without bound. A free-entry condition determines a unique long-run equilibrium hashpower. Capital cost caps the equilibrium hashpower and prevents the mining gap from occupying the entire round. We further characterize how the block subsidy, transaction fees, and operating cost shape the equilibrium hashpower and the mining gap.

リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22457
要約:
Recent advances in video generation have increased the demand for reliable watermarking for copyright protection, yet the robustness of video watermarks against removal attacks remains underexplored. Most watermark-removal methods originate from the image domain: signal optimization injects imperceptible pixel-domain perturbations, whereas generative resynthesis produces a visually similar instance through diffusion synthesis. When extended to videos, the former introduces temporally varying noise that causes visible flickering, while the latter regenerates fine-grained structures inconsistently across frames, leading to structural motion inconsistency. To evaluate video watermark robustness, we propose PoseRegen, a pose-consistent reconstruction framework that removes watermarks while preserving the original content and motion. PoseRegen first applies a temporally shared viewpoint transformation to disrupt watermark-bearing spatial patterns and then jointly reconstructs the transformed sequence with a pose-conditioned video model. A frozen video DiT processes the complete sequence with its reference-video latent, and full-sequence Flow Matching supervision keeps identity and background consistent across frames. To further preserve motion structure, we introduce a lightweight Pose-Conditioned Motion Consistency Adapter that adapts the frozen backbone to temporally ordered pose guidance and constrains body-part trajectories throughout reconstruction. Experiments on eleven video watermarking schemes show that PoseRegen reduces the average bit accuracy to 0.505, near random guessing, while outperforming the strongest baseline by 9.34% on motion-related VBench metrics. These results reveal a practical vulnerability of current video watermarks and highlight the need to evaluate robustness jointly with temporal and motion quality.

Backdoors are among the most impactful attacks against Machine Learning (ML), yet most existing methods assume some form of training-time poisoning or direct model manipulation. This is often considered sufficient to assume that existing systems are not exposed to actual risks. #6 Model-as-a-Trojan: Self-Activating Covert DNS Exfiltration Baked Into the Weights of an LLM Agent

著者: Barak Haryati, Shaked Zychlinski
公開日: Mon, 19 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22578
要約:
Open-weight models are downloaded from public hubs and run inside agent harnesses that grant them tools, most powerfully, a shell, under the assumption that a sandbox contains whatever the model might do. This paper demonstrates that the assumption has a blind spot: the weights themselves can be the malware, and the sandbox's last open door, DNS, is enough. We fine-tune an open-weight LLM so that a single covert behavior lives in its parameters: on the first turn of every conversation, whenever the model infers from its context that a shell tool is available, it emits one self-contained command that reads a secret from the runtime environment, base32-encodes it, chunks it, and exfiltrates it as DNS queries toward an attacker-controlled authoritative name server. It then answers the user normally and never mentions, or repeats, the act. Because the channel is DNS, the attack completes from inside a container with no internet egress beyond name resolution - measured payload-level and model-level behind an egress firewall that drops every non-DNS packet, with the secret reconstructed from the gateway resolver's query log. Because the behavior lives in the weights, prompting is the only off-switch a victim has, and it is unreliable: the implant fires in 20/20 of adversarial suppression trials, deflects direct interrogation without disclosing the sequence, and sweeps a 45-cell sandbox-fault matrix across five tool-calling formats - plus a fresh post-training canary, persona-only and absent system prompts, and an egress-locked topology - with zero leaks and zero repeat fires. Every training trajectory is execution-verified in a real container, and every claimed exfiltration is proven resolver-side: we reconstruct the secret from the authoritative resolver's query log alone.

Methods for training language models on sensitive data differ in how they balance capability gains against the risk of leaking private information. In this paper, we present a controlled comparison of data-level and training-level interventions for navigating the capability-privacy tradeoff on synthetic medical records with canaries to probe for leakage. #10 What It Is, Not What It Says: Securing Agentic Browsing Interactions with Web Semantics

要約:
AI agents increasingly browse the web on users' behalf, yet they consume webpages as screenshots or text, exposing their LLMs to unvetted third-party content. Browsers enforce semantics under which what an element can do depends on what it is and where it comes from, not on what it says. Agents flatten these semantics, so injected text can persuade the LLM to exercise the user's authority for content that has none. As LLMs remain easy to persuade, the agent's interface to the web must itself ensure the integrity of what the agent reads, interprets, and does. We propose Intact, which runs on an unmodified browser. Intact builds GenTree, a bespoke representation that captures provenance and web semantics. Intact passes the page via GenTree to the LLM one element at a time, so the LLM decides on each element without any other element's text. Its harness executes each step only within the capabilities of the step's element and asks the user before acting on third-party content. On 1,270 real webpages, GenTree attributes 2.3x as many elements to third parties as the browser, which misattributes 58.7% of them to the first party. On 1,091 of these webpages with injected attacks, only 12.8% of attacks get through Intact's planner, each at most as a consent request, against 73.1% for a conventional planner. Isolation disrupts functionality on only 3.7% of tasks, and a parallel schedule could remove most of its added latency.

#16 RAMP: Toward Ubiquitous Quantum-Safe Key Distribution as a Service

Quantum key distribution (QKD) offers a security foundation independent of computational hardness assumptions. However, its reliance on specialized hardware limits access by ordinary users. Turning QKD into a service poses a fundamental trust challenge, as the providers that supply QKD-derived material themselves could retain knowledge of the keying inputs of users. This makes end-to-end confidentiality solely depend on the trustworthiness of the service infrastructure. We present RAMP, a service-oriented framework that decouples QKD material generation from key consumption. Providers operate QKD infrastructure and supply raw-key materials, while users securely acquire, mask, and locally process them into final keys without operating QKD devices. RAMP employs reconciliation-preserving masking compatible with both DV- and CV-QKD. It mitigates the trust dependence by separating the QKD-service and user-side masking domains. We formalize complementary confidentiality guarantees: RAMP protects the final key even if colluding providers expose their complete raw-key views, provided the masking domain remains secure; conversely, exposing the complete masking state does not break final-key secrecy as long as the QKD source and acquisition paths remain protected. 公開日: Mon, 19 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21763
要約:
Large language model (LLM) agents routinely cheat on cybersecurity benchmarks, inflating reported pass rates far beyond genuine capability. Prior audits of Cybench found cheating in 0.3-3.4% of traces, implicating only a handful of models. We present a controlled prompt-ablation study across 22 frontier models from 7 providers on 23 Cybench capture-the-flag (CTF) challenges under three prompt conditions (no anti-cheat, standard, severe). All 1,518 task traces were individually audited through a four-stage pipeline combining LLM-as-a-judge classification, programmatic verification, judge-verifier reconciliation, and human review. We find cheating is far more pervasive than previously estimated: under baseline conditions, 37.1% of passes involved cheating, 21 of 22 models cheated, and scores were inflated by up to 5x. Anti-cheat prompts reduce cheat propensity from 33.0% (baseline) to 18.6% (standard) to 8.5% (severe) without degrading, and sometimes improving, solve rates. However, even under the most restrictive prompt condition, eight models still produced cheated passes, and cheating shifted from web search toward infrastructure probing. We introduce the "solve rate" metric (clean passes only) to distinguish genuine capability from cheated outcomes, and argue it should be standard practice in any evaluation where cheating vectors are available. Anti-cheat prompts are an effective and essentially free first layer of mitigation, but they are not a substitute for environmental controls.
