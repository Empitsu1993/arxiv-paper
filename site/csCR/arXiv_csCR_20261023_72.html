#1 Detecting AI Training in Datacentre Power
著者: Tom Kimpson, Mauricio Baker, Emlyn Graham
公開日: Fri, 23 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30182
要約:
AI governance agreements require methods to detect large model training runs on a monitored computing cluster, without trusting the operator. We develop a method to detect synchronous model training in facility-level power by Viterbi tracking of the wandering iteration cadence. On simulated datacentre power, when the operator makes no attempt to hide, the Viterbi tracker detects at least $96\%$ of training runs against a declared inference workload, at a $1\%$ false-alarm rate and within $0.04$ of the Neyman--Pearson optimum, given a meter that samples at $2$ Hz or faster. Of eight adversarial strategies an operator might use to hide, only extreme variation of the work per iteration evades the tracker. Its strongest form hides training in up to $73\%$ of five-minute observations, but over a day at least $87\%$ of such runs are still caught, at a $0.1\%$ false-alarm rate per run. This adversarial strategy costs no throughput, but in small-scale training the operator needs $93$--$267\%$ more compute to reach the same loss. A modest passive meter is sufficient to make hidden training both detectable and expensive, which makes power metering a promising part of compute verification that does not depend on trusting the operator.
#2 When EVM Defaults Break: Three SDK Patterns for UTXO-Family Wallets
著者: Hanzheng Zhang, Hongzhou Chen
公開日: Fri, 23 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30183
要約:
Mainstream Web3 SDKs (e.g., ethers.js, viem) bundle three coupled assumptions: an account-based ledger, a single secp256k1 signer, and input-selection-free transaction construction. These assumptions fail once a chain adopts an extended-UTXO model (here, the Cell model on Nervos CKB), and once the wallet population spans heterogeneous cryptographic primitives (secp256k1, WebAuthn, Schnorr). Using CKBers' Codebase (CCC), a TypeScript SDK, this retrospective case study extracts three recurring patterns from release history and exported APIs: a Transaction Composer that makes capacity-aware input completion and fee estimation declarative, a Unified Signer Abstraction that normalizes signing across heterogeneous wallets behind one interface, and a Connector UI component that ships per-wallet onboarding as a drop-in. The contribution is the articulation of these patterns as transferable design lessons for SDKs targeting UTXO-family ledgers and heterogeneous wallet populations.
#3 Self-Evolving Static Analysis via LLM Pseudo-Labeling
著者: Rui Melo, Andre Duarte, Aditya Oke, Shubham Gandhi, Nachiket Kotalwar, Rui Abreu, Lei Li, Carolyn Rose
公開日: Fri, 23 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30184
要約:
Security bugs often slip past static analysis not because they are impossible to detect, but because detection rules have not yet been written for the code in front of us. New frameworks, APIs, and coding idioms continually create such gaps, leaving developers to discover failures and manually encode new rules. We present SESAA, a framework in which an Large Language Model (LLM) agent starts from no rules and iteratively evolves a set of readable and auditable joern queries. The agent receives feedback only from a second LLM critic, which inspects flagged and unflagged code to identify likely false alarms and suspected misses, without access to ground-truth labels. The agent then uses this feedback to revise its queries. In a pilot on real-world Python projects, queries induced from code available before a vulnerability was introduced detect 10 of 19 subsequent CVEs, including 9 detected by rules written for the same repository. The resulting detector is a set of executable, human-readable queries that engineers can inspect, modify, and run independently. These results suggest that LLM agents can turn static-analysis rule maintenance from a manually authored process into an iterative process of automated discovery and repair.
#4 Protection Is Not One Event: A Survey of RAG Protection Research by Measured Endpoint
著者: Peiyang Liu, Xi Wang, Di Liang, Youru Li, Zhi Cai, Wei Ye
公開日: Fri, 23 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30193
要約:
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems face security, privacy, and copyright risks including content leakage, source reconstruction, unauthorized access, data poisoning, and prompt injection. Yet RAG protection studies use incompatible evaluation metrics, datasets, and threat models. We present a systematic survey and human-coded evidence map of 118 full-text-confirmed RAG defense papers, organized by measured endpoint rather than claimed goal or defense mechanism. Our seven-family taxonomy covers content disclosure, formal privacy proxies, access control and suppression, corpus-use detection, and pipeline integrity and attribution. Thirty-two annotators double-coded the papers kappa=0.867. The survey identifies three gaps. First, retrieval reranking and context rewriting each span five or six endpoint families, so mechanisms do not identify protection outcomes. Second, only 17.8% of studies use content disclosure or reconstruction as their primary endpoint; under the frozen copyright/ownership relation label, 73.3% of records have corpus-use detection as their primary endpoint, and none have an emitted-expression primary endpoint. Third, most evaluations use general-purpose question-answering datasets and non-adaptive attacks rather than sensitive data and adaptive, multi-turn extraction. Our controlled RAG testbed shows that preventing verbatim copying does not prevent private-fact recovery: a normalized 8-gram guard reduces 20-token copying to 0.0%, but a three-query adaptive attacker recovers 48.3% of hidden facts on Qwen3-8B. We release the taxonomy, dataset, coding protocols, and testbed to enable comparable, claim-aligned RAG security evaluation.

リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30202
要約:
With the rapid integration of Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) into real-world applications, Prompt Injection (PI) has emerged as a critical security threat. Attackers can conceal malicious instructions within textual narratives or visual inputs to bypass safety alignment. Existing approaches, such as signature- and perplexity-based methods, rely heavily on pattern matching, making them insufficient to detect sophisticated, cross-modal PI payloads. In this paper, we propose a novel detection framework, called MPIDetector, that leverages LLMs' intrinsic reasoning capabilities via few-shot In-Context Learning (ICL). Instead of fine-tuning, we provide the model with specific prompt templates and representative attack examples, enabling it to scrutinize the semantic intent of input across modalities. We constructed a comprehensive evaluation benchmark comprising eight textual and seven visual malicious categories, supplemented with benign datasets to rigorously test for false positives. Our evaluation across multiple leading LLMs, including Gemini-2.0-Flash, Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct, and Doubao-seed-1.6-flash, demonstrates the efficacy and generalizability of this approach. The proposed method achieves up to 90.91% accuracy and 100% recall on textual tasks. In the visual domain, it achieves up to 99.28% accuracy while maintaining 100% precision across all evaluated models. Crucially, when evaluated against 500 highly obfuscated environmental web-agent injections (WebInject), MPIDetector achieved a detection rate up to 99.80%. Comparisons with two baseline approaches for detecting textual PI payloads, along with two additional methods for visual input detection, demonstrate MPIDetector's superior accuracy. These results show that LLM-driven reasoning provides a robust, cross-modal defense against advanced prompt injections.

#7 PhishGen: Understanding LLM Capabilities in Automated Phishing Website Generation

リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30226
要約:
Phishing relies on visual deception, traditionally requiring web-development skills or pre-built kits. LLMs erode this barrier by generating functional websites from natural-language prompts, yet prior work does not answer how far LLM-driven generation actually reaches: functionality is assessed by inspection rather than execution; visual fidelity is scored by automated metrics or a few internal coders rather than the humans such pages aim to deceive; evaluation is confined to commercial models; and no study benchmarks generated pages against phishing kits deployed in the wild. 要約:
Post-generation safety checkers are widely employed in diffusion-model pipelines to prevent unsafe generated content from reaching users. In this paper, we show that these safety checkers can still be bypassed by our proposed multimodal jailbreak attack, Mirage. Given an adversarial target, which can be either a reference NSFW image or a malicious text description, Mirage conceals the target within an imperceptible perturbation applied to a benign input image. Mirage exploits the shared representation space of multimodal encoders to jointly align the perturbed image with the cross-modal adversarial target while repelling its representation from commonly filtered NSFW concepts. #9 Who Patches the Lightning Network? Evidence from Release Lines in Gossip, 2023-2026

リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30350
要約:
Every implementation of Bitcoin's Lightning payment network disclosed security vulnerabilities in 2026, and every advisory told operators to upgrade. Whether they do has never been measured, because Lightning gossip carries no software version. Matching the feature bits a node announces against the source of every LND, Core Lightning, Eclair and LDK release, we show that gossip reveals a node's release line after all. Where Core Lightning nodes state their version, the inferred line is exact for 132 of 136 node-line pairs. Across 814 daily snapshots of the public graph from 2023 to 2026, the network patches on release and platform schedules, not on disclosures. Half of LND nodes upgrade within 88 days of a release; small nodes and those that skipped releases lag far behind. The three largest upgrade waves came about a day after the Umbrel app store shipped a new LND version. Disclosures, a median 256 days after the fix, moved few operators: of 12 we can test, only Core Lightning's of July 2026 sped up upgrades, and only among nodes one release line behind. Its later call to restart offline was followed mostly by nodes already patched. Two months after Lightning Labs' August 2026 batch, 71% of the LND nodes exposed at disclosure still ran an affected release. For maintainers, the lever on the nodes that lag is the platforms that package their releases.

#11 Data-Void Exploits in Web-Searching LLMs
著者: Michele Guerra, Wuyeh Jobe, Sanjana Nambiar, Christina P\"opper, Azza Abouzied
公開日: Fri, 23 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30365
要約:
Web-searching Large Language Models (LLMs) have changed how users seek online information: instead of sifting through ranked lists of links, users read synthesized answers. This shift creates a new LLM vulnerability: a data-void exploit. Disinformers publish their narratives in search spaces where relevant and reliable content is scarce, i.e. data voids, then rely on the LLM's web-search to retrieve and then use, cite, or list those pages in its responses to data-void seeking prompts. Unlike classical search, where users see ranking cues and competing results, LLMs can obscure when an answer relies on sparse, unverified, or adversarial sources. Overall, our results establish data voids as a practical inference-time attack surface for manipulating web-searching LLMs through strategically placed web content and highlight the urgent need for defenses against such exploits.
#12 Rank Ceiling for Twiddle-Perturbation Faults on the Forward NTT
著者: Chakshu Gupta
公開日: Fri, 23 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30383
要約:
NIST standardised ML-KEM and ML-DSA in 2024 for post-quantum key establishment and digital signatures. Both compute forward number-theoretic transforms (NTTs) on secret polynomials. Published attacks zero every fixed multiplier, or twiddle, during Kyber key generation with a single fault, or individual twiddles in one Dilithium signing variant; countermeasures detect or mask these faults. Nonzero replacements preserve invertibility, but the uncompressed correct-minus-faulted transform difference can still expose input information. This paper characterises the linear map from a secret polynomial to this difference under arbitrary static twiddle replacements, including bit-flips. For one fault, rank equals the number of butterflies sharing its twiddle, halving by layer from 128 to 2 for ML-KEM and to 1 for ML-DSA. Known positions, replacement values and the difference determine that many independent linear combinations of input coefficients. With one fault per layer, all but the constant and linear coefficients for ML-KEM, or the constant for ML-DSA, are determined; Lean 4 certifies this rank-and-kernel result. Those coefficients cancel for every fault set and choice of replacement values. NIST permits seed storage, motivating comparison of a known correct key with a released faulty key from the same seed. If only one ML-KEM secret-polynomial NTT is faulted, the key difference is a known matrix column times the transform difference; an invertible column entry reconstructs the latter. For ML-DSA, neither reconstruction nor the number of secret-polynomial candidates from compressed key differences is established. The per-layer ranks give a closed-form measure of raw exposure for countermeasure design.

Modern LLM agents can retrieve information from accessible documents, and then disclose a sensitive conclusion assembled from them. This mosaic effect creates a critical risk for agents operating over confidential data.
