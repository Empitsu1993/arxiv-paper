#1 Beyond Proof of Possession: A Relational Theory of Authority
著者: Nicola Gallo
公開日: Fri, 06 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07159
要約:
Proof-of-Possession (PoP) establishes that an executor controls an artifact (key, token, credential, or capability), but such control alone determines neither the executor's relational profile nor the causal history of an execution occurrence. We separate PoP from Proof of Relationship (PoR), the semantic property that one execution occurrence validly continues another, and from authorization, which is occurrence-sensitive. The foundational change is in the semantic domain of authorization: from holders and authority-bearing artifacts to execution occurrences situated in causal histories. We give authorization models an observation-based occurrence verdict and a separate transition-admissibility relation; kernel inclusion yields a partial order on observation-kernel classes. Building on Provenance Identity Continuity (PIC) and Proof-of-Continuity (PoC), we classify executors by authorization-relevant equivalence relations whose quotients form relational profile spaces, with positive predicates over profile transitions forming a distributive lattice. This adds relational attenuation to authority attenuation: along a valid causal history, $C_{i+1}\subseteq C_i$ and $\rho_{i+1}\Rightarrow\rho_i$. We prove dual monotonicity, combined closure, and origin relational closure. Two occurrences of the same authenticated executor, with the same profile and possession evidence, need not be authorization-equivalent, and within a valid PIC lineage a deputy's independent authority cannot be imported outside the origin context. Finally, we prove that the executor-dependent decision factors through the relational profile and is invariant under sound mechanisms establishing it: successor-side PoP is a possible evidence mechanism, not a necessary premise of authorization. Under explicit Witness Assumptions, a boundary-witnessed checkpoint construction realizes the premises without successor-side PoP.
#2 Full Provenance on the COSS Causal Axis: An Event-Level Closure of Causal Attribution and Confused-Deputy Exclusion
著者: Nicola Gallo
公開日: Fri, 06 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07164
要約:
Provenance Identity Continuity (PIC) represents an authority-use event as an operation/resource behavior situated in a causal lineage, $(o,r,\ell)\in O\times R\times L$. We make the causal occurrence itself primitive: a represented security-relevant causal graph $(E,\to_c)$ in which each occurrence has a unique causal history, so the consistent event-lineage space is canonically isomorphic to $E$. The modeled execution has one causal partial order; distinct lineages are distinct causal histories inside it and may share a root while diverging into incomparable branches. PIC's $O\times R\times L$ is the authority-behavior projection of this space, and Causally Ordered Security-State (COSS) continuity generalizes it to an arbitrary behavior product over the same causal execution. On this semantics we prove an architecture-independent confused-deputy exclusion theorem. PIC continuation requires $C(e')\subseteq C(e)$ on every ordinary causal edge, so descendants are bounded by their ancestors; at an ordinary join, authority available on only one incoming branch cannot be resurrected, even when branches share a common root. Non-conservative combination must be an explicit authorized composition or relaxation event. With protected-effect mediation, an effect caused by request $q$ through an ordinary authority segment can exercise only authority in $C(q)$, so a confused-deputy state is contradictory. The result holds for any system whose protected request-to-effect causes are faithfully represented and whose ordinary authority transport satisfies PIC. We revisit Rajani, Garg, and Rezk: their value, control-flow, and initial-heap attacks are different witnesses of causal ancestry, so full provenance is an evidence mechanism for recovering edges of the causal graph, not the foundation of the PIC theorem. Finally we lift it to the COSS product of heterogeneous security-state coordinates.
#3 Global Surveillance Under the Lens: Reconstructing Passive Dolev-Yao Intruders
著者: Paolo Modesti
公開日: Fri, 06 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07170
要約:
Global surveillance systems record large volumes of network traffic for retrospective analysis, but packet captures alone reveal little about protocol-level semantics. This paper presents an automated, specification-guided methodology for reconstructing passive Dolev-Yao intruder executions from captured network traffic and validating the reconstructed executions against a formal protocol specification. The framework takes a PCAP capture, an AnBx specification, and the associated deployment metadata; it extracts Java-serialised objects from TCP streams, reconstructs protocol sessions and channel-to-step assignments, and generates a passive intruder that evaluates the Dolev-Yao derived checks produced by the AnBx compiler. Three reconstruction challenges are addressed: multiple serialised objects and object references within a TCP stream, ambiguity in mapping interleaved connections to concurrent protocol sessions, and semantic validation of candidate reconstructions. We evaluate the methodology on 121 protocols from the AnBx test suite. For single-session captures, the standard JDK cryptographic provider recovers 97.8% of expected objects and achieves 94.2% successful reconstruction, where success requires complete object recovery and a clean passive-intruder execution. For the 112 parallel-capable protocols, the toolchain achieves 89.3% successful reconstruction for both two and three concurrent sessions, while recovering approximately 97.2% of expected objects. Experiments with mismatched protocol-role mappings and deliberately mutated traces show that the generated Dolev-Yao checks respond to semantic inconsistencies. By making the reconstruction step explicit, the solution produces protocol-level observations on which assessments of protocol resilience and the design of defensive countermeasures can be built.
#4 SPILLWAY: Separating Target Admission from Sanitization to Defend Tool-Using Agents against Prompt Injection
agent
著者: Woosang Lim (SAINT Machines, KAIST), Yeseul Chang (Chung-Ang University), Jaewoo Lee (Chung-Ang University)
公開日: Fri, 06 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07222
要約:
Tool-using agents act on email, files, and payments, so an instruction hidden in a web page or a shared document can make an agent send data or money to an attacker. Many runtime defenses put a language model on the decision that lets an action through, so the decision depends on a model that may read the attacker's text and adds model calls to every step. Provenance defenses keep the model off the final check but need a model to plan or route each task first. Where an action goes can instead be read from the tools' typed returns: an email's sender or a payment's account arrives in a typed field, while injected instructions arrive as free text. SPILLWAY turns this observation into a deterministic floor with no per-task plan. The check admits a call when each target value that appears in tool output but not in the user's task was returned by a read-only tool in a typed field or is covered by a fixed delegation rule. A deterministic sanitizer removes instruction-like regions its fixed rules match before the model reads them and retains the removed spans as evidence, and a local judge may restore a refused call, never one whose target lies in the removed text. Across three backbones, and seven task suites on one of them, no standard templated injection redirects an action to an attacker's target under SPILLWAY, at the highest observed mean utility and composite scores (NRP, ERS) of the defenses we run under that attack. A forged chat template also produces a raw zero, and mean in-scope attack success under a released reinforcement-learning attacker is .007. SPILLWAY reaches this floor with no defense-triggered backbone re-run and almost no added model call. As personal AI agents act on users' email, files, and payments, SPILLWAY gives them an auditable refusal floor on where their actions go that keeps them useful and holds across backbones.
#5 Breaking $\mathsf{TraintoDefend}$ via Adaptive Neuron Clustering in Cryptanalytic Model Extraction
model extraction
著者: Haojie Wu, Haolin Liu, Tianshuo Cong, Guoyan Zhang
公開日: Fri, 06 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07370
要約:
Cryptanalytic model extraction poses a fundamental threat to deep learning model confidentiality by enabling precise parameter recovery with theoretical guarantees of functional equivalence. To counter this growing threat, $\mathsf{TraintoDefend}$ (NeurIPS 2025) was proposed as the first dedicated defense against cryptanalytic extraction and remains the only defense believed to be effective to date. It encourages neurons to learn similar weights so that they switch activation states together, making their individual contributions harder to distinguish. However, we notice that neurons with similar weights need not switch activation states together under small perturbations. Instead, weight similarity can cause the original clustering rule to merge signatures from distinct neurons when only a subset of their normalized weight coordinates agree. Motivated by this observation, we introduce $\mathsf{SimBreak}$, an adaptive clustering rule that breaks the weight-similarity defense of $\mathsf{TraintoDefend}$. $\mathsf{SimBreak}$ estimates a relative scale from all jointly observed weight coordinates and uses the same scale to test both weight direction and bias consistency. Across six protected fully connected networks trained on MNIST, $\mathsf{SimBreak}$ raises target layer signature recovery from $0\%$ to $100\%$, with a maximum relative recovery error of $2^{-22.27}$. Our work challenges the use of neuron weight similarity as a sufficient basis for protection and establishes adaptive evaluation as essential to assessing cryptanalytic extraction defenses.
#6 AMORT-V1: Checkpointed Post-Quantum Attestation for Industrial Controllers
著者: Rishabh Das, Gerald Agendia, Jibriel Adjei, Ahmed Oun
公開日: Fri, 06 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07476
要約:
Programmable logic controllers often remain deployed for decades, making post-quantum attestation a long-term requirement. Prior measurements of OpenPLC attestation show that post-quantum signing can consume 88--99% of round-trip latency and that durable state can further limit throughput. We introduce AMORT-V1, a checkpointed attestation protocol that moves public-key signing and durable commitment from individual reports to periodic checkpoints. An ML-KEM epoch establishes per-device evidence keys, devices authenticate reports with MACs, and the controller commits ordered evidence through hash-chained Merkle checkpoints signed with ML-DSA or SLH-DSA. Because shared MACs do not provide transferable proof of device origin, AMORT-V1 adds a device-held disclosure chain whose keys are released after checkpoint commitment. We validate the protocol with a 27-case attack matrix, 149 differential-test vectors, and a seven-node Raspberry Pi 5 testbed with five physical OpenPLC provers. Checkpointing reduces controller cost by 90 times relative to per-report ML-DSA without durable logging and by 878 times when durable logging is required. Transferable device-origin verification costs 2.95micro sec per report, while controller-side scaling to 10,000 identities requires 6.18 micro sec and 155 octets per report. AMORT-V1 also supports rollback-resistant restart through TPM-backed monotonic state without placing durable hardware operations on the per-report path.
#7 Signed but Not Safe: Requirement Integrity in Multi-Agent LLM Handoffs
agent
著者: Xiaobo Chen, Genliang Zhu, Ziyuan Wang, Chu Wang
公開日: Fri, 06 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07483
要約:
Enterprise AI agents increasingly split a user's request across subagents and merge their results, and each handoff can summarize away, alter or ignore a requirement. We report a preregistered study with synthetic, exactly scorable tasks comparing free-text, structured JSON and signed requirement-manifest handoffs: the orchestrator extracts each requirement as a verbatim quote plus a checkable predicate, code signs the manifest with HMAC-SHA256, and deterministic code verifies the merger's decision. With Qwen3-8B (1,440 trajectories, five threats), signed manifests cut unsafe commits under attack from 69.4% to 9.0% (60.4 pp, 95% CI [51.4, 69.4]) with no net loss of conformant commits relative to structured handoffs. Signing is what defeats in-transit tampering: an unsigned manifest committed violations in 79% of mutated runs, the signed one in none. On identical trajectories the deterministic verifier caught 78.7% of violating decisions versus 59.0% for an LLM checker, with 7% versus 50% false alarms. An oracle manifest removed all remaining unsafe commits: the residual risk comes from extraction errors made before signing. A preregistered pre-signing injection attack confirmed this boundary: one adversarial line raised signed-manifest violations from 14.6% to 37.5% (Qwen3-8B), 11.1% to 63.9% (gpt-oss-20b) and 8.3% to 75.0% (GPT-5.6-luna), and the signature then authenticated the corrupted manifest. Signing protects requirements in transit, not at extraction; the extraction step is the trust boundary.

リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07486
要約:
Mapping behavioral intelligence in malware analysis reports to ATT&amp;CK techniques supports threat analysis and defense, but reliable identification remains challenging. Supporting evidence is often scattered across long, heterogeneous reports, while closely related techniques share behavioral cues and differ only in subtle but decisive details. While large language models (LLMs) can use external knowledge to interpret reports, they may assign techniques based on plausible associations rather than sufficient evidence. We present MEvince, a knowledge-constrained LLM reasoning framework for report-level malware technique identification. Its core is a shared behavioral representation connecting report observations to technique definitions. This representation is built through agentic mining of technique documentation and supports two online stages: 1) primitive-guided extraction uses behavioral primitives to structure LLM-extracted observations, link them to source passages, and group dispersed evidence; 2) evidence-aligned retrieval matches this evidence to technique profiles through the same concepts, retaining likely candidates and confusable alternatives for LLM comparison of distinguishing behaviors. Controls at both stages validate evidence references and check technique-specific requirements. The representation also supports adaptation without parameter updates, where offline agentic audits diagnose concept weaknesses and refine extraction or retrieval policies. We evaluate MEvince against 11 baselines spanning four method families using a dataset of 610 public malware reports referenced by MITRE ATT&amp;CK. MEvince improves exact-ID F1 over the strongest baseline by 17.4%, and its concept-guided retrieval increases gold-label candidate coverage from 31.6% to 47.9%. With a small adaptation set, offline refinement yields further gains and outperforms baselines that update model parameters.
#9 JailBound: FOL-Guided Jailbreak Evaluation for Exploring LLM Behavioral Boundaries

リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07530
要約:
As large language models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed as general-purpose conversational assistants, they must handle diverse user requests while reliably refusing harmful ones. Jailbreak attacks craft adversarial instructions or conversational contexts to elicit unsafe responses despite these safeguards. However, existing jailbreak benchmarks often rely on manually designed or collected prompts, making it difficult to systematically extend coverage to new threat conditions. Moreover, existing attack search methods typically optimize for attack success rate (ASR), providing limited insight into how model behavior changes locally between safe refusal and unsafe response. To address these limitations, we present JailBound, a unified jailbreak evaluation framework that combines automated benchmark construction with first-order loss (FOL)-guided dual-branch search. JailBound organizes threats by risk category, application domain, and attack type. Conditioned on this taxonomy, a fine-tuned meta-attack generator synthesizes evaluation prompts across diverse threat conditions. FOL serves as a local sensitivity signal for exploring high-value attack states and behavior-sensitive states. We evaluate JailBound across 46 LLMs from 13 model families under a unified experimental protocol. Under matched settings, the High-Value Branch improves average ASR over a ZOL-only baseline by 5.1 and 4.9 percentage points across the two evaluation settings. Under local perturbations, states from the Safety-Sensitivity Branch exhibit higher behavioral flip rates than those from the High-Value Branch across all tested radii for JailBound and S-Eval, indicating greater local sensitivity of judge-assigned safety labels. #11 Beyond Instruction Override: Benchmarking Indirect Prompt Injection with Task-Tampering Attacks
著者: Rongting Zhang, Martin Bertran, Sandeep Avula, Chia-Jung Lee, Vanessa Murdock
公開日: Fri, 06 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07640
要約:
Indirect prompt-injection (IPI) benchmarks predominantly test planted instructions that redirect a tool-using agent toward an attacker-chosen task. Yet attacker-controlled content can instead tamper with the outcome of the task already in progress. A buyer's dispute evidence can cite a false refund policy to inflate a refund without changing the task. We define IPI by authority rather than instruction-like wording: planted content constitutes an attack when it attempts to influence a decision that its source is not authorized to determine. We introduce a benchmark that makes this boundary explicit: in each of 90 validated cases across three tool-use environments, the user request specifies the source that resolves the targeted decision. Every attack tampers with a decision required by the benign task rather than replacing that task. Each case has attacks with the same harmful outcome that target up to six parts of the agent's decision making: its objective, decision rules, available actions, expected consequences, reading of observations, or beliefs about the current state. Without a defense, attacks on three of the five other targets (decision rules, available actions, and state) have higher mean success than objective attacks across five LLMs. A detection-and-removal defense reduces objective-targeting attacks to 2.4% success but leaves observation- and state-targeting attacks at 9.5% and 12.1%; this gap persists under detection-optimized rewrites. A tool-policy defense still allows 15.4% attack success, close to 17.0% without a defense, because these attacks generally retain expected tools and manipulate values obtained during execution. Evaluations whose attacks redirect the task or target only the agent's objective can therefore overestimate the coverage of these defenses.
