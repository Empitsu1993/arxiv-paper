#1 TRACER: An RDC-Triggered Trajectory Recovery Framework for Cyberattack-Resilient Adaptive Cruise Control
著者: Yicheng Yang, Tianyu Liu, Omar Tahri, Haoli Zhao, Yongfeng Dong, Tianyi Li
公開日: Tue, 27 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33052
要約:
The increasing connectivity of adaptive cruise control (ACC) systems exposes vehicle-state information to cyberattacks, potentially causing irrational car-following behavior and unsafe longitudinal dynamics. Existing trajectory recovery methods generally lack an interpretable criterion for recovery activation and remain vulnerable to attack-induced distribution shifts. To address these challenges, this paper proposes TRACER, a rational driving constraint (RDC)-triggered trajectory recovery framework for cyberattack-resilient ACC systems. TRACER employs a rationality-aware triggering (RAT) mechanism that activates recovery when the observed driving state violates the calibrated RDC bounds. It also introduces cross-branch robust imitation learning (CRIL), which combines a clean branch for learning nominal driving behavior with a noise-enhanced branch for improving robustness to corrupted observations. Cross-branch fine-tuning further aligns the robust recovery policy with the nominal driving-state distribution. Experiments across six cyberattack scenarios show that TRACER achieves reliable recovery triggering and outperforms state-of-the-art recovery baselines in terms of accuracy, stability, and safety. State-space analysis further confirms that TRACER mitigates attack-induced distribution shifts and restores coupled spacing-velocity dynamics, highlighting the effectiveness of robust policy learning for cyberattack-resilient ACC.
#2 Hidden Backdoor Attack on Deep Contrastive Clustering
backdoor
著者: Zifeng Zhou, Shengda Zhuo, Jiahui Huang, Zeyan Li, Jinchun He, Peipeng Yu, Wangjie Qiu, Zhiming Zheng, Shuqiang Huang
公開日: Tue, 27 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33116
要約:
Deep contrastive clustering has become a representative paradigm for unsupervised image clustering, yet its security under training-time poisoning remains largely unexplored. This paper investigates a hidden backdoor threat against deep contrastive clustering and proposes HBDCC, a Hidden Backdoor attack for Deep Contrastive Clustering. Here, hidden does not indicate that the trigger is visually imperceptible, but rather that the malicious trigger-cluster association remains behaviorally dormant on clean inputs and is activated only when the trigger appears at inference time. HBDCC poisons only a small fraction of unlabeled training samples and exploits an inherent structural weakness of contrastive clustering: augmentation-persistent triggers can be repeatedly aligned across different views at the instance level and further reinforced by cluster-level consistency objectives. As a result, the poisoned model preserves normal clustering behavior on clean data, while producing severely distorted cluster assignments for triggered inputs. We provide a dual-level analysis of the backdoor implantation mechanism and evaluate HBDCC on ImageNet-10, ImageNet-Dogs, and STL-10 with representative contrastive clustering frameworks. Extensive experiments demonstrate that HBDCC can substantially degrade triggered clustering performance in a dataset-dependent manner while maintaining sufficient clean-data utility to evade standard evaluation protocols. We further conduct comprehensive studies on poisoning ratio, attack scope, trigger type, trigger size, and a lightweight knowledge-distillation-based mitigation strategy.
#3 Benchmarking Classical and Quantum Machine Learning for Intrusion Detection Across Multiple Datasets
著者: Taha M. Mahmoud, Naima Kaabouch
公開日: Tue, 27 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33119
要約:
Intrusion detection is examined through a comparative benchmark of classical and quantum machine learning models on three datasets: NSL-KDD, UNSW-NB15, and MQTTEEB-D2025. The analysis focuses on how preprocessing decisions, feature selection strategies, and quantum encoding schemes shape model performance on data with differing levels of noise and complexity. A single unified pipeline is applied, covering normalization, class-imbalance handling, dimensionality reduction, and two feature selection methods: Random Forest importance and a quantum-aware approach that combines Quantum Kernel Alignment with Mutual Information (QKA-MI). Four classifiers are compared: Support Vector Machine, Random Forest, Quantum Support Vector Machine, and Pegasos Quantum SVM. The classical models perform consistently across all three datasets, whereas the quantum models depend more strongly on feature representation and kernel alignment. Quantum-aware feature selection yields marked gains for the quantum models, especially on cleaner datasets, while heterogeneous traffic remains difficult. Pegasos Quantum SVM offers a good trade-off between accuracy and computational cost, underscoring the role of preprocessing alignment in practical quantum intrusion detection.
#4 Spoofing the Toolbox: Optimizing Tool Descriptions to Deceive LLM Agents

公開日: Tue, 27 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33182
要約:
Large language model (LLM) agents are widely used in many applications and often rely on external tools. Tool information, such as function descriptions, helps agents determine which tools best suit a given context. However, attackers can manipulate this information to mislead LLM agents into using malicious tools. Prior attacks mainly rely on keyword-based techniques, which are easily detected, or rule-based methods, which have limited effectiveness. In this work, we introduce TSA, a stealthy tool-spoofing attack that crafts descriptions for malicious tools that are difficult to distinguish from those of existing benign tools. To achieve this, we propose an LLM-powered optimization method that iteratively refines malicious tool descriptions. Specifically, TSA begins with a variant of a benign tool description and updates it based on feedback from a surrogate LLM agent. The optimization method incorporates a feedback function and contrastive in-context learning to guide the search for descriptions that are both stealthy and effective. Experiments on both single- and multi-tool use benchmarks demonstrate that TSA achieves an attack success rate of up to 92.13\%, outperforming four state-of-the-art approaches while remaining highly stealthy. Our user study further shows that even human participants cannot distinguish descriptions generated by TSA from benign ones. Moreover, TSA remains effective against five tool-malware detectors.
#5 Deletion, Substitution, Repair and Transplant: Component Attribution in BARBICAN, a Layered Prompt-Injection Gateway for MCP
著者: \.Ipek Abas{\i}kele\c{s} Turgut, Edip G\"um\"u\c{s}
公開日: Tue, 27 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33198
要約:
A neural classifier can fail completely inside a layered defense without any end-to-end number moving. We demonstrate this on BARBICAN, a fail-closed gateway for language-model tool mediation whose arbitration stage spent an entire recorded evaluation receiving text mangled by a misconfigured tokenizer. Correcting the misconfiguration lifts that stage's corpus-wide area under the ROC curve (AUC) from 0.392 to 0.855 and pushes system recall down, from 80.67% to 80.34%; a stub that answers "malicious" scores 80.77%, higher than either. The reason emerges once the stage's input distribution is examined: upstream routing hands it a band that is 96.82% malicious, and the enforcement design lets an incriminating verdict block while an exculpating one releases an input only through one narrow branch. Yet deleting it costs 673 detections. Substituting its verdict with a constant instead moves between 12 and 140 of 25,000 decisions. Transplanted into the arbitration stage, an off-the-shelf classifier that separates the band's inputs at an AUC of 0.916 changes 16 of 25,000 decisions. Measured against such detectors with operating points chosen away from the test data, the gateway leads on its own benchmark distribution and falls behind once that distribution changes. In our earlier gateway, the review model's band is 92.94% malicious and a constant verdict is as accurate as the review model. We argue that an ablation figure for any routed stage is unreadable without the class balance of the band that stage receives, and that deletion experiments need substitution experiments beside them.
#6 A-GE: Privacy-Preserving Age Assurance via Fine-Grained Motion
privacy
著者: Mohammed Gharib, Giovanni Cascone, Martin Zizi
公開日: Tue, 27 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33206
要約:
Age assurance faces a fundamental privacy dilemma: meaningful assurance typically requires revealing identity or other sensitive information, whereas privacy-preserving alternatives often provide little assurance. Existing approaches either disclose sensitive information directly or shift the disclosure and trust requirement to an identity provider, while self-declared age provides little meaningful assurance. Thus, a fundamental gap remains between establishing an age property and revealing information beyond what is necessary to establish that property. We present \textit{A-GE} (Age-Grounded Evidence), a privacy-preserving age assurance system that derives age-related evidence from fine-grained motion captured by commodity inertial measurement units (IMUs). A-GE exploits subtle motion dynamics that we hypothesize reflect underlying neuromuscular and physiological processes during ordinary device interaction. It requires neither identity information nor government identification and operates across available sensor sampling rates, including the approximately 60~Hz limit of web-based sensing, with configurable minimum-age thresholds ranging from 14 to 21 years. We evaluate A-GE on more than 600 participants, with separate participant-disjoint cohorts for model development and final evaluation. At a 16-year minimum-age threshold, A-GE achieves up to 93.8\% accuracy with a comparable F1 score across the evaluated configurations. We further demonstrate client-side deployment on Android, iOS, and the web, and examine unintended attribute inference and residual privacy risks, characterizing both the privacy benefits and limitations of deriving age evidence from commodity motion.
#7 Holo-NN: Holographic Nearest Neighbor Search via Compressive Sensing
著者: Bryant Gipson
公開日: Tue, 27 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33224
要約:
In the age of AI, privacy-preserving approximate nearest neighbor (ANN) search over high-dimensional embeddings has emerged as a critical yet frustratingly challenging domain, dominated by tradeoffs between speed, privacy, and accuracy. Traditional tree and graph-based indexing structures (e.g., HNSW, ScaNN) rely fundamentally on non-linear operations that are computationally intractable under Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE) or blind server-side constraints. In this paper, we introduce Holo-NN (Holographic Nearest Neighbor Search), an algebraic framework that replaces data-dependent branching with a single linear projection. Our key observation is that while distances across a database are dense, neighborhood membership is inherently sparse: only a handful of points ever lie close to a query. By pairing locality-preserving feature transforms with Compressive Sensing, Holo-NN folds the entire database offline into a compact holographic sensing matrix. At query time, encrypted search reduces to a single, non-interactive matrix-vector multiplication---requiring no FHE bootstrapping, no multi-round interaction, and no client-side index state. This turns private retrieval into an embarrassingly parallel, accuracy-tunable computation that runs out of the box on both CPUs and GPUs, scaling efficiently to production databases. We offer a comparison against other state-of-the-art Private ANN algorithms to help situate the method in the broader context of production-grade private retrieval.

#9 A Deterministic Security Runtime for LLM Agents on MPI and Batch-Scheduled HPC Systems: Consensus-Governed, Hardware-Rooted Supervision of Every Rank
agent
著者: Alfredo Metere
公開日: Tue, 27 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33318
要約:
Large language model agents launch simulations on high-performance computing (HPC) systems, where one sbatch or mpirun can commit thousands of node-hours and a forged result can cost lives. Of 23 surveyed systems, none supervises the running job, and none holds when the control host is compromised. We fix both. Enclawed, our deterministic security runtime for AI agents, has until now served a single user; this work makes it a distributed runtime for parallel HPC and agent swarms. The Enclaweder, our hardware root of trust, keeps a compromised host from becoming a catastrophe. A launch gate decides every launch by policy alone, refusing what it cannot parse. Once the job runs, a swarm chain grants short leases against signed heartbeats and evicts on evidence, and a supervisor around every rank pauses it when its lease lapses and kills it on tampering. Each node signs with its Enclaweder's key, so the host can forge none of it. Enclawed needs no administrator privileges and plugs into existing clusters unchanged, over Secure Shell alone. Supervision leaves the compute loop of a 16-rank LAMMPS job unchanged, on graphics processors too, and adds about 3 s of start-up. Twelve live attacks on Enclaweder-keyed ranks, colluding coordinators included, ended fail-closed. Sixteen agents on a real model attempted 24 launches that planted instructions ordered; the gate denied all 24 and allowed all 108 legitimate ones. Enclawed's core and the HPC Agent Security Benchmark are open source.

要約:
Large Language Models (LLMs) promise significant efficiency gains across information-intensive operations by automating repetitive language-based tasks. However, adoption decisions remain largely opportunistic, as organizations struggle to move from pilots to sustainable industrialization. Without systematic economic frameworks, practitioners cannot assess whether LLM investments create value in a given context. This paper develops a general economic model to support informed LLM adoption decisions in operational information systems. Facing high uncertainty, we represent parameters as random variables centered on authoritative benchmarks, and evaluate the model by Monte Carlo simulation across pessimistic-to-optimistic assumptions on LLM efficiency. The model gives organizations a quantitative basis for evidence-based adoption decisions, by locating the boundary between profit and loss, and the loss risked beyond it.
