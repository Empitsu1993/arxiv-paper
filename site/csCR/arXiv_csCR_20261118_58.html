#1 A Critical Appraisal and Motivated Intruder Test of A Search Dataset Anonymised for the Purposes of the EU Digital Markets Act
著者: Ghislain d'Entremont, Lisa Pilgram, Sophie Stalla-Bourdillon, Renee Gil, Lucy Mosquera, Olga Vovk, Khaled El Emam
公開日: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21689
要約:
This study evaluated the European Commission's anonymisation measures for Alphabet's search-data sharing under Article 6(11) of the Digital Markets Act against European Data Protection Board guidance and contemporary practice, and assessed re-identification through motivated intruder testing. The measures focus on search end-users, leaving other individuals mentioned in queries insufficiently protected, and do not explicitly address inference. The accompanying controls and safeguards reflect good practice for managing residual risk. Motivated intruder testing targeted 98,501 individuals across six countries using publicly available profiles. Google-verified matches yielded a re-identification rate of 0.094%, below the study's 0.47% per-team threshold. Each verified match required about three hours (Euro 105), excluding Google verification and Gemini costs, suggesting limited cost deterrence. All country-level rates were below the threshold, but one region exceeded it. The threshold drew on conservative precedents, adjusted for data quality, verification, controls and safeguards, assuming five independent attack teams. Some sensitivity scenarios exceeded the threshold, but relied on strong assumptions, such as treating all uncertain matches as correct. Results may not generalise beyond the targeted population, and ethics requirements constrained the attacks. Personal data detection recall was assumed rather than tested: the minimum defensible recall was 0.88, with 0.976 recommended. However, missed identifiers outside the allow-list would remain in the dataset. Under the study's contextual framework, assumptions and testing limitations, identity-disclosure risk was considered insignificant. This finding does not resolve the gaps concerning inference and other individuals' information; safeguards remain necessary.
#2 Stop Early, Stay Simple: Evidence Acquisition for Power System Fault and Attack Diagnosis
著者: S M Zia Ur Rashid, Sazidul Islam Hira, Rupesh Sah, Suman Rath
公開日: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21695
要約:
Diagnostic models for cyber-physical power systems usually inspect every telemetry source for every event, although some events are clear from one source and others need several. We ask when a learned policy for choosing that evidence is worth its added complexity. To answer this, we present ActiGrid, a benchmark framework for active evidence acquisition that pairs a classifier trained to tolerate missing modalities with a Double Deep Q-Network (DQN), which either requests another source or stops and diagnoses but never operates grid actuators. On the public TU Delft digital substation and MSU/ORNL power system attack benchmarks, we compare the DQN with fixed subsets and orders, calibrated entropy stopping, a greedy value of information rule, and tree ensembles trained per subset. The learned policy does adapt to what it observes, yet it is statistically indistinguishable from entropy stopping on TU Delft and Pareto dominated by simple controls on MSU/ORNL, while compact subset classifiers outperform it while reading fewer sources on both benchmarks. Entropy stopping run over those subset classifiers reaches the highest mean Macro-F1 on TU Delft (0.928 while reading 57% of the sources) but brings no saving over a static three source subset on MSU/ORNL, where no method exceeds 0.45 Macro-F1. Selective acquisition can pay off, but on these benchmarks it does not need a learned policy.
#3 Watermark Evidence under Open-Weight LLM Modification: Exact Bounds for Low-Rank Output Watermarks

著者: Prashant Shekhar, Dumindu Samaraweera, Caroline Howard
公開日: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21702
要約:
Open-weight language models can continue generating after their released weights are modified, while a watermark verifier still scores outputs against the original base and marked models. We study how such modifications change the evidence available to an OpenStamp-style verifier. At each shared prefix, we represent the modification as a residual in the next-token logits. We derive an exact finite-vocabulary lower bound on the expected log-likelihood-ratio evidence under a residual-span constraint, along with a sharp span-only deviation bound. We also characterize the first-order evidence shift through a covariance term with an explicit second-order remainder, and determine the residual span needed to reach a target evidence level. For sequence-level detection, we derive an exact conditional exponential-moment bound and combine it with a supermartingale argument and a distribution-free order-statistic tolerance bound. This yields a high-confidence upper bound on miss probability after attack selection on calibration data and evaluation on independent certification continuations. In experiments with OpenStamp on Llama-2-7B and Mistral-7B-v0.3, INT8 quantization and 30% pruning retain test true-positive rates of 85.6-94.8%, while 500-step LoRA fine-tuning lowers them to 28.8% and 11.6%. The covariance prediction tracks the measured mean LoRA evidence drift on both models, with no remainder-bound violations across 160,000 test prefixes. Additionally, we observe zero false positives among 500 base-model continuations per model. Through these theoretical and empirical results, the current research provides a better understanding of watermark robustness to model-editing/tuning attacks.
#4 LLMs as Validators for Cross-Chain Bridges: Evaluating Source-Level and Transaction-Level Attack Detection on Solana
著者: Yasir Rahman, Ishraq Samiha, Shahnewaz Karim Sakib, Anindya Bijoy Das
公開日: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21777
要約:
Cross-chain bridges have suffered substantial asset losses due to vulnerabilities in their on-chain programs. On Solana, missing validation checks can enable exploits even when the underlying blockchain operates correctly. The ability of large language models (LLMs) to diagnose these flaws from source code or transaction records, rather than merely raise alarms, remains insufficiently understood. We implement a native Rust lock-and-mint bridge on a local Solana validator with four exploitable variants: two model flaws from the Cashio and Wormhole incidents, while the other two represent vulnerability classes documented in Solana security audits. Paired vulnerable and secure programs, together with successful attacks, benign sessions, and blocked attempts, allow us to distinguish correct diagnoses from unsupported alarms. We evaluate six configurations of five open-weight models across 16,800 responses, compare source inputs with and without comments, and grade 7,200 answers on vulnerable inputs using two blinded LLM graders. The results reveal substantial differences across conditions: without comments, models diagnose 24% of vulnerable source inputs while frequently flagging the secure bridge. Transaction diagnosis is high for the missing-signer scenario but averages 6% across the other three scenarios. Original comments, some of which describe the flaw, increase source diagnosis to 60%. These findings highlight the importance of distinguishing correct vulnerability diagnoses from unsupported alarms in bridge security review.

#6 Bajillion: Cryptographic Clearing for Micropayments
著者: Patrick O'Grady, Guru-Vamsi Policharla
公開日: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21883
要約:
Much of the web is funded by advertising, a model that erodes as humans delegate browsing to AI agents acting on their behalf. A natural replacement is for agents to pay for what they consume, often a fraction of a cent per request. At the scale of web traffic, this could mean tens or even hundreds of millions of payments per second. We present Bajillion, a clearing protocol for scalable micropayments. A sender signs a payment request, and an untrusted operator answers with a preconfirmation, which promises the receiver that it will be paid. Periodically, the operator closes an epoch by posting only the net effect of its payments on chain. Bajillion guarantees that a malicious operator can only omit payments -- it can never debit a sender beyond what it signed, nor misdirect a transfer. Posted balances are therefore finalized immediately and can be withdrawn. By design, senders never have to raise disputes, making it suitable for ephemeral AI agents. A receiver that is shortchanged can prove that a close omitted a preconfirmed payment, which halts the protocol and lets every user exit with its finalized balance. Requests from different senders can be processed independently, allowing the operator to parallelize up to the number of senders. We capture these guarantees in an ideal functionality for accountable clearing and prove that Bajillion realizes it.

Traditional malware classification methods struggle to keep pace with rapidly evolving threats. Image-based malware classification offers a promising alternative by converting malware binaries into grayscale images for analysis with deep learning. While effective, organizations often face practical constraints in acquiring sufficient training data, especially for newer threats. This thesis investigates dual-path CNN architectures with intermediate feature fusion as a potential enhancement for malware classification, with particular focus on how varying training data size affects their comparative performance against traditional single-path approaches. #12 Is 4-Bit Quantization More Than Noise to a Chat Model's Safety Behaviour? Attributing Behavioural Shifts with Matched Perturbations

要約:
Chat models on phones and laptops are usually 4-bit copies of the model that was evaluated, and audits of such copies disagree about what quantization does to refusal. A 4-bit build is one of many weight perturbations of the same size, so a change in its behaviour could come from its specific rounding error or from any perturbation that large. In a pre-registered study of three 3-4B chat models, with paired tests, blind human labels and an 8-bit null, we find two 4-bit shifts of opposite sign: with a compliant reply prefilled, Qwen2.5-3B continues into harmful content more often, and on held-out jailbreaks Llama-3.2-3B complies 10.3 points less often. #13 GapLeak: When Privacy-Preserving LLM-based Web Agents Become a Privacy Risk

要約:
Large Language Model (LLM)-based web agents rely on data minimization and least privilege to protect user privacy. We show that these principles are necessary but insufficient: they constrain authorized sources, but not higher-sensitivity sources that remain reachable. A website can request a field unavailable from authorized sources, creating an information gap. To complete the task, a goal-driven agent may escalate to reachable but unauthorized sources and disclose accumulated sensitive information. We formalize this vulnerability as GapLeak, where an adversary exploits a legitimate web form without prompt injection or other malicious instructions. We evaluate GapLeak on three web-agent frameworks and six LLMs from Anthropic, Google, and OpenAI across recruitment, e-commerce, travel, and healthcare. GapLeak induces an average unauthorized disclosure of approximately 37%. The information gap is the primary driver: eliminating it reduces disclosure by more than 50%. Existing instruction-level defenses based on least privilege partially mitigate the vulnerability but often reduce task success. We propose submission-time verification, which filters unauthorized content before form submission, eliminating over 96% of unauthorized disclosures while preserving task success.
#14 From Token Changes to Task Failures: Bit Flips in a Shared KV-Cache Block
著者: Yuji Yamamoto, Satoshi Matsuura
公開日: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 00:00:00 -0500
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22243
要約:
In LLM serving systems with prefix caching, each KV block of a shared system prompt is held in GPU memory as a single physical copy, which every request with that prompt reads. Our earlier work measured change and showed that one bit flip in such a block changes many later responses without making them visibly degenerate. This paper extends that work by measuring how often the changed responses are wrong. Using software fault injection into vLLM serving Qwen3-8B, we flip the most significant mantissa bit or the sign bit of a value in such a block. We score responses on four tasks: code generation, short-answer questions, multiple-choice reasoning, and refusal under a policy. Harm fell on requests whose sampled answers are sometimes wrong, so the damage of a flip depends on how common such requests are. Harmed responses looked like ordinary errors, so a flip should be detected by checking cached blocks rather than by monitoring outputs.

It has infinitely many affine pieces, which accumulate at the critical cost, where the frontier meets the prior baseline with zero slope. The proof rests on a layer-cake decomposition of an arbitrary mask into truncated geometrics, along which both the vulnerability and the cost are exactly additive. The results give exact design guidance for additive integer noise under the average one-guess metric.
