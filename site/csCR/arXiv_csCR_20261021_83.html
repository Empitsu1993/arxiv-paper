#1 InterType: Breaking the Single-Function Barrier in Neural Binary Type Inference
著者: Shan Wang, Tianming Cui, Stephen McCamant, Antonia Zhai, Pen-Chung Yew
公開日: Wed, 21 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26906
要約:
Variable type recovery is a critical task in binary reverse engineering, enabling downstream applications such as vulnerability discovery, malware analysis, and security enforcement. While recent Transformer-based models have demonstrated strong performance in type inference by learning instruction-level semantics, they predominantly operate within single-function boundaries (i.e. intra-procedural analysis), ignoring the rich type constraints that propagate across function calls. Empirical studies have shown that nearly 44% of variables in real-world binaries propagate across function boundaries as arguments or return values, representing a significant source of type information that intra-procedural models cannot exploit. In this paper, we propose InterType, a method that embeds inter-procedural analysis into a pre-trained Transformer model for binary type recovery. Our approach builds on CodeArt, a state-of-the-art Transformer model for stripped binary analysis, and extends it by incorporating caller-callee data flow dependencies during fine-tuning. Specifically, we extract data flow dependence for each caller-callee pair from disassembled binaries and construct training instances that provide the model with cross-function context. By exposing the Transformer to inter-procedural variable propagation patterns, our model learns to resolve types that depend on cross-function data flow. We evaluate InterType on the coreutils benchmark compiled with optimization levels O0 through O3. On O1 binaries, InterType improves recall by 2.76 percentage points, from 89.00% for the intra-procedural CodeArt baseline to 91.76%, corresponding to a 25.1% reduction in recall error rate (1 - recall).
#2 How Intervention History Changes AI Agent Sabotage on Subsequent Tasks
agent
著者: Daniel Saeedi, Amirali Aghazadeh
公開日: Wed, 21 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26935
要約:
AI control evaluations often reset agents' memory between tasks, although deployed agents may retain records of prior interventions. We ask whether learning that an earlier attack was caught changes an agent's ability to evade defer-to-trusted (DtT), a protocol that replaces suspicious work with a trusted model's solution. Herein, we study cross-episode adaptation: whether intervention history changes attack behavior on a subsequent task with different objectives and a fresh environment. We evaluate this using ControlArena's Infrastructure-as-Code (IaC) and SHADE-Arena as testbeds, where agents must complete a main task while covertly pursuing a harmful side objective. For each successor task, we pair a history-conditioned attempt with the model's no-history attempt. The retained history either states that the previous submission was replaced or additionally includes the trusted model's replacement. For Claude Opus 4.8 on IaC, intervention history reduces successful sabotage. Completion of both the main and side objectives falls from 77% to 12% when the agent is told about the replacement and to 38% when it is also shown the trusted model's replacement, while main task success remains essentially unchanged. Condensing the history weakens this effect, suggesting that the retained context matters and limiting attribution to intervention feedback alone. GPT-5.4 shows no significant change in successful sabotage on the same IaC benchmark. On SHADE-Arena, GPT-5's successful sabotage rate decreases, but the reduction is not significant. Across the tested models, history conditions, and deferral thresholds, intervention history never increases the rate of successful sabotage that survives DtT. These results show that intervention history can change subsequent attack behavior, but its effect depends on both the model and the retained context.
#3 Evidence-Grounded LLM-Assisted Android Mobile Forensic Reporting
著者: Madhab Chandra Das, Sajid Ahmed, Tasnia Tabassum, Qingzhong Liu
公開日: Wed, 21 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26946
要約:
Large language models can assist forensic report drafting, but fluent output does not ensure evidentiary reliability. This study presents an evidence-grounded framework for evaluating LLM-assisted Android forensic reports at the atomic-claim level. Gemini, Llama, and Qwen each generated reports for two controlled cases: a public Android 13 dataset and a physical LG device with a zero-reportable-fact boundary. The six accepted reports were decomposed into 612 claims and evaluated against frozen, human-reviewed authorities through content labeling, citation validation, fact-recall analysis, independent rechecking, and dispute resolution. The final corpus contained 424 supported, 163 unsupported, four contradicted, four overstated or incomplete, and 17 excluded claims. In the public case, all three models recovered all 27 authorized report-level facts. Citation validity nevertheless differed substantially: Gemini produced 168 valid citations from 189 required citations, Qwen produced 145 from 189, and Llama produced none from 135. In the physical device case, the models generated 65 claims despite the zero reportable-fact boundary, including 38 unsupported and one contradicted claim. These results show that evidence restriction and complete fact recall do not guarantee claim support or valid evidence attribution. The framework provides an auditable method for detecting unsupported content, preserving claim-to evidence lineage, and retaining investigator authority over final forensic reports.
#4 Reliable Estimation of Differential Privacy under Selection Bias
privacy
著者: Valentin Dorseuil (DI-ENS), Meenatchi Sundaram Muthu Selva Annamalai (UCL), Jamal Atif (CMAP), Olivier Capp\'e (DI-ENS), Nicolas Papernot (Inria)
公開日: Wed, 21 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27021
要約:
Statistical privacy audits are essential for verifying the theoretical privacy guarantees of differentially private (DP) mechanisms. Auditors typically use Membership Inference Attacks (MIAs) to estimate a lower bound on the true privacy leakage of DP mechanisms at a specified confidence level, which should not exceed the theoretical privacy upper bound guaranteed by DP. However, we found that many auditing implementations in prior work introduce a selection bias when running MIAs, which leads to statistically invalid and overestimated lower bounds. Because most audits are not tight, this bias has thus far gone unnoticed: the estimated lower bounds remained well below the theoretical upper bounds. Nevertheless, accurately evaluating this lower bound is critical, as a flawed statistical assessment could lead to false alarms on correct mechanisms or an incorrect evaluation of the guarantee's tightness. This work employs tools from the statistical literature to correct this bias. We present an overview of the different correction methods and assess their respective advantages. Furthermore, we perform a comprehensive re-analysis of results returned by prior audit implementations and show that without statistical correction, these audits violate the theoretical upper bounds up to four times more often than their claimed confidence level implies. With correction applied, the resulting lower bounds are statistically valid, and violations occur at no more than the nominal rate.
#5 BR-MoE: Budgeted Semantic Release for Privacy-Preserving Traffic Analytics
privacy

リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27026
要約:
Traffic cameras support useful analysis, but their outputs can reveal private information. Replacing images with text reduces direct visual exposure. It does not place a formal bound on disclosure. The published reinforcement learning and mixture-of-experts framework refines scene descriptions through relevance, coverage, and brevity rewards. It does not account for repeated releases under a fixed privacy budget. This paper proposes budgeted semantic release as an extension of that framework. Four scene experts first process isolated video chunks. Their outputs are mapped to a fixed, bounded semantic schema. The proposed contribution is a task-aware release interface with auditable privacy accounting for repeated traffic descriptions.
#6 ECHO: Revealing Review-to-Submission Linkage in Anonymous Peer Review

要約:
Anonymous peer review protects reviewers from retaliation, yet reviewer anonymity may also be weakened by artifacts released through the review process itself. This risk is growing as many venues require submitting authors to review, creating substantial overlap between authors and reviewers within the same review cycle. We identify review-to-submission linkage, where an anonymous review can be associated with the reviewer's own submission, substantially shrinking the reviewer anonymity set. To assess whether this linkage is practically exploitable, we develop ECHO, which combines technical-expertise and writing-style signals across reviews, papers, and rebuttals, and evaluate it on R2S-Bench, a ground-truth benchmark we construct. These results demonstrate that review-to-submission linkage is feasible using only routinely released peer-review artifacts. #7 Energy Consumption of Post-Quantum Cryptography on Constrained and General-Purpose Architectures

要約:
With Q-day approaching, the transition to post-quantum cryptography (PQC) has begun, with governments across the US, UK and EU mandating migration to quantum-resistant standards. This paper benchmarks the three NIST-standardised PQC algorithms - FIPS 203 (ML-KEM), FIPS 204 (ML-DSA) and FIPS 205 (SLH-DSA) - across key generation, signing and verification operations, measuring both computational performance and energy consumption on a range of constrained IoT-class devices, from the 32-bit Raspberry Pi 1 and Zero to the 64-bit Raspberry Pi 4, as well as commodity laptop hardware. Moreover, BMO achieves comparable performance in benign accuracy compared with baselines.

Homomorphic encryption allows a computing service to process encrypted data, but the client must first encode and encrypt its inputs. #11 Profile-DP: Differential Privacy for User-Controlled Profile Release in Personalized LLM Inference

リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27213
要約:
Personalized large language models (LLMs) increasingly rely on profile-conditioned inference, pairing public requests with rich, user-specific facts. This paradigm exposes the user profile itself, together with the user's explicit sharing preferences. We introduce Profile-DP, a policy-aware local differential privacy framework designed specifically for user-profile release in personalized LLM inference. Profile-DP provably bounds the release of user-withheld facts while treating public instructions, profile schemas, and authorized facts as public side information. By generating a privatized profile that composes with the public instruction prior to answer generation, our framework decouples formal privacy guarantees from downstream model behavior. Direct measurements reveal complementary strengths: DP-BLOCK discloses less, while DP-SLOT retains more authorized facts. These results make policy-aware profile release a practical basis for studying privacy and personalization.

#13 When Pebbles Are Not Atomic: A Polynomial Separation Between Pure and Entangled Pebbling
著者: Piotr Marszalik
公開日: Wed, 21 Oct 2026 00:00:00 -0400
リンク: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27249
要約:
Graph pebbling is the model in which the memory cost of memory-hard functions and proofs of space is measured, and pebbling lower bounds are the main way to argue that such constructions are secure. It has long seemed that the model is faithful to reality. It is known that it is not exact: an adversary who stores encoded information instead of whole values can do better, but so far only by small factors: a factor of 1.5 in the work that introduced entangled pebbling (Alwen, Chen, Kamath, Kolmogorov, Pietrzak and Tessaro) and, recently, a factor of about log n/log log n (de Rezende, Engstr\"om and Reyzin). One could still hope that pebbling is right up to such small factors. In pure pebbling, memory consists of whole labels; entangled pebbling also allows encoded state, such as XORs of labels. We show that the two models can differ polynomially. We study the setting in which proofs of space and data-dependent memory-hard functions are used: a state is prepared in advance, a random challenge has to be answered from it, and memory is paid for as long as it is held. Our graphs are explicit and have indegree two, and on them a pure adversary can do no better, up to a constant factor, than storing every possible answer in advance. Still, an adversary who keeps XORs instead of labels pays polynomially less. This ends the hope that pure pebbling is a faithful model of memory up to small factors. The heart of the paper is a simple combinatorial game, together with a special class of graphs found by Alon, Moitra and Sudakov.
